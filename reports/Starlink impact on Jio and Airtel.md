# Starlink threatens Jio's edges, not its core

Starlink's dish broadband can reach only the edges of Jio Platforms' business, and Jio's own prospectus draws the same boundary. Jio reports a single business segment: **76.37% of its revenue in the June 2026 quarter came from "pre-paid connectivity services"** sold entirely through Reliance Retail, and the Red Herring Prospectus (RHP) names only JioFiber, JioAirFiber, UBR fixed wireless, rural broadband and enterprise and government links as lines satellite could substitute, a base of **28.6 million homes against 533.3 million customers** ([Jio RHP, 14 Oct 2026](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)). Starlink still cannot sell in India: it holds licences but, like Jio's and Bharti's satellite arms, waits for a Home Ministry security clearance and Cabinet approval of spectrum pricing, and Musk's posts of 7–16 October fell inside that wait and in the fortnight before Jio's IPO opens on 21 October ([Communications Today, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/satcom-spectrum-pricing-heads-to-cabinet-as-dot-finalizes-its-note/); [Business Standard, 17 Oct 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/satcom-row-starlink-says-india-infra-has-govt-approvals-ready-for-launch-126101700036_1.html)). Abroad, the record is consistent: standard plans that differ by "less than US$25 across markets at very different income levels", medians that sink below 50 Mbps where users crowd a cell, sign-up freezes in busy cities, and shares of **1.0% in Kenya and 1.4% in Brazil** where fixed broadband is established ([Ookla, 12 Apr 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/low-orbit-high-stakes-starlinks-asia-pacific-performance-and-path-to-market); [Ookla, 15 Jun 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/starlink-ssa-q1-2026); [Communications Authority of Kenya, Sep 2026](https://www.ca.go.ke/sites/default/files/2026-09/Sector%20Statistics%20Report%20Q4%202025-2026.pdf); [Ookla, 29 Jul 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/satellites-at-scale-a-performance-audit-of-starlink-in-brazil)). Indicative Indian pricing of **₹3,000–4,200 a month plus a kit of about ₹33,000** compares with fibre from ₹399 and wireless data that earns operators ₹7.51 a GB, so the realistic market is rural homes beyond fibre, which is Jio's fixed-wireless turf, and enterprise and remote sites, where Airtel-linked Hughes and Bharti-backed OneWeb are the incumbents, rather than Airtel's urban premium users ([Broadcast and CableSat, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.broadcastandcablesat.co.in/satcom-spectrum-pricing-heads-to-cabinet-as-dot-finalizes-its-note/); [RechargePlans.in, Oct 2026](https://rechargeplans.in/broadband/jiofiber/); [TRAI, Jun 2026](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-06/QPIR_22062026.pdf)). Satellite-to-phone service, the only version that could touch mobile, is not permitted in India, runs everywhere else on a partner telco's SIM and spectrum, and is modelled by SpaceX itself at about **$2 a month per user** in lower-middle-income markets such as India ([Broadcast and CableSat, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.broadcastandcablesat.co.in/satcom-spectrum-pricing-heads-to-cabinet-as-dot-finalizes-its-note/); [SpaceX S-1, 20 May 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026036936/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm)). For the IPO, the grey-market premium dipped ₹15 the day after Musk's first post and has since climbed to ₹249, no brokerage note opened for this research puts a number on Starlink, and the risk Nomura stresses is how much of the next tariff hike Jio can keep ([InvestorGain, 18 Oct 2026](https://www.investorgain.com/gmp/jio-platforms-ipo/2306/); [Fortune India, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.fortuneindia.com/markets/ipo/bharti-airtel-vs-jio-ipo-nomura-sees-valuation-gap-narrowing-as-tariff-hikes-loom/164287)).

## The eight answers in plain words

**1. How exposed Jio is.** Jio Platforms reports one business segment and does not disclose revenue by product. The only split in its RHP is "pre-paid connectivity services": 77.08% of FY26 revenue and **76.37% in the June 2026 quarter**, down from 79.35% in FY24 ([Jio RHP, p. 33, 14 Oct 2026](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)). That label covers more than prepaid mobile, so the widely repeated "77% prepaid mobile" is a mislabel. The RHP confines the satellite threat to home broadband, fixed wireless, rural broadband and enterprise and government links, about 5% of customers, and never mentions mobile or satellite-to-phone service. The exposure is small in revenue but sits in Jio's fastest-growing line: fixed broadband grew 55.19% in FY26 ([Jio RHP, p. 215](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)).

**2. Why Musk went after Ambani.** No source has evidence of Musk's intent. The record shows timing: Starlink holds its licences but cannot launch until the Home Ministry finishes a security review and the Cabinet approves spectrum pricing, and both were pending when he posted, in the same week Jio put a 1,600-satellite plan on show and two weeks before its IPO ([Communications Today, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/satcom-spectrum-pricing-heads-to-cabinet-as-dot-finalizes-its-note/); [TelecomTalk, 8 Oct 2026](https://telecomtalk.info/imc2026-jio-plans-satellite-network-connect-600000villages/1012635/)). The March 2025 distribution deals with Jio and Airtel did not restrain him: they are conditional and inactive, and SpaceX described Airtel's as a complement to "our direct offering" ([Bharti Airtel, 11 Mar 2025](https://www.bharti.com/press-release-2024-2025-airtel-announces-agreement-with-spacex-to-bring-starlinks-high-speed-internet-to-its-customers-in-india.html)). Ambani rather than Mittal because Jio is the consumer-broadband incumbent Starlink's dish would compete with, has the IPO, and was the side Reuters described as having "lobbied New Delhi" for auctions in 2024; Bharti-backed OneWeb sells to businesses and governments, was licensed in 2021 and is still waiting, which undercuts any claim that an incumbent controls the queue ([Onmanorama (Reuters), 12 Mar 2025](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/business/2025/03/12/reliance-jio-musk-spacex-starlink-satellite-deal.amp.html); [Outlook India, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.outlookindia.com/national/i-applied-five-years-back-sunil-mittal-takes-a-dig-at-starlink-says-oneweb-still-awaits-govts-nod)).

**3. Whose users.** In homes, Jio's, but only at the edge: rural and semi-urban homes beyond fibre that buy fixed wireless today, a segment where Jio holds about 79% and 5G fixed-wireless users are 51.9% rural ([TRAI, 28 Sep 2026](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-09/PR_No125of2026_0.pdf)). Airtel's premium users are mostly urban postpaid mobile customers and urban fibre homes; a dish does not replace a phone, and in cities 1 Gbps fibre costs ₹3,999 a month against Starlink's only official Indian figure of "up to 200 Mbps" ([MobileTelco, 5 Oct 2026](https://mobiletelco.in/telecom/airtel-xstream-fiber-plans-full-list/); [Business Today, 29 Jul 2025](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/starlink-limited-to-20-lakh-users-in-india-monthly-cost-around-rs-3000-telecom-minister-486720-2025-07-29)). In enterprise, government and remote sites, the incumbents Starlink would fight are Bharti-linked. This report's reading of the evidence: no mass price war, incumbents that are hard to dislodge, and contested ground limited to rural homes and institutional contracts.

**4. Abroad.** Starlink serves 167 countries and markets and had 12.0 million subscribers at 30 June 2026 ([SpaceX 10-Q, filed 4 Aug 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026052535/spcx-20260630.htm)). It did best where fixed broadband was weak or broken (Nigeria, where it is the second-largest ISP; Brazil; Jamaica after a hurricane) and stayed small where fibre was cheap and spreading (1.0% of Kenya's fixed subscriptions against Safaricom's 36.1%; a few thousand users in Bangladesh; shrinking use in Malaysia) ([Space in Africa, 26 Aug 2026](https://spaceinafrica.com/2026/08/26/starlink-narrows-the-gap-to-under-13000-as-nigerias-isp-market-fragments-further-in-q2-2026/); [Communications Authority of Kenya, Sep 2026](https://www.ca.go.ke/sites/default/files/2026-09/Sector%20Statistics%20Report%20Q4%202025-2026.pdf); [Daily Star, 31 Mar 2026](https://www.thedailystar.net/business/economy/news/starlink-gains-just-3500-users-after-8-months-bangladesh-4139626); [Ookla, 12 Apr 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/low-orbit-high-stakes-starlinks-asia-pacific-performance-and-path-to-market)). Against entrenched rivals it got creative on hardware and access (rentals, instalments, Mini kits, Lite tiers, community passes, telco resale, free disaster service) rather than on the monthly bill. Regulators stopped it more often than rivals did.

**5. Speed.** Starlink advertises "up to" speeds, and India's only official figure is "up to 200 Mbps". Measured medians are lower and fall as users pile into a cell: Indonesia's slid to 40.69 Mbps as tests rose a third, Nigeria sits around 48 Mbps, and the US, with capacity added, reached 127.39 Mbps ([Ookla, 12 Apr 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/low-orbit-high-stakes-starlinks-asia-pacific-performance-and-path-to-market); [Ookla, 15 Jun 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/starlink-ssa-q1-2026); [Ookla, 5 May 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/starlink-hits-new-us-highs)). Brazil's median drops about 40% between night and midday, and Jamaica's halved after users nearly tripled ([Ookla, 29 Jul 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/satellites-at-scale-a-performance-audit-of-starlink-in-brazil); [Ookla, 5 Aug 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/jamaica-starlink-data-speeds-drop-after-hurricane-melissa)). Early speeds in a new market flatter because few people share the network. India's median fixed line delivers 63.94 Mbps at 7 ms latency, and its median mobile connection 128.20 Mbps ([Ookla Speedtest Global Index, Aug 2026](https://www.speedtest.net/global-index/india)).

**6. Cost.** There is no official Indian price. Indicative figures are ₹3,000–4,200 a month plus a ₹33,000 kit ([Broadcast and CableSat, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.broadcastandcablesat.co.in/satcom-spectrum-pricing-heads-to-cabinet-as-dot-finalizes-its-note/)), and South Asian neighbours pay about ₹3,000–4,650 a month at today's exchange rate ([BBS, 5 Dec 2024](https://www.bbs.bt/?p=221487); [Financial Express, 20 May 2025](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/national/starlink-officially-begins-operations-in-bangladesh)). ₹3,000 is 7.5 times JioFiber's ₹399 entry plan, and the kit equals 70 months of JioFiber's ₹471 bill with GST ([RechargePlans.in, Oct 2026](https://rechargeplans.in/broadband/jiofiber/)). Abroad, Starlink cuts hardware cost and adds slower tiers rather than cutting the monthly price towards local levels, and it raises prices where cells fill. Musk promised "affordable" pricing on 10 October without a number ([The Week, 11 Oct 2026](https://www.theweek.in/news/sci-tech/2026/10/11/elon-musk-says-starlink-will-be-affordable-in-india-govt-officials-call-claims-on-licence-blatantly-false.html)). This report's reading: a mass broadband price war is unlikely; hardware financing and introductory offers aimed at rural homes are the plausible moves.

**7. Starlink Mobile.** Not in the near term. Today it is a text-and-light-data add-on sold by about 30 partner telcos, delivering roughly 3 Mbps per beam outdoors ([SpaceX S-1, 20 May 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026036936/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm); [arXiv 2506.00283, v8, 23 Nov 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00283)). India has no direct-to-device framework, satellite-to-phone service sits outside the current satcom allocation, and Starlink's phone-capable Gen2 constellation is not authorised in India ([Broadcast and CableSat, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.broadcastandcablesat.co.in/satcom-spectrum-pricing-heads-to-cabinet-as-dot-finalizes-its-note/); [MediaNama, 20 Aug 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/08/223-starlink-india-satellite-network/)). Every route to Indian phones runs through an Indian telco's spectrum or rules not yet written, and SpaceX's own model assumes about $2 a month per user in markets like India, below Jio's ₹215.6 ARPU.

**8. The Zerodha video.** Its first 18 minutes are sound sector economics built on filings and TRAI data. Its 2.5-minute satellite segment is where it goes wrong: it says Airtel, Jio and Starlink have "all regulatory approvals" with only spectrum left (wrong at upload; all three still await security clearance), credits "Airtel" with a $1 billion OneWeb rescue (Bharti Global put in $500 million alongside the UK government's $500 million, per [gov.uk, 3 Jul 2020](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/uk-government-to-acquire-cutting-edge-satellite-network)) and presents Jio's satellite plan as decided (Jio said it was "evaluating"). Uploaded on 11 July, it predates the RHP, the feud and the pricing decision, gives no Starlink prices, speeds or capacity, never mentions satellite-to-phone service, and declines to answer its own title ([Markets by Zerodha Hindi, 11 Jul 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-hT__VuZn2k)).

**How to read the figures.** Rupee figures quoted by a source are kept as that source gave them. Where this report converts dollars or other currencies into rupees, it uses **₹95.08 per US dollar** and the matching cross rates published by open.er-api.com at 00:02 UTC on 18 October 2026 ([open.er-api.com, 18 Oct 2026](https://open.er-api.com/v6/latest/USD)); foreign prices are as of each source's date, so their rupee equivalents are indicative. Jio's RHP converted at an IMF rate that implies about ₹87 a dollar, so its "~₹4,800 (~US$55)" would be about ₹5,229 at today's rate. ## Jio's prospectus fences satellite risk into home and enterprise broadband

### A single segment and a single distributor hide the product mix

Jio Platforms tells investors very little about which products earn its money. The RHP says "the Company operates as a single operating segment" and that "standalone revenue contributions for individual products are not separately disclosed as they are bundled with or subsumed within connectivity services" ([Jio RHP, fn. 75, p. 200, 14 Oct 2026](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)). The one product-type split it gives is the share of revenue from "pre-paid connectivity services", and that share is falling. Reliance Retail, a subsidiary of the promoter, is "the sole distributor of our pre-paid connectivity services", buying recharge vouchers "on a principal-to-principal basis", and the RHP treats any disruption to that arrangement as a stand-alone risk ([Jio RHP, p. 33](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)). The label matters. IndMoney's widely repeated line that "77.08% of total operating revenue comes from pre-paid mobile connectivity recharges" has the right FY26 number and the wrong product: JioFiber and AirFiber also sell prepaid plans, and the RHP does not say whether they sit inside the share ([IndMoney, updated 13 Oct 2026](https://www.indmoney.com/blog/ipo/jio-ipo-starlink-threat)). The newest figure is 76.37%.

| Period | Revenue from operations (₹ crore) | Share from pre-paid connectivity services | Everything else (₹ crore) |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY24 | 1,09,558 | 79.35% | 22,624 |
| FY25 | 1,28,218 | 78.57% | 27,477 |
| FY26 | 1,46,885 | 77.08% | 33,666 |
| Apr–Jun 2026 (Q1 FY27) | 39,173 | 76.37% | 9,257 |

*Source: [Jio RHP, pp. 33 and 131, 14 Oct 2026](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf). "Everything else" is revenue multiplied by the non-prepaid share (arithmetic on RHP figures).*

The non-prepaid pool grew about 22% a year from FY24 to FY26 against about 14% for prepaid, and it holds postpaid mobile, enterprise services, device sales and digital services as well as home broadband, none of which Jio sizes separately ([Jio RHP](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)). An illustration shows why any "home broadband is X% of Jio's revenue" figure is an estimate: if all 28.6 million fixed-broadband homes paid the ₹599–699 list price of entry plans, they would bring in about ₹20,600–24,000 crore a year, 14–16% of FY26 revenue, which the ₹33,666 crore non-prepaid pool could not hold alongside its other lines (UNVERIFIED: arithmetic on list prices, not a disclosure). Either effective home-broadband bills sit well below list, or some home broadband is booked inside "pre-paid". The money is also concentrated in one entity: Reliance Jio Infocomm, the telecom arm, earned **87.74% of FY26 revenue, 94.88% of EBITDA and 93.76% of profit**, and ₹6,830 crore of the parent's ₹16,167 crore of digital-services revenue was billed to that same telecom arm ([Jio RHP, pp. 34, 132, 393](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)).

The customer numbers show where satellite could bite. At 30 June 2026 Jio had **533.3 million customers**, 503.6 million on mobile broadband and 284.9 million on 5G, plus **28.6 million fixed-broadband homes** (14.6 million on JioFiber, 14.0 million on AirFiber), 43.32% of India's 66.1 million connections and about 1.9 times Airtel's 14.9 million ([Jio RHP, pp. 131, 169–170](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)). AirFiber alone held 78.62% of 5G and UBR fixed wireless, Jio took 61.73% of the industry's fixed-broadband additions in the quarter, and its fixed-broadband base grew 55.19% in FY26. ARPU, which the RHP calculates on all of the telecom arm's revenue including home and enterprise, was ₹215.6 a month in the June 2026 quarter on 43.7 GB of data per customer; the frequently quoted ₹214 and 42.3 GB are the March 2026 figures ([Jio RHP, pp. 131–132](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)). Two details tie the exposed base to rural India, where satellite pitches itself: about 46% of the data on Jio's network is consumed in rural areas, and 36% of its June-quarter home-broadband additions were rural ([Jio RHP, p. 203](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)). TRAI's end-August count, the newest, puts Jio at about 43.5% of fixed broadband (arithmetic on TRAI's operator counts), though TRAI's wired category also includes leased lines ([TRAI, 28 Sep 2026](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-09/PR_No125of2026_0.pdf)).

### The RHP argues both sides of the satellite question

The core text is Risk Factor 6, which in the RHP carries satellite in its title: "If we are unable to respond to changes in technologies, including for instance, more competitive satellite-based broadband services and the evolving demands of our customers, we may face technological obsolescence" ([Jio RHP, p. 35](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)). Its central sentence reads, verbatim: "This administrative allocation framework may lower entry economics for satellite-based broadband service providers, including operators of low earth orbit and non-geostationary satellite orbit constellations, when compared to existing digital connectivity players, which are required to acquire spectrum through auctions." The RHP has no "(LEO)" or "(NGSO)" in that sentence; Business Standard's widely copied quotation added them ([Business Standard, 15 Oct 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/jio-platforms-satellite-broadband-india-starlink-leo-spectrum-risk-126101500576_1.html)). The next sentence names the substitutable products: satellite providers "may offer services that directly compete with or substitute our JioFiber, JioAirFiber, UBR-based fixed wireless access, rural broadband, and enterprise and government connectivity solutions, particularly in coverage-gap and underserved areas". The same paragraph says Jio itself "would seek satellite spectrum under the administrative allocation of spectrum", the framework it says lowers rivals' entry costs, and that it "cannot assure" it can deploy its own satellite services "in a timely manner, or at all" ([Jio RHP, p. 35](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)).

The price argument comes from a different voice. The comparison with Starlink's tariffs sits in the Industry Overview, which draws on the Analysys Mason industry report commissioned for the IPO: "Satellite technology is not likely to be able to compete with FWA on pricing (especially with UBR-based FWA which is even cheaper than 5G FWA). For example, Starlink's entry level fixed broadband plans in the USA are priced at ~₹4800 (~US$55) per month, and in most African countries are priced at ~₹2500-4500 (~US$28-50) per month", based on Starlink's website as of August 2026 ([Jio RHP, p. 168](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)). So the consultant argues satellite cannot win on price, while the company's own risk factor lists five lines of business satellite could replace. "Starlink" appears exactly twice in the 591 pages, in that price example and its footnote; "direct-to-device", "D2D", "OneWeb", "Kuiper", "JioSpaceFiber" and "1,600 satellites" do not appear at all ([Jio RHP](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)).

The disclosure grew between June and October. The June draft (DRHP) already carried a shorter satellite passage, as Outlook Money reported at the time, but its technology-risk title did not mention satellite, and it had no sentences on administrative allocation, the draft 2026 spectrum rules or "lower entry economics", no list of substitutable products, and no statement that Jio would seek spectrum administratively ([Jio DRHP, 19 Jun 2026](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/Registration_19062026154852_DRHP.pdf); [Outlook Money, 23 Jun 2026](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/straight-out-of-science-fiction-5-futuristic-risks-mentioned-in-jios-drhp)). The quoted Starlink US price rose from "~₹4500 (~US$50)" (Starlink's website, February 2026) to "~₹4800 (~US$55)" (August 2026), which matches Starlink's own June 2026 increase of its US 100 Mbps plan from $50 to $55 ([9to5Google, 10 Jun 2026](https://9to5google.com/2026/06/10/starlink-drops-purchase-option-raises-prices-for-some-plans/)). The RHP is dated 14 October, one week after Musk's first post; no source links the new language to the feud, so the dates are the only reportable fact.

### Jio's own satellite business barely registers in its accounts

Jio's satellite position today is a licence, a small joint venture and a plan that is not in the prospectus. Jio Satellite Communications holds a unified licence covering GMPCS and VSAT services, valid until March 2042, but earned **₹34.3 crore in FY26, 0.02% of revenue**, apparently all billed to Jio's own telecom arm; "satellite infrastructure" capex fell from ₹81.2 crore in FY24 to ₹3.0 crore in FY26, against total capex of ₹34,184 crore ([Jio RHP, pp. 43, 261, 393, 475](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)). The 51:49 joint venture with SES, which uses SES's geostationary and medium-orbit satellites, is carried at ₹4.1 crore ([Jio RHP, p. 291](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf); [SES, 14 Feb 2022](https://www.ses.com/press-release/jio-platforms-ltd-and-ses-announce-joint-venture-deliver-high-performance-satellite)). TRAI counts **194 JIO-VSAT terminals** in June 2026, 0.11% of India's VSAT market ([TRAI, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-10/QPIR_12102026.pdf)). The sovereign constellation of about 1,600 low-orbit satellites and 23 ground stations shown at India Mobile Congress remains, in Jio's words, "part of a proposed plan" ([TelecomTalk, 8 Oct 2026](https://telecomtalk.info/imc2026-jio-plans-satellite-network-connect-600000villages/1012635/)); Livemint reported, without confirmation from Jio, a test flight in September 2027, 400–450 satellites by 2030 and the full constellation by 2035 ([Livemint, 8 Oct 2026](https://www.livemint.com/technology/jios-1-600-satellite-leo-network-23-ground-stations-remote-area-coverage-and-more-11791457201362.html)). None of the ₹27,500 crore of IPO proceeds earmarked for a stated use goes to satellites; it repays Reliance Jio Infocomm borrowings ([Chittorgarh, updated 15 Oct 2026](https://www.chittorgarh.com/ipo/jio-platforms-ipo/3152/)).

### The IPO market priced the feud for about two days

The issue is 27 crore fresh shares at ₹1,065–1,119, about ₹30,213 crore at the top of the band, with the anchor book on 19 October, subscription from 21 to 23 October and listing around 28 October ([Chittorgarh, updated 15 Oct 2026](https://www.chittorgarh.com/ipo/jio-platforms-ipo/3152/)). Reuters put the valuation at $108 billion on 16 October, the newest figure (it had reported $106 billion on 11 October) ([Khaleej Times (Reuters), 16 Oct 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/asia/ambani-reliance-starlink-competition-first-response-musk)). Bloomberg reported that earlier expectations "had reached as high as $170 billion" and that roadshow investors questioned the valuation target and Jio's "plans to charge for its digital services" ([Business Today (Bloomberg), 14 Oct 2026](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/ipo-corner/story/jio-platforms-ipo-anchor-book-to-draw-bids-over-20-billion-says-report-561477-2026-10-14)). No source attributes that reset to Starlink. The grey-market premium, unofficial dealer talk rather than a forecast, fell from ₹182 to ₹167 on 8 October, was back at ₹182 by 12 October and stood at **₹249 at 21:37 on 18 October**, 22.25% over the top of the band ([InvestorGain, 18 Oct 2026](https://www.investorgain.com/gmp/jio-platforms-ipo/2306/)). ## Ambani was the target with the consumer overlap, the IPO and the 2024 grudge

### Starlink is licensed; it lacks a security clearance and a spectrum price

Musk asked on 8 October "why still no license?", but Starlink has held ISP, GMPCS and VSAT authorisations since 6 June 2025, an IN-SPACe authorisation for its first-generation constellation of 4,408 satellites dated 8 July 2025 (Gulf News gives 9 July), and provisional spectrum for trials since September 2025 ([Outlook India, 9 Oct 2026](https://www.outlookindia.com/international/modis-starlink-dilemma-how-to-balance-security-competition-and-corporate-power); [PTI via The Week, 9 Jul 2025](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/07/09/del69-space-ld-starlink.html); [Business Standard, 8 Oct 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/starlink-india-entry-why-elon-musk-satellite-internet-launch-clearance-126100800416_1.html)). Government sources called his licensing claim "blatantly false" ([The Week, 11 Oct 2026](https://www.theweek.in/news/sci-tech/2026/10/11/elon-musk-says-starlink-will-be-affordable-in-india-govt-officials-call-claims-on-licence-blatantly-false.html)). What Starlink lacks is the Home Ministry's second-stage security clearance, Cabinet approval of spectrum pricing followed by formal assignment, and, uniquely among the three licensees, approval of foreign investment in its wholly SpaceX-owned Indian unit, still awaited on 8 October ([Broadcast and CableSat, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.broadcastandcablesat.co.in/satcom-spectrum-pricing-heads-to-cabinet-as-dot-finalizes-its-note/); [Business Standard, 8 Oct 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/starlink-india-entry-why-elon-musk-satellite-internet-launch-clearance-126100800416_1.html)). Telecom minister Jyotiraditya Scindia says security clearance is for the Home Ministry, "a decision that will be taken by them", and gives no timeline ([MediaNama, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-satcom-licensees-security-clearance/)). The government says Starlink, Jio Satellite Communications and Eutelsat OneWeb are "at broadly the same regulatory stage" and calls claims of discrimination "baseless and misconceived" ([ANI, 9 Oct 2026](https://www.aninews.in/news/business/mha-assesses-risks-of-all-satcom-firms-mos-communications-pemmasani-responds-to-musks-claims20261009131119/); [BBC via AOL, 9 Oct 2026](https://www.aol.com/articles/why-elon-musk-taking-indias-064242000.html)). Bloomberg reported on 9 June that India had "effectively frozen approvals" for Starlink over terminals used in the Iran war; Starlink called the report misleading, and no official document confirms it ([Bloomberg via Bloomberg Tax, 9 Jun 2026](https://news.bloombergtax.com/capital-markets/starlink-india-launch-hits-security-roadblock-before-spacex-ipo)).

The security conditions explain why Starlink's public defence centres on its Indian gateways. Since May 2025 all Indian traffic must pass through gateways in India with no bypass through inter-satellite links, lawful interception and DNS resolution must happen in India, terminals must report their location, and operators must support monitoring within 50 km of borders and coastlines ([Business Standard, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/explained-why-starlink-s-india-launch-plans-have-raised-security-concerns-126101200434_1.html); [ORF, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.orfonline.org/expert-speak/india-s-satellite-imperative-starlink-and-the-terms-of-entry)). A Takshashila Institution report, cited by Business Standard and MediaNama, notes seizures of unauthorised Starlink hardware in the Andaman Sea, off Gujarat, on the Punjab border and in Manipur, about 50,000 smuggled terminals operating in Iran at the start of 2026, and that India cannot independently verify in-orbit compliance; the ₹900 crore National SATCOM Monitoring Facility had not started operating as of April 2026 ([Business Standard, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/explained-why-starlink-s-india-launch-plans-have-raised-security-concerns-126101200434_1.html); [MediaNama, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-rss-affiliate-wants-india-to-block-starlink/)). Starlink's VP Lauren Dreyer says it has built "20 gateway sites comprising hundreds of antennas"; the newest trade report, on 16 October, says "around 10 gateways and a hub in Mumbai" ([ThePrint, 7 Oct 2026](https://theprint.in/tech/as-launch-awaits-clearance-starlink-says-20-gateways-built-indian-user-data-to-stay-in-india/3064053/); [Broadcast and CableSat, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.broadcastandcablesat.co.in/satcom-spectrum-pricing-heads-to-cabinet-as-dot-finalizes-its-note/)). Musk's "five years complying" also covers November 2021, when Starlink took US$99 pre-orders without a licence and the Department of Telecommunications told the public not to subscribe ([Business Standard, 8 Oct 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/starlink-india-entry-why-elon-musk-satellite-internet-launch-clearance-126100800416_1.html); [ORF, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.orfonline.org/expert-speak/india-s-satellite-imperative-starlink-and-the-terms-of-entry)).

The spectrum price has moved away from Starlink's request and towards the incumbents', though not by their method. TRAI recommended in May 2025 an annual charge of 4% of adjusted gross revenue (AGR), a ₹500 fee per urban subscriber, a floor of ₹3,500 per MHz and subsidies for rural terminals; the Digital Communications Commission (DCC) approved on 3 September 2026 a **5% charge (4% in notified remote, hilly, island and border areas)** for five years plus two, and dropped the urban fee and the terminal subsidy ([Communications Today, 9 Sep 2026](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/dcc-clears-satcom-spectrum-charges-amazon-leo-faces-relicensing/)). With the 8% licence fee, operators would pay 13% of AGR (12% in remote areas); Starlink and Amazon's Kuiper had asked for under 1%, and the Cabinet note was still being prepared in mid-October ([Communications Today, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/satcom-spectrum-pricing-heads-to-cabinet-as-dot-finalizes-its-note/)). The telcos' body COAI called TRAI's proposal "unjustifiably low" and argued that terrestrial operators' auction costs come to "18-53% of AGR" ([MediaNama, 3 Jun 2025](https://www.medianama.com/2025/06/223-trai-level-playing-field-satcom-recommendations-coai/)). That gap is the mechanism behind the RHP's "lower entry economics" sentence. TRAI chairman Anil Kumar Lahoti answered that "it's not factually correct that satcom services are competing with terrestrial services" ([TelecomTalk, 2 Jun 2025](https://telecomtalk.info/telcos-oppose-trai-satcom-spectrum-plan-unfair/995229/)).

### The motives on record, from best to least supported

No source documents Musk's intent, and the explanations on record differ sharply in support. The best-supported is competitive frustration. Next is pressure on the two pending decisions: Moneylife finds "no evidence that Ambani decides the outcome" but concedes that the security review is "the only step with no clock" and that "incumbents have a commercial interest in the pace" ([Moneylife, 10 Oct 2026](https://www.moneylife.in/article/musk-vs-india-is-ambani-really-blocking-starlink/81910.html)). Reuters tied the escalation to the IPO, coming "days before Jio's parent launches the country's largest-ever initial public offering" ([Al Jazeera (Reuters), 11 Oct 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/10/11/musk-vows-affordable-starlink-in-india-as-he-escalates-feud-with-ambani)), and Benzinga's Ananya Gairola read Musk as arguing "that affordable Starlink internet could challenge Jio's dominance" as the IPO approached ([Benzinga via Yahoo, 13 Oct 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/articles/elon-musk-takes-swipe-billionaire-183008778.html)). The weakest are US leverage, where Outlook India found "no established evidence that Musk's campaign is coordinated with Washington's trade negotiations" even though the US Trade Representative's 2026 report asks India for an "open skies" satellite policy ([Outlook India, 9 Oct 2026](https://www.outlookindia.com/international/modis-starlink-dilemma-how-to-balance-security-competition-and-corporate-power)), and signalling to SpaceX shareholders, which no analyst or journalist argued; SpaceX's shares fell 15% from 6 to 16 October, and coverage tied that to a Ukraine row and tech selling, not India ([Yahoo Finance chart data, retrieved 18 Oct 2026](https://query1.finance.yahoo.com/v8/finance/chart/SPCX?period1=1790000000&period2=1792400000&interval=1d); [Motley Fool, 15 Oct 2026](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/10/15/why-spacex-stock-is-sinking-today/)).

### Why Ambani and not Mittal

Bloomberg read the first post's "certain oligarchs" as aimed at both Ambani and Mittal; every later post named only Ambani ([Yahoo Finance (Bloomberg), 8 Oct 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/articles/elon-musk-accuses-indian-billionaires-122408378.html)). The 2024 fight was framed as Reliance's: Reliance wrote privately to the minister arguing for auctions, and Reuters later wrote that "Reliance had urged an auction but the Indian government sided with Musk", even though Mittal argued at IMC 2024 that satellite firms serving cities should "take the telecom licenses like everybody else" ([Outlook Business (Reuters), 14 Oct 2024](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/amp/story/explainers/mukesh-ambani-vs-elon-musk-war-over-satellite-spectrum-intensifies-as-jio-lobbies-hard-for-auction); [Onmanorama (Reuters), 12 Mar 2025](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/business/2025/03/12/reliance-jio-musk-spacex-starlink-satellite-deal.amp.html); [TechCrunch, 15 Oct 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/10/15/india-backs-musk-in-satellite-spectrum-allocation-row)). Jio's IPO gave the posts a platform, and its RHP names Starlink. And Reliance has a political profile Musk chose to use, sharing reports on Reliance-linked donations; TechCrunch notes Reliance "works with Prime Minister Narendra Modi's government on major projects", and Leader of the Opposition Rahul Gandhi joined in without naming anyone: "Welcome to India, Elon. Mittal was a weaker target. Airtel signed with SpaceX first, and Shotwell said Airtel "has played a pivotal role in India's telecom story" ([Bharti Airtel, 11 Mar 2025](https://www.bharti.com/press-release-2024-2025-airtel-announces-agreement-with-spacex-to-bring-starlinks-high-speed-internet-to-its-customers-in-india.html)). Airtel has no IPO to make news around. Bharti's group is also a SpaceX partner abroad: Airtel Africa agreed in May 2025 to distribute Starlink to businesses in its 14 markets and in August 2026 launched Starlink's satellite-to-phone service in the DRC ([Via Satellite, 6 May 2025](https://www.satellitetoday.com/connectivity/2025/05/06/airtel-africa-to-distribute-starlink-connectivity-in-african-markets); [Connecting Africa, 18 Aug 2026](https://www.connectingafrica.com/connectivity/airtel-africa-starlink-launch-satellite-to-mobile-service-in-drc)). No source links these ties to Musk's choice of target, no reporting of a personal Musk–Mittal relationship was found, and no Musk reply to Mittal's "queue" remark was found. The web runs deeper than the feud suggests: SpaceX director Donald Harrison "also sits on the board of directors of Reliance Jio", according to SpaceX's own prospectus, and SpaceX launches satellites for SES (Jio's joint-venture partner) and Eutelsat OneWeb ([SpaceX 424B4, 11 Jun 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026042639/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm)).

### What "Much appreciated" did and did not change

## Starlink's realistic Indian customers sit beyond fibre and inside enterprises

### In homes, the overlap is Jio's fixed wireless, not Airtel's fibre

India's home-broadband market is small and split by geography. At end-August 2026 there were 48.63 million wired and 19.34 million fixed-wireless connections against 1,033 million mobile broadband users; Jio led wired (14.81 million against Airtel's 11.44 million) and 5G fixed wireless (9.42 million against 4.03 million), and was the only operator reporting UBR fixed wireless (5.37 million) ([TRAI, 28 Sep 2026](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-09/PR_No125of2026_0.pdf)). **5G fixed-wireless users are 51.90% rural**, and Jio holds about 79% of 5G and UBR fixed wireless combined (arithmetic on TRAI's counts). Airtel's fixed base is almost entirely urban: only 163,579 of its 12.28 million wireline lines (1.3%) are rural, against 7.4% for Jio and 32.9% for BSNL (arithmetic on the same TRAI release). Airtel is also retreating from the product that looks most like satellite. Executive vice chairman Gopal Vittal said "almost 95% odd of the market is in 400 cities... we have decided to pivot to fiber and make sure that fixed wireless access only goes where fiber is not accessible", and Airtel India CEO Shashwat Sharma said low-priced fixed-wireless entry plans brought "higher churn" ([Airtel Q1 FY27 call, 5 Aug 2026](https://assets.airtel.in/static-assets/cms/investor/docs/quarterly_results/2026-27/Q2/Earnings-Call-Transcript.pdf)). Airtel's Homes ARPU fell to ₹523 from ₹537 a year earlier, and its net additions fell to 473,000 from 1,135,000 the quarter before ([Airtel IR Pack, Aug 2026](https://assets.airtel.in/static-assets/cms/investor/docs/quarterly_results/2026-27/Q1/Quarterly-IR-Pack-Bharti-Airtel-Consolidated.pdf)). Only about 20% of Indian households have fixed broadband (about 42% urban, 6% rural) ([Analysys Mason for Jio, 17 Jun 2026](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Analysys%20Mason%20-%20Industry%20Report%20for%20Jio%202026-06-17.pdf)), and 8,985 of 6,44,131 villages had no mobile network or internet in Ministry data presented in March 2026 ([ETV Bharat, 12 Oct 2026](https://etvbharat.com/hi/bharat/indian-8985-villages-no-mobile-internet-network-india-pakistan-border-248-villages-also-have-no-tower-eb-prime-hin26101204701)).

At these prices Starlink is a premium-priced, lower-speed product in any city with fibre: ₹3,000 a month sits between Airtel's ₹1,599 plan for 300 Mbps and the ₹3,999 1 Gbps tier, and the indicative range is 5–7 times Jio's ₹599 AirFiber entry plan ([MobileTelco, 5 Oct 2026](https://mobiletelco.in/telecom/airtel-xstream-fiber-plans-full-list/)). An illustration of the affordability gap: Analysys Mason puts Indian connectivity spending at about 1.2% of disposable income, so a household spending that share on a ₹3,000 plan would need about ₹30 lakh a year of disposable income, against the ₹8.29 lakh threshold the same report uses for fixed-broadband affordability (this report's arithmetic on [Analysys Mason, 17 Jun 2026](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Analysys%20Mason%20-%20Industry%20Report%20for%20Jio%202026-06-17.pdf)). Satya N. Gupta, a former principal adviser at TRAI, says satellite internet is being positioned for higher-income users ([Communications Today, 9 Sep 2026](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/dcc-clears-satcom-spectrum-charges-amazon-leo-faces-relicensing/)), and in 2024 Mittal's stated worry was precisely satellite firms "who have ambitions to come into urban areas serving elite retail customers" ([Times of India, 15 Oct 2024](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/technology/tech-news/bharti-airtel-chairman-sunil-mittal-supports-this-reliance-jio-demand-in-his-address-at-imc2024/articleshow/114242279.cms)). There is a counterweight. India's median fixed line delivers 63.94 Mbps, 100th in the world, below Starlink's measured medians in Bangladesh (88.95 Mbps) and Sri Lanka (140.68 Mbps), and abroad Starlink wins city users where fixed service is poor: in Brazil it has more subscribers in highly urbanised areas than in rural ones ([Ookla Speedtest Global Index, Aug 2026](https://www.speedtest.net/global-index/india); [Ookla, 12 Apr 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/low-orbit-high-stakes-starlinks-asia-pacific-performance-and-path-to-market); [Ookla, 29 Jul 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/satellites-at-scale-a-performance-audit-of-starlink-in-brazil)). Starlink's latency (35–103 ms in those two markets, against 7 ms on Indian fixed lines) and its capacity limits in dense cells, set out below, cap that niche. This report's reading: the household overlap is Jio's, in fibre-dark towns and villages, among the minority of homes that can pay several times a fixed-wireless bill.

### Premium mobile users are beyond a dish's reach

Airtel's premium franchise is mobile. It earned ARPU of ₹264 in the June 2026 quarter against Jio's ₹215.6, a gap of ₹48.4 (22.4%) that widened from ₹43.2 the quarter before, and it had 30.0 million postpaid customers, 7.97% of its 376.5 million mobile base ([TelecomTalk, Aug 2026](https://telecomtalk.info/airtel-reliance-jio-vodafone-idea-arpu-q1fy27/1010651/); [Airtel IR Pack, Aug 2026](https://assets.airtel.in/static-assets/cms/investor/docs/quarterly_results/2026-27/Q1/Quarterly-IR-Pack-Bharti-Airtel-Consolidated.pdf)). With fewer customers, Bharti earns 40.0% of access revenue against Jio's 44.6% (arithmetic on TRAI's April–June 2026 figures) ([TRAI, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-10/QPIR_12102026.pdf)). Nomura argues Airtel's "higher proportion of premium subscribers" should insulate it from downtrading after the anticipated tariff hike ([Business Today, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/markets/stocks/story/jio-ipo-why-bharti-airtel-share-price-is-rising-ahead-of-rs-30213-crore-offer-562037-2026-10-16)). A rooftop dish does not substitute for a phone, and satellite-to-phone service is not permitted in India (see the direct-to-cell section), so "skimming Airtel's premium users" applies only to homes, where Airtel's base is urban, served by fibre and paying a falling average of ₹523 a month. Airtel's own June-quarter earnings call never mentions satellite, Starlink or OneWeb ([Airtel Q1 FY27 call, 5 Aug 2026](https://assets.airtel.in/static-assets/cms/investor/docs/quarterly_results/2026-27/Q2/Earnings-Call-Transcript.pdf)).

### Enterprise, government and remote sites are Bharti's turf

India's existing satellite-broadband market is small, shrinking and Airtel-linked. VSAT users fell 8.28% in a quarter to **1,84,579 in June 2026**; Hughes Communications India holds 67.64% and Nelco 28.64%, while JIO-VSAT has 0.11% ([TRAI, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-10/QPIR_12102026.pdf)). Airtel has owned about 33% of Hughes Communications India since January 2022 ([Bharti Airtel filing to NSE, 5 Jan 2022](https://archives.nseindia.com/corporate/BHARTIARTL_05012022090755_VSATdealupdate.pdf)), and Bharti Space owns 17.88% of Eutelsat, the second-largest stake after the French state's 29.65% ([Eutelsat, 25 Nov 2025](https://www.eutelsat.com/system/files/2025-11/DOC_Investor_Amendment-URD-2024-25_EN_251125.pdf); [Economic Times, 14 Oct 2026](https://m.economictimes.com/industry/telecom/telecom-news/eutelsat-oneweb-much-ahead-of-starlink-in-licence-queue-sunil-mittal/articleshow/134948632.cms)); Business Standard's 8 October description of Bharti as Eutelsat's largest shareholder is out of date ([Business Standard, 8 Oct 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/sunil-mittal-responds-to-musk-says-eutelsat-oneweb-also-awaiting-approvals-126100800646_1.html)). OneWeb's lined-up customers are government and enterprise users on land, at sea and in the air through Tata's Nelco, plus disaster recovery with the National Disaster Management Authority, and Airtel's regulatory chief Rahul Vatts says its infrastructure is ready and security demonstrations are done ([Developing Telecoms, 2025](https://developingtelecoms.com/telecom-technology/satellite-communications-networks/18906-tata-s-nelco-to-sell-eutelsat-oneweb-leo-services-in-india.html); [ANI, 9 Oct 2026](https://www.aninews.in/news/business/airtel-backed-eutelsat-oneweb-ready-with-infrastructure-awaits-govt-nod-for-satellite-services-rahul-vatts20261009194528/)). Trade press sees "enterprise, government, and backhaul contracts the more natural early revenue base", from mining and maritime to remote branch banking, defence and disaster response ([Broadcast and CableSat, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.broadcastandcablesat.co.in/satcom-spectrum-pricing-heads-to-cabinet-as-dot-finalizes-its-note/)), and Deloitte's 2023 forecast of a US$1.9 billion Indian satellite-broadband market by 2030 rests on the same institutional buyers ([ORF, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.orfonline.org/expert-speak/india-s-satellite-imperative-starlink-and-the-terms-of-entry)). This report's reading: in satellite against satellite, Starlink's main Indian rival is Mittal's group, not Ambani's.

### Capacity, kit cost and a 13% levy constrain a price war

Musk's only pricing commitment is qualitative, and he framed the stakes himself: "Ambani's profits will be lower, but he is not going out of business" ([Deccan Herald, 11 Oct 2026](https://www.deccanherald.com/business/musk-calls-criticism-of-starlinks-india-entry-hypocrisy-backs-competition-4179019)). The case for aggressive pricing comes from a July 2025 JM Financial expert discussion, which sketched introductory consumer plans of US$20–25 a month (about ₹1,900–2,380 at today's rate) falling to about US$10 for the mass market, a cost per user that drops from about ₹11,250 a month at 100,000 users to about ₹450 at 2.5 million, and "a market share risk to local telecom operators' home broadband services in the long term" ([Business Today, 16 Jul 2025](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/markets/stocks/story/starlinks-satellite-internet-launch-in-india-potential-impact-on-airtel-and-jio-484844-2025-07-16)); JM Financial's March 2025 report had concluded that Starlink "will not pose a major threat" to Jio or Airtel ([The Tribune, 15 Mar 2025](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/musks-starlink-poses-limited-threat-to-local-telecom-giants-report/)).

Three constraints weigh against a mass price war. The first is capacity. Minister of State Chandra Sekhar Pemmasani said in July 2025 that "Starlink can have only 20 lakh customers in India and offer up to 200 Mbps speed. That won't affect telecom services", and officials said the figure reflects Starlink's capacity rather than a policy limit; no licence or rule text containing a cap was found, although Finshots describes it as a government cap ([Business Today, 29 Jul 2025](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/starlink-limited-to-20-lakh-users-in-india-monthly-cost-around-rs-3000-telecom-minister-486720-2025-07-29); [Finshots, 15 Oct 2026](https://finshots.in/archive/can-starlink-win-indias-internet-war/)). PTI described the authorised first-generation constellation as capable of "approximately 600-GBPS throughput over India" ([PTI via The Week, 9 Jul 2025](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/07/09/del69-space-ld-starlink.html)): running flat out for a month, that is about 194 petabytes, roughly 0.75% of the 25,984 petabytes India's wireless networks carried each month in early 2026, and shared among 20 lakh users online at once it is 0.3 Mbps each (this report's arithmetic on PTI's figure and [TRAI, Jun 2026](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-06/QPIR_22062026.pdf)). An unattributed estimate repeated by trade press puts one constellation at "only 10,000 to 20,000 connections in a market the size of Delhi, against broadband demand estimated at 50 lakh connections" ([Communications Today, 9 Sep 2026](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/dcc-clears-satcom-spectrum-charges-amazon-leo-faces-relicensing/)). The same capacity is large against today's satellite demand, which Analysys Mason, as cited by Finshots, puts at 44 Gbps in 2023 rising to over 3 Tbps by 2032 ([Finshots, 15 Oct 2026](https://finshots.in/archive/can-starlink-win-indias-internet-war/)). COAI argued the opposite in 2025, claiming Starlink's and Kuiper's planned capacity of about 29 billion GB a month exceeds the telcos' 23 billion GB, without giving its basis ([Broadcast and CableSat, 6 Jun 2025](https://www.broadcastandcablesat.co.in/coai-to-govt-trais-satellite-spectrum-pricing-defies-level-playing-field-2/)). The second constraint is the kit: the DCC dropped TRAI's rural terminal subsidy, so operators "will either absorb hardware costs or keep premium pricing" ([Broadcast and CableSat, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.broadcastandcablesat.co.in/satcom-spectrum-pricing-heads-to-cabinet-as-dot-finalizes-its-note/)). The third is the levy of 13% of AGR on every rupee of revenue.

This report's reading: Starlink's 20 lakh ceiling would equal about 3% of India's 66.1 million fixed-broadband connections and about 11 times today's entire VSAT base, which makes Starlink a serious rival to satellite incumbents and a marginal one to Jio's and Airtel's home businesses. The plausible price moves are introductory offers, hardware financing and resale through Jio and Airtel themselves, not a sustained cut towards fixed-wireless prices.

### Shares stay near 1% wherever fixed broadband is established

Kenya is the cleanest test of Starlink against a strong incumbent. At 30 June 2026 Starlink had **27,616 fixed data subscriptions, a 1.0% share, against Safaricom's 1,024,950 (36.1%)** ([Communications Authority of Kenya, Sep 2026](https://www.ca.go.ke/sites/default/files/2026-09/Sector%20Statistics%20Report%20Q4%202025-2026.pdf)), and in the first quarter of 2026 Safaricom added 83,107 fixed subscribers while Starlink added 2,717, about 30 to 1 ([TechCabal, 18 Jun 2026](https://techcabal.com/2026/06/18/starlink-grabs-headlines-but-safaricom-keeps-winning-broadband-users/)). Brazil, which Starlink calls its second-biggest market, had 791,052 contracts in May 2026, a 1.4% share and 13th place, though Starlink was the second-largest net adder from January to May (134,639, behind Vivo's 253,974) and had more subscribers in highly urbanised areas (347,990) than rural ones (213,291) ([Ookla, 29 Jul 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/satellites-at-scale-a-performance-audit-of-starlink-in-brazil)). Nigeria is the outlier: 98,642 subscribers in the June 2026 quarter made Starlink the second-largest ISP behind Spectranet's 111,384, and it kept growing after a 50% price rise, but in a market with about 320,000 fibre connections against 192 million mobile users ([Space in Africa, 26 Aug 2026](https://spaceinafrica.com/2026/08/26/starlink-narrows-the-gap-to-under-13000-as-nigerias-isp-market-fragments-further-in-q2-2026/); [Ookla, 28 Sep 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/nigeria-connectivity-2026)). Bangladesh is the nearest Indian analogue and the thinnest result: the newest figure is 5,389 users by May 2026, mostly corporate backup users, up from the regulator's count of 3,469 in January ([Finshots, 15 Oct 2026](https://finshots.in/archive/can-starlink-win-indias-internet-war/); [Daily Star, 31 Mar 2026](https://www.thedailystar.net/business/economy/news/starlink-gains-just-3500-users-after-8-months-bangladesh-4139626)). There, local broadband runs from about Tk 400 to Tk 1,200, Starlink is "at least ten times higher", and Felicity IDC's chief executive Sharful Alam says "It's unlikely that people will abandon existing broadband services, given the significant price difference" ([Daily Star, 31 Mar 2026](https://www.thedailystar.net/business/economy/news/starlink-gains-just-3500-users-after-8-months-bangladesh-4139626)). Satellite broadband reaches 2.2% of connected Philippine households ([Ookla, 12 Apr 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/low-orbit-high-stakes-starlinks-asia-pacific-performance-and-path-to-market)).

### Incumbents answered with speed, and Starlink answered with hardware

Kenya shows the full sequence. In July 2024 Safaricom asked the regulator to block satellite ISPs with operations abroad, a proposal the regulator did not take up ([TechCabal, 23 Sep 2024](https://techcabal.com/2024/09/23/safaricom-internet-speeds-upgraded/); [TechCabal, 18 Apr 2025](https://techcabal.com/2025/04/18/safaricom-5g-router-versus-starlink-push-rural-kenya/)). On 23 September 2024 Safaricom doubled its fibre speeds "in response to Starlink's growing presence", in TechCabal's words ([TechCabal, 23 Sep 2024](https://techcabal.com/2024/09/23/safaricom-internet-speeds-upgraded/)); three days later Starlink launched a KES 27,000 Mini kit and a US$30.87 Lite plan ([TechCabal, 26 Sep 2024](https://techcabal.com/2024/09/26/starlink-residential-internet/)). A KES 1,950 (US$15) monthly kit rental from August 2024 drove a 104.7% jump in satellite subscriptions in one quarter, after which Nairobi's sign-ups were halted for oversubscription ([Connecting Africa, 22 Aug 2024](https://www.connectingafrica.com/connectivity/kenyans-can-now-rent-starlink-kits-for-15-per-month); [Connecting Africa, 28 Jan 2025](https://www.connectingafrica.com/connectivity/starlink-doubles-market-share-in-kenya-launches-ground-station)); the rental has since been discontinued, as far as Ookla understands ([Ookla, 15 Jun 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/starlink-ssa-q1-2026)). In 2025 Safaricom cut 5G router prices and sent sales teams into fibre-dark rural areas, where one customer told TechCabal "Starlink's initial cost is too high" ([TechCabal, 18 Apr 2025](https://techcabal.com/2025/04/18/safaricom-5g-router-versus-starlink-push-rural-kenya/)). Starlink added a six-month instalment plan for its kit in January 2026, in a market where, Business Daily wrote, "uptake has recently shown volatility", and in April 2026 Safaricom doubled speeds on several fibre packages without raising prices ([Business Daily, 15 Jan 2026](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/technology/starlink-cuts-upfront-cost-with-instalment-payments-plan-5327602); [TechCabal, 18 Jun 2026](https://techcabal.com/2026/06/18/starlink-grabs-headlines-but-safaricom-keeps-winning-broadband-users/)).

Elsewhere the pattern repeats. In Malaysia, Starlink's test samples fell 27.2% as the MYR 21 billion JENDELA fibre programme spread, and in June 2026 it withdrew a Lite plan that Ookla had called "a direct response" to fibre competition ([Ookla, 12 Apr 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/low-orbit-high-stakes-starlinks-asia-pacific-performance-and-path-to-market); [Lowyat, 23 Jun 2026](https://www.lowyat.net/2026/396556/starlink-malaysia-removes-residential-lite-plan/)). In Indonesia, the launch was anchored on connecting more than 2,700 community health centres, and the competition authority KPPU judged launch discounts "promotional", not predatory, after the satellite industry complained ([Ookla, 12 Apr 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/low-orbit-high-stakes-starlinks-asia-pacific-performance-and-path-to-market); [Developing Telecoms, 31 May 2024](https://developingtelecoms.com/telecom-technology/satellite-communications-networks/16786-starlink-s-indonesia-pricing-is-not-predatory-antimonopoly-agency.html)). In Harare's Mbare, five households split one connection at US$10 each ([MyZimbabwe, 7 May 2026](https://www.myzimbabwe.co.zw/technology/187394-us10-per-household-for-starlink-in-harare-ghetto-starlink-shakes-internet-market-zim-isps-forced-to-reduce-prices.html)), and Starlink has since formalised sharing, selling hourly to monthly passes through community hosts in countries it has not named ([TechAfrica News, 7 Oct 2026](https://techafricanews.com/2026/10/07/starlink-expands-communities-program-with-new-shared-internet-income-model/)). After Hurricane Melissa, Starlink gave free service in Jamaica; Liberty Latin America's Flow lost 57,000 residential internet subscribers and won back about a thousand ([Ookla, 5 Aug 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/jamaica-starlink-data-speeds-drop-after-hurricane-melissa)). Many incumbents chose to resell Starlink rather than fight it: Airtel Africa, Vodacom, Globe, Robi and TelOne all signed distribution, network or satellite-to-phone deals ([Via Satellite, 6 May 2025](https://www.satellitetoday.com/connectivity/2025/05/06/airtel-africa-to-distribute-starlink-connectivity-in-african-markets); [MarketScreener (Reuters), late 2025](https://in.marketscreener.com/news/vodacom-inks-africa-internet-deal-with-musk-s-starlink-ce7d5fded18af522); [GMA News, 8 Jul 2026](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/money/companies/994149/globe-sets-p99-entry-price-for-starlink-satellite-to-mobile-service/story/); [Daily Star, 31 Mar 2026](https://www.thedailystar.net/business/economy/news/starlink-gains-just-3500-users-after-8-months-bangladesh-4139626); [Connecting Africa, 4 Nov 2024](https://www.connectingafrica.com/connectivity/starlink-s-entry-into-zimbabwe-lowers-internet-tariffs)). Ookla analyst Karim Yaici sums up Africa: "Satellite is a partner for mobile operators, not a rival" ([Ookla, 15 Jun 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/starlink-ssa-q1-2026)). This report's reading: cheap entry and good speed in dense areas did not coexist, because Kenya's rental filled Nairobi within months.

### Regulators, not rivals, stopped it outright

Ookla's Johan concludes that "regulatory posture, not demand, determines where Starlink operates" ([Ookla, 12 Apr 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/low-orbit-high-stakes-starlinks-asia-pacific-performance-and-path-to-market)). South Africa's 30% local-ownership rule has kept Starlink out, with a 2027 launch "the likely timeline" according to Ookla; Namibia rejected its licence in March 2026 under a 51% local-ownership law; Thailand stalled in November 2025 over foreign ownership; and Pakistan had issued no licence by September 2026, 18 months after provisional registration, with monitoring and in-country gateway conditions attached ([Ookla, 15 Jun 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/starlink-ssa-q1-2026); [Capital FM (BBC), 24 Mar 2026](https://capitalfm.africa/elon-musks-starlink-blocked-from-operating-in-namibia/); [NextUp News, 2 Sep 2026](https://www.nextupnews.pk/2026/09/starlink-in-pakistan-2026-license.html)). Price regulators slowed it too: Malawi's regulator rejected a currency-linked price rise and Starlink paused residential sales, and Nigeria's regulator forced it to reverse a 2024 rise before a later one went through ([Techzim, 30 Oct 2024](https://www.techzim.co.zw/2024/10/starlink-suspends-new-kit-sales-and-subscriptions-in-malawi-due-to-currency-pricing-and-regulatory-issues-similar-problems-in-nigeria/); [Space in Africa, 30 Apr 2025](https://spaceinafrica.com/2025/04/30/starlink-implements-50-price-increase-for-nigerian-residential-plans/)). Vietnam caps Starlink at 600,000 terminals, about 2.5% of its market, and Sri Lanka's launch slipped to July 2025 over lawful interception ([VnEconomy, 15 Aug 2026](https://en.vneconomy.vn/starlink-officially-launches-in-vietnam-prices-starting-at-43month.htm); [Ookla, 12 Apr 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/low-orbit-high-stakes-starlinks-asia-pacific-performance-and-path-to-market)). This report's reading: India's security-led wait resembles Pakistan and Sri Lanka more than any market where Starlink has been beaten commercially.

## Speeds fall to 35–50 Mbps where users crowd a cell

Starlink sells "up to" speeds: up to 200 Mbit/s in Kenya, 300 Mbps in Bangladesh, 110 Mbps in Bhutan and, by the minister's account, 200 Mbps in India ([Connecting Africa, 22 Aug 2024](https://www.connectingafrica.com/connectivity/kenyans-can-now-rent-starlink-kits-for-15-per-month); [Financial Express, 20 May 2025](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/national/starlink-officially-begins-operations-in-bangladesh); [BBS, 5 Dec 2024](https://www.bbs.bt/?p=221487); [Business Today, 29 Jul 2025](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/starlink-limited-to-20-lakh-users-in-india-monthly-cost-around-rs-3000-telecom-minister-486720-2025-07-29)). What users get depends on how many neighbours share the same patch of sky: "Because Starlink is a finite, shared resource, users' network speeds can fluctuate on a daily – or even hourly – basis" ([Ookla, 4 Feb 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/2025-global-satellite-broadband-performance-report)). Ookla's data contain several natural experiments.

The daily cycle shows the same constraint. In Brazil the median peaked at 149.5 Mbps around 2 a.m. and fell to 90.6 Mbps by 1 p.m. in the first quarter of 2026, "a nearly 40% drop in speed during peak business hours", with "virtually no users consistently reaching gigabit speeds" ([Ookla, 29 Jul 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/satellites-at-scale-a-performance-audit-of-starlink-in-brazil)). Starlink manages full cells by closing them: sign-ups were halted at times in Nairobi, Lagos, Abuja, Lusaka and Harare, Indonesia was declared "sold out" nationwide until July 2025, and since February 2026 residential users in the most congested parts of Lagos and Abuja can get service only on a business priority tier ([Ookla, 15 Jun 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/starlink-ssa-q1-2026); [Antara, 22 Jul 2025](https://www.antaranews.com/berita/4983973/kemkomdigi-sebut-starlink-mulai-buka-layanan-aktivasi-pelanggan-baru); [Space in Africa, 26 Aug 2026](https://spaceinafrica.com/2026/08/26/starlink-narrows-the-gap-to-under-13000-as-nigerias-isp-market-fragments-further-in-q2-2026/)). In the US, the reference market, the national median reached 127.39 Mbps in the first quarter of 2026 and 44.7% of users met the FCC's 100/20 Mbps benchmark in late 2025, but the states with the most subscribers lag (California 42.8%, Texas 31.4%, Florida 30.9% in the first half of 2026), which Ookla links to "greater capacity demands and potential congestion" ([Ookla, 5 May 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/starlink-hits-new-us-highs); [Broadband Breakfast, 15 Sep 2026](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/ookla-starlink-performance-surged-in-first-half-of-2026/)).

Relief is coming, but slowly and not for dense cities. SpaceX says its V3 satellites are "designed to offer one Tbps of downlink capacity per satellite", deploying on Starship from the second half of 2026, and that "as network capacity increases... we expect to compete increasingly in suburban and urban environments" ([SpaceX S-1, 20 May 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026036936/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm)); Ookla notes V3 needs new user terminals, so the effect "won't happen overnight" ([Ookla, 5 Aug 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/jamaica-starlink-data-speeds-drop-after-hurricane-melissa)). MoffettNathanson's Craig Moffett argues that the binding limit is the capacity each spot-beam cell can carry, not the number of satellites, that expectations of disrupting suburban and urban broadband are "unreasonably high", and that V3 takes about five years to roll out ([Light Reading, 13 Jul 2026](https://www.lightreading.com/satellite/even-with-bigger-birds-starlink-still-best-suited-to-low-density-markets-study)). Network capacity "recently passed 600 Tbps" after more than 270 Tbps was added in 2025, while subscribers doubled from 4.4 million to 8.9 million that year, so capacity per subscriber fell slightly (this report's arithmetic on [Ookla, 4 Feb 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/2025-global-satellite-broadband-performance-report), [Starlink Progress Report 2025](https://starlink.com/public-files/starlinkProgressReport_2025.pdf) and the S-1).

New markets flatter: Bangladesh posted 88.95 Mbps and 35 ms in its second operating quarter, with a few thousand users ([Ookla, 12 Apr 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/low-orbit-high-stakes-starlinks-asia-pacific-performance-and-path-to-market)). Latency depends on local ground stations rather than load: Kenya's fell from 289 ms to 53 ms after a Nairobi point of presence opened, and to 39 ms by early 2026, while markets with local gateways record 35–36 ms and those without reach 118–157 ms ([Ookla, 4 Feb 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/2025-global-satellite-broadband-performance-report); [Ookla, 15 Jun 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/starlink-ssa-q1-2026); [Ookla, 12 Apr 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/low-orbit-high-stakes-starlinks-asia-pacific-performance-and-path-to-market)). India's benchmark is demanding: in August 2026 the median Indian fixed line delivered 63.94 Mbps at 7 ms and the median mobile connection 128.20 Mbps at 26 ms, about the same download as Starlink's US median, though periods and samples differ ([Ookla Speedtest Global Index, Aug 2026](https://www.speedtest.net/global-index/india)). This report's reading: early Indian speed tests would look strong, and the test that matters comes after a few quarters of sign-ups in dense cells.

## Starlink discounts the dish, rarely the monthly bill

SpaceX is trading revenue per user for volume. Starlink's ARPU fell from US$99 a month in 2023 to US$91, US$81 and **US$66 in early 2026** (about ₹6,275 at today's rate, roughly 29 times Jio's ARPU), and the company says it expects ARPU "to continue to decline over the next few years as the portion of our subscriber base outside North America continues to grow, as we add lower priced service plans, and as we adjust the monthly service plan fees" ([SpaceX S-1, 20 May 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026036936/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm)). Yet the cuts land mostly on hardware and on slower, deprioritised tiers: Kenya's rental, instalments and Mini kit, Indonesia's 40% launch discount on kits, and a US$39 US promotion with a free rental kit in March 2026 ([Business Daily, 15 Jan 2026](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/technology/starlink-cuts-upfront-cost-with-instalment-payments-plan-5327602); [Developing Telecoms, 31 May 2024](https://developingtelecoms.com/telecom-technology/satellite-communications-networks/16786-starlink-s-indonesia-pricing-is-not-predatory-antimonopoly-agency.html); [Notebookcheck, 4 Mar 2026](https://www.notebookcheck.net/SpaceX-cuts-Starlink-residential-plan-price-to-39-month-ahead-of-IPO.1242274.0.html)). Where cells fill, Starlink charges more: Indonesia added "demand surge fees" of IDR 8–9.4 million (about US$490–574) in high-demand areas, Reddit-sourced US reports describe one-time congestion charges of up to US$1,500 in Alaska, and Ookla's Kerry Baker suggests Jamaica's June 2026 rise may have been meant "to encourage customers to leave in order to regain capacity" ([Ookla, 12 Apr 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/low-orbit-high-stakes-starlinks-asia-pacific-performance-and-path-to-market); [SatelliteInternet.com, 27 Jan 2026](https://www.satelliteinternet.com/resources/starlink-congestion-surcharge/); [Ookla, 5 Aug 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/jamaica-starlink-data-speeds-drop-after-hurricane-melissa)). Ookla's Johan puts it plainly: "Starlink's pricing responds to demand and capacity conditions in ways that fixed-line operators typically do not" ([Ookla, 12 Apr 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/low-orbit-high-stakes-starlinks-asia-pacific-performance-and-path-to-market)). No market in the research shows a sustained cut of the monthly price towards local mobile-data levels; the only Starlink product sold at mobile price points is satellite-to-phone, and telcos sell it ([GMA News, 8 Jul 2026](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/money/companies/994149/globe-sets-p99-entry-price-for-starlink-satellite-to-mobile-service/story/)).

India's benchmarks are the hardest Starlink has faced. Indian wireless data earned operators ₹7.51 a GB in the quarter to March 2026, down every quarter from ₹9.11 a year earlier, and industry wireless ARPU was ₹196.04 ([TRAI, Jun 2026](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-06/QPIR_22062026.pdf)); the RHP compares India's ₹7.5 a GB with ₹228.4 in the US and ₹33.4 in China ([Jio RHP, pp. 160–161](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)). IndMoney's widely repeated "₹7.74 a GB" for Jio does not follow from its own ₹214 and 42.3 GB, which divide to about ₹5.1, so TRAI's figure is the cleaner benchmark ([IndMoney, updated 13 Oct 2026](https://www.indmoney.com/blog/ipo/jio-ipo-starlink-threat)). Jio's cheapest 28-day plan with daily data costs ₹299 for 1.5 GB a day ([Jio plan feed, retrieved 18 Oct 2026](https://www.jio.com/api/jio-mdmdata-service/mdmdata/recharge/plans?productType=MOBILITY&billingType=1)). Even Starlink's cheapest plan in the research, Kenya's 50 GB tier at KES 1,300, works out to about ₹19 a GB at today's rate, roughly two and a half times India's figure (this report's arithmetic on [tech.africa, Jun 2026](https://tech.africa/starlink-africa/)). Business Today puts Starlink's Bangladesh plan at ₹3,295 a month plus ₹20,800 of hardware, about ₹60,000 in the first year, against Airtel's ₹1,599 for 300 Mbps and BSNL's ₹899 for 200 Mbps; its hardware figure is well below the Tk 47,000 standard kit reported at Bangladesh's launch, about ₹36,305 at today's rate ([Business Today, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/starlink-vs-jio-vs-airtel-vs-bsnl-why-indias-broadband-economics-could-be-hard-to-crack-560889-2026-10-12)). Fintech investor Sheel Mohnot contrasts 60 GB of mobile data for about US$4 (₹350, as Mint converts it) in an Indian village with US$27 for Starlink in Rwanda; Aaron Burnett of mach33 counters that home fibre users consume about 300 GB a month against 30–40 GB on mobile, figures Mint says it could not independently verify ([Livemint, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.livemint.com/technology/starlink-vs-jio-can-elon-musk-make-satellite-internet-affordable-in-india-s-cheap-data-market-11791776817938.html)). Reuters calls cheap data "the playbook that built Jio in India, and the one Musk has used in markets like Kenya to unsettle local telecom players" ([Al Jazeera (Reuters), 11 Oct 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/10/11/musk-vows-affordable-starlink-in-india-as-he-escalates-feud-with-ambani)); in Kenya that playbook ended at a 1.0% share. This report's reading: the likelihood of a mass monthly price war is low; the moves to expect are kit financing, introductory offers and shared village or apartment connections, sold partly through Jio's and Airtel's own stores.

## Satellite-to-phone service reaches Indians only through a telco

### What Starlink Mobile is and how it performs

"Starlink Mobile" is SpaceX's new name for direct-to-cell, "separate from the broadband capability" ([Via Satellite, 5 Aug 2026](https://www.satellitetoday.com/finance/2026/08/05/spacex-outlines-starlink-mobile-plans-in-first-earnings-report/)). At 31 March 2026 about 650 first-generation satellites served about **7.4 million monthly unique devices in about 30 countries through about 30 mobile operators**, offering "light data, text messaging (SMS), and over-the-top voice services (e.g., WhatsApp and FaceTime)" outdoors where the sky is visible ([SpaceX S-1, 20 May 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026036936/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm)). Shotwell says today's system "uses about 5 megahertz of bandwidth, and we operate that through local telco providers" ([Motley Fool transcript, 11 Aug 2026](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/11/spacex-spcx-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)). The business model is wholesale: "We charge MNOs either a fixed fee or a per-mobile user fee-based amount, which is typically passed through to the customer via the carrier as an 'add-on' feature" ([SpaceX S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026036936/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm)). No market has a standalone Starlink phone plan, and US carriers have refused to host a satellite reseller; Verizon's Dan Schulman says there is "no backdoor to our MVNOs in any structure" ([Via Satellite, 24 Jul 2026](https://www.satellitetoday.com/connectivity/2026/07/24/telco-ceos-shut-down-the-idea-of-satellite-mvno-say-leo-isnt-a-threat-to-broadband/)). Carriers price it as a cheap add-on or a free retention feature: T-Mobile charges US$10 a month (about ₹951, 4.4 times Jio's ARPU) or includes it in top plans, O2 charges £3, Globe charges PHP 99 (about ₹149), Japan's three operators give messaging free, Airtel Africa offers a free 30-day trial in the DRC, and Bangladesh's Banglalink announced South Asia's first Starlink Mobile service on 9 October 2026 ([T-Mobile, accessed 18 Oct 2026](https://www.t-mobile.com/coverage/satellite-phone-service); [ISPreview, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.ispreview.co.uk/index.php/2026/10/opensignal-map-shows-where-uk-mobiles-connect-to-starlinks-d2d-satellites.html); [Ookla via Communications Today, 29 Jul 2026](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/?p=417644); [Connecting Africa, 18 Aug 2026](https://www.connectingafrica.com/connectivity/airtel-africa-starlink-launch-satellite-to-mobile-service-in-drc); [Via Satellite, 9 Oct 2026](https://www.satellitetoday.com/connectivity/2026/10/09/veons-banglalink-to-launch-starlink-mobile-in-bangladesh/)). Free adds up fast: NTT docomo counted 5 million connected users within ten weeks, about 5% of its subscribers ([Ookla via Communications Today, 29 Jul 2026](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/?p=417644)).

It is a narrow pipe. A crowdsourced measurement study by Universidad Politécnica de Madrid authors estimates the data service at "around 3 Mbps per beam in outdoor conditions", possibly rising to 18 Mbps depending on "regulatory and business decisions" ([arXiv 2506.00283, v8, 23 Nov 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00283)); one user test off Hawaii measured 1.7 Mbps down, about 200 kbps up and more than 200 ms of latency ([BGR, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.bgr.com/2283260/starlink-direct-to-cell-phone-network-explained/)). Coverage thins towards the equator: only 15.5% of Philippine signal scans reached −118 dBm or better, about half the share in Australia, Japan and New Zealand, because the satellites' inclined orbits concentrate coverage at mid-latitudes ([Ookla via Communications Today, 29 Jul 2026](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/?p=417644)). Demand has disappointed: T-Mobile chief executive Srini Gopalan says "most of the usage we're seeing is in national parks... we're seeing a lot less usage than we were originally thinking" ([Light Reading, 29 Apr 2026](https://lightreading.com/satellite/starlink-d2d-use-is-less-than-expected-says-t-mobile)). By this report's arithmetic, a 3 Mbps beam running flat out for a month carries about 972 GB, roughly what 22 average Jio customers use at 43.7 GB each.

### In the US SpaceX is buying spectrum to become a carrier; in India it owns none

In the US, SpaceX is assembling its own airwaves: about US$19.6 billion for EchoStar spectrum in total (the often-quoted US$17 billion was the September 2025 initial figure), an 800 MHz low-band deal with Grain Management announced on 8 October 2026 at an undisclosed price that financial media put near US$8 billion, and FCC approval on 6 October for 15,000 direct-to-device satellites ([SpaceX S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026036936/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm); [Via Satellite, 8 Oct 2026](https://www.satellitetoday.com/connectivity/2026/10/08/spacex-moves-to-acquire-nationwide-low-band-spectrum-for-starlink-mobile/); [SatNews, 11 Oct 2026](https://satnews.com/2026/10/11/spacex-acquires-grain-managements-800-mhz-spectrum-portfolio-to-launch-standalone-starlink-mobile-carrier/); [Via Satellite, 7 Oct 2026](https://www.satellitetoday.com/connectivity/2026/10/07/fcc-grants-spacex-approval-for-15000-direct-to-device-satellites-in-mss-spectrum/)). SpaceX says the 800 MHz deal will "pave the way for @Starlink to become a major mobile carrier in the US", and Shotwell expects to "acquire quite a few" of AT&T's, Verizon's and T-Mobile's customers, with satellites flying "next year" and service from "end of next year" ([SpaceX on X, 8 Oct 2026](https://x.com/SpaceX/status/2108291133025698301); [Motley Fool transcript, 11 Aug 2026](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/11/spacex-spcx-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)). US carrier shares fell about 10–13% in a day on the news (AT&T −10.8%, Verizon −10.1%, T-Mobile −13.3%, per MarketBeat; other outlets reported slightly different falls, UNVERIFIED), while Raymond James called the threat "3+ years away" and MoffettNathanson said SpaceX would still need terrestrial infrastructure costing "tens of billions of dollars" ([MarketBeat, c. 16 Oct 2026](https://www.marketbeat.com/articles/spacexs-spectrum-deal-hits-telecom-stocks-but-gives-tower-reits-a-new-opportunity/)). None of that spectrum works in India. SpaceX's own filing says current phones do not support its satellite versions of these bands, and that abroad it "will be unable to offer 5G-like direct-to-consumer service on our own international spectrum" until handsets change, relying instead on "MNO spectrum partnerships on a market-by-market basis" ([SpaceX S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026036936/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm)).

### India's rules route any phone service through the telcos

Satellite-to-phone service is "not permitted in India due to the absence of a regulatory framework" ([TelecomTalk, 14 Nov 2025](https://telecomtalk.info/dot-trai-direct-to-device-satellite-service/1001639/)), and the satcom spectrum now being priced "covers fixed data and internet services", leaving satellite-to-mobile outside it ([Broadcast and CableSat, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.broadcastandcablesat.co.in/satcom-spectrum-pricing-heads-to-cabinet-as-dot-finalizes-its-note/)). TRAI's April 2026 consultation asks whether direct-to-device service should use satellite (MSS) or terrestrial mobile (IMT) spectrum and proposes that satellite networks sell capacity to service providers "rather than directly to end users"; TRAI said the consultation was complete in July, and no final recommendation was found by 18 October ([MediaNama, 9 Apr 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/04/223-trai-satellite-communication-framework-2026/); [ANI, 22 Jul 2026](https://aninews.in/news/business/trai-expects-satellite-communication-framework-to-provide-regulatory-certainty-boost-d2d-deployment-chairman20260722170448/)). IN-SPACe has authorised only Starlink's broadband Gen1 constellation; it rejected the phone-capable Gen2 filing, which MediaNama dates to January 2026, and Starlink refiled for nearly 30,000 satellites in August 2026 ([MediaNama, 20 Aug 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/08/223-starlink-india-satellite-network/); [MediaNama, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-rss-affiliate-wants-india-to-block-starlink/)). Starlink's own filing to TRAI asks that service over mobile spectrum "be permitted at this stage" with "open-access obligations that require SCN authorised entities to offer their service to all suitable carriers... rather than one MNO using it exclusively", which describes a wholesale layer under Indian telcos, not a rival SIM ([Starlink India submission to TRAI, 27 May 2026](https://trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-05/Starlink_27052026.pdf)). Airtel's Rahul Vatts drew the battle line at IMC: "If the satellite intends to use the similar spectrum that is being used by mobile players, then naturally every condition which we have been through with regard to pricing, licensing conditions, security – they have to be similar for anybody", citing the 2012 Supreme Court ruling that such spectrum must be auctioned ([Communications Today (PTI), 10 Oct 2026](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/airtel-calls-for-same-rules-for-satcom-and-mobile-operators/)). Telcos want equal obligations "since D2D sells straight to consumers and threatens their core business" ([MediaNama, 20 Aug 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/08/223-starlink-india-satellite-network/)).

Indian rivals are lining up their own versions: Vi with AST SpaceMobile, whose first next-generation satellite launched from ISRO's Satish Dhawan Space Centre and whose commercial service has slipped to 2027; BSNL with Viasat for emergency messaging, whose commercial status is unclear and conflicts with the "not permitted" reporting; Tata's Nelco, targeting early 2028 through its stake in Elveo; and Jio, whose 1,600-satellite plan includes "direct-to-device calling for rural areas" although its RHP never mentions the service ([AST SpaceMobile, accessed 18 Oct 2026](https://ast-science.com/our-journey/); [Ookla via Communications Today, 29 Jul 2026](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/?p=417644); [Telecom Review Asia, 15 Jul 2026](https://www.telecomreviewasia.com/news/technology-news/29744-bsnl-rolls-out-satellite-handset-to-strengthen-indias-communication-resilience/); [Outlook Business, 19 Aug 2026](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/tata-backed-nelco-targets-2028-for-direct-to-device-services-india-among-first-markets); [TelecomTalk, 8 Oct 2026](https://telecomtalk.info/imc2026-jio-plans-satellite-network-connect-600000villages/1012635/)).

The economics confine the service to coverage gaps. SpaceX's market model assumes "a weighted average monthly mobile ARPU of $8 per user... $18 in high-income markets, $5 in upper-middle income markets, $2 in lower-middle, and $2 in low income markets" ([SpaceX S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026036936/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm)), and the World Bank classifies India as lower-middle income ([World Bank, accessed 18 Oct 2026](https://datahelpdesk.worldbank.org/knowledgebase/articles/906519-world-bank-country-and-lending-groups)); US$2 is about ₹190, below Jio's ₹215.6. Even the industry forecast in Jio's RHP has mobile broadband ARPU reaching only "₹326.4 (US$3.6) per month by FY2031" ([Jio RHP, p. 215](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)). Coverage leaves little room: Jio's wireless broadband reaches more than 99% of the population ([Jio RHP, p. 128](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)), and Hughes India chief Shivaji Chatterjee calls direct-to-device "the only solution" for the roughly 25% of India's area, not population, that mobile does not cover ([Voice&Data, 21 Sep 2026](https://www.voicendata.com/space/d2d-and-5g-india-moves-towards-a-converged-connectivity-layer-12260485)). Analysys Mason, as cited by Ookla, projects roughly a 1% revenue uplift for operators that add satellite messaging, with the larger value in reduced churn ([Ookla via Communications Today, 29 Jul 2026](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/?p=417644)). A July 2025 JM Financial expert discussion judged direct-to-cell unlikely to disrupt the mobile businesses that make up "80-90% of their valuations" ([Business Today, 16 Jul 2025](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/markets/stocks/story/starlinks-satellite-internet-launch-in-india-potential-impact-on-airtel-and-jio-484844-2025-07-16)), GlobalData's Pradeepthi Kantipudi says satellite connectivity "is not expected to significantly alter the trajectory of India's SIM-based 4G and 5G market" ([Telecom Review Asia, 15 Jul 2026](https://www.telecomreviewasia.com/news/technology-news/29744-bsnl-rolls-out-satellite-handset-to-strengthen-indias-communication-resilience/)), and Starlink's Dreyer says "Starlink isn't here to replace India's terrestrial networks. It's here to extend them" ([TelecomTalk, 8 Oct 2026](https://telecomtalk.info/imc2026-starlink-setup-over20-gateway-sites-india/1012624/)). This report's reading: through 2028, the realistic Starlink Mobile outcome in India is a dead-zone and emergency add-on that Jio, Airtel or Vi resell if the rules allow; the long-run risk to Jio's core is a contest over spectrum and customer ownership in which Indian telcos hold the spectrum.

## Conclusion

The public fight and the real contest are about different products and different gatekeepers. Musk aimed at Ambani over dish broadband, the part of Jio's business with the least revenue and the most growth, but the decision Starlink needs belongs to the Home Ministry and the Cabinet, and the product that could one day reach Jio's core, satellite-to-phone service, depends on spectrum that Jio, Airtel and Vi already hold, under rules TRAI has not yet written. That shifts the decisive moment away from the Musk–Ambani exchange to TRAI's direct-to-device recommendation and whether terrestrial mobile spectrum can be shared with satellite operators. Airtel's position previews the incumbents' line: in India it demands auction-level conditions for satellite on mobile spectrum, while its African arm sells Starlink Mobile, so the objection is to a satellite company owning the customer, not to satellites. For Jio's IPO, satellite is a risk to the growth narrative, in which Jio took 61.73% of India's fixed-broadband additions in the June quarter ([Jio RHP, pp. 169–170](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)), rather than to the revenue base; the fact that Jio's lawyers gave satellite its own risk title and new paragraphs between June and October shows the company now treats it as more material, whatever caused the change.

Several things would change this picture, and none had happened by 18 October 2026: Cabinet approval of the 5% charge ([Communications Today, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/satcom-spectrum-pricing-heads-to-cabinet-as-dot-finalizes-its-note/)) and the first Home Ministry clearance, including whether OneWeb gets it "a few days before" Starlink, as Mittal hopes ([Outlook India, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.outlookindia.com/national/i-applied-five-years-back-sunil-mittal-takes-a-dig-at-starlink-says-oneweb-still-awaits-govts-nod)); Starlink's first official Indian monthly and kit prices, and whether it sells through Jio's and Airtel's stores or direct; IN-SPACe's ruling on Starlink's Gen2 constellation; Ookla's first Indian Starlink readings after a few quarters of sign-ups in dense cells; Jio's next tariff move and its September-quarter home-broadband additions; and any funding line for Jio's 1,600 satellites. Four questions the record cannot yet answer should stay open in any telling of the story: what Musk intended, whether the reported June freeze was specific to Starlink, whether the "20 lakh" figure is any kind of limit, and how much Jio actually earns from home broadband.
