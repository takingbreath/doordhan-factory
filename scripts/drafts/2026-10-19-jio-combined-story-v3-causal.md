## English dialogue

Musk is publicly fighting Ambani just as Jio prepares to go public. For an investor, that raises a useful question: how much of Jio's future can Starlink actually threaten?

Today, Starlink's dish-based broadband has high upfront costs and shared capacity. It isn't a substitute for Jio's mass-market mobile network.

Yet Jio's prospectus warns about satellite competition. What could change?

The clue is where Jio's next earnings growth could come from.

With more than 53 crore customers, even a small ARPU increase matters. But raising recharge prices doesn't guarantee the full increase reaches revenue: customers can downgrade.

Home broadband offers another route to growth. Jio's customer base there grew 55% in FY26.

AirFiber lets it reach homes where laying fibre is difficult. Much of India's household market is still unconnected.

And some of those hard-to-connect homes are also where satellites make sense.

If Starlink becomes cheaper and adds sufficient capacity, Jio could face more competition as it expands. Winning those connections might require better offers or more spending.

That's how Musk could affect Jio's future without taking away its existing mobile customers: by making the next phase of broadband growth less profitable.

## Narrative checks

1. Opening question: the investor's exposure to Starlink. It remains the governing question throughout.
2. Establish the present mismatch: current dish broadband and mass-market mobile are different services. This is a partial answer that gives the next question meaning, not the final payoff.
3. Complication: the issuer still identifies a conditional satellite risk. Risk disclosure is not evidence of secret fear, an imminent price war or the magnitude of a threat.
4. Motivation: future earnings growth explains why monetisation and home broadband enter this story. A raw Airtel comparison is no longer dropped into the second line.
5. Opportunity: ARPU improvements at scale and more connected homes. Home broadband is another growth route, not a historical decision asserted to have been caused by stagnating mobile ARPU.
6. Obstacle: future cost-effective satellite capacity could contest part of that same household market. The condition precedes the adverse scenario.
7. Payoff answers the opening: future broadband growth economics, without requiring loss of the existing mobile customer base. No cliffhanger, hidden separate answer or unsupported valuation number.

Approximately 200 spoken words with numbers expanded; allow roughly 73–80 seconds with pauses. Not audio-measured. Runtime is subordinate to restoring the causal structure in this revision. The ₹48/₹31,000 crore illustration remains available in earlier drafts; here it would create an additional loop and qualification burden. It has been cut as an editorial choice, not contradicted.

- [Pixar/Khan Academy, Activity 1: Beats](https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/pixar/storytelling/story-structure/a/activity-1-struc): read the published activity and story-spine sequence. The useful transfer is causal progression: a development produces a consequence that produces the next development. Do not force an animated-feature plot or fictional character arc into a factual business explainer.
- [YouTube's own audience-retention guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en): read the official guidance on matching the opening to audience expectations, moving compelling material earlier and diagnosing losses. This does not support withholding all substantive information until the last line or guarantee better retention.
- Our working application: keep one specific question active, supply evidence/partial answers continuously, establish relevance before numbers, and make the final inference resolve the opening. These remain writing hypotheses to evaluate with actual audience data.
