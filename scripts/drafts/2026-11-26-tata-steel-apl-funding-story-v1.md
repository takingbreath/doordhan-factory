## English story for approval

Under the announced structure, APL Apollo would pay Tata Steel ₹28,000 crore for its tubes and coated-steel businesses, with borrowing in the initial funding plan.

Tata Steel would also buy APL Apollo shares from existing shareholders. Those payments go to the sellers.

If Tata gains control, any acquisition debt still at APL Apollo enters Tata Steel's consolidated accounts too.

Management plans to replace debt with longer-term capital, and the structure is now under review.

So Tata receiving sale proceeds doesn't mean the group's debt disappears. Follow both legs—and who funds them.

## Progression and scope

Seller receives cash → buyer borrows → seller also acquires shares of buyer → distinguish share-seller proceeds from funding injected into the company → conditional consolidated debt → recapitalisation/restructuring qualification → evaluate the whole financing rather than one receipt. The accounting consequence is our inference from control-based consolidation, not a claim to have read a final post-deal balance sheet.

Do not assert ₹28,000 crore is all permanent debt, that net group debt increases by exactly that amount, or that all transactions have closed. Control and outstanding borrowing are conditions. Initial borrowing is intended to be replaced with longer-term capital; that plan and the reported review must survive into the eventual script. Neither a recapitalisation price nor its eventual instrument/mix is fixed here.

Consolidated reporting does not itself make the parent the legal borrower, guarantor or liable for every subsidiary obligation. A liability to an external lender is different from an internal group balance that is eliminated. No allegation of a sham, fraud, hidden debt or an illegal cash loop. No verdict on valuation, minority treatment or whether to tender/buy/sell. Do not infer a cause for every share-price move.
