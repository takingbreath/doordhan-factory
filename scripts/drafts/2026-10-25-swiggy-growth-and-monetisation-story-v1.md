## English story for approval

Swiggy's food business delivered record adjusted EBITDA of ₹341 crore. Instamart lost ₹895 crore on the same measure.

So how does Instamart stop overwhelming that progress?

Swiggy's own break-even plan has two conditions: roughly 2.3 times today's scale, and ₹30 contribution per order.

That second condition changes the story. Contribution is what an order leaves after variable costs to cover the remaining business costs. Reaching the scale target alone does not fulfil this plan.

Management expects most of the improvement from earning more per order through product mix, own brands and advertising, with the rest from cost efficiencies.

The business question becomes: can Instamart monetise customers better while competing hard to keep them? Faster delivery alone doesn't settle that.

## Story movement and payoff

Record food performance → larger grocery loss → how that gap closes → two conditions in management's plan → the revenue-per-order condition reveals the commercial task. The viewer gets a business lens to apply to future results: assess scale alongside monetisation and costs. Do not imply grocery profitability is guaranteed, that growth never improves economics, or that customers must personally pay the entire increase through higher fees.

The food result/Instamart comparison is not a trace of cash transfers between segments and does not reconstruct consolidated net profit. The final question is an analytical interpretation, not an explanation of every seller's motive on Friday.
