## Proposed hook

Anthropic’s IPO has a “God share” you can’t buy.

## English story

Anthropic, the company behind Claude, has filed for an IPO. Public investors will be offered Class A shares, with one vote per share.

But the founders are reserving something much more powerful: a single Class F share carrying 50.1% of the voting power on matters put to shareholders generally. All the ordinary shareholder votes combined cannot outweigh it on those questions. That is what the reference video calls the “God share.”

Who gets it? An entity called Anthropic Founders, LLC, whose initial members will be Dario Amodei, Daniela Amodei and their five fellow co-founders. This one share has no economic rights. Its purpose is voting power, and issuing more ordinary shares will not dilute that power.

Anthropic presents its governance structure as a way to protect its mission: developing AI safely while balancing financial interests with public benefit. It wants access to public capital while preserving that mission.

There is another layer, though. A separate Long-Term Benefit Trust generally gets to appoint and remove a majority of the board through a different share, Class T. So the founders’ “God share” does not give them unrestricted command over the whole company. The founders and the trust together can elect the entire board, leaving public investors with little practical influence over its composition.

The IPO therefore opens a financial stake in Anthropic to the public while keeping decisive governance power with the founders and the trust. Before buying a share, it matters to understand exactly which rights come with it.
