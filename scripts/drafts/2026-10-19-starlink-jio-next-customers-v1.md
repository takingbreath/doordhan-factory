## English script

Musk could hurt Jio without touching your mobile recharge.

Starlink's dish supplies home internet. Its separate phone service has no confirmed Indian launch.

And satellite capacity is shared: covering an area doesn't mean connecting everyone in it.

But remote homes are where the businesses could overlap.

Jio's home-broadband customer base grew fifty-five percent last financial year. If Starlink becomes affordable, it could compete for some of those same homes.

Jio may need better offers or more investment to win them.

The fight could be over how much Jio earns from tomorrow's customers.

## Sources and claim boundaries

- Hook payoff: competition for future home-broadband growth can affect acquisition economics even without mass mobile substitution. This is conditional analysis, not a forecast of a price war or a claim Starlink currently operates in India.
- [Jio RHP](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf), downloaded from official Jio CDN and read locally on 19 October: printed p.215 reports fixed-broadband customers grew 55.19% in FY26. Printed p.35 names JioFiber/JioAirFiber, rural and enterprise/government connectivity as potentially substitutable, especially in coverage gaps; it warns of greater investment/cost if satellite solutions become more competitive.
- [SpaceX technical account](https://starlink.com/public-files/DIRECT_TO_CELL_FIRST_TEXT_UPDATE.pdf) distinguishes the direct handset network from terminal broadband. [T-Mobile live app service](https://www.t-mobile.com/news/network/t-satellite-data-ready-app-expansion) shows mobile is a real separate service abroad; do not imply Starlink has no mobile ambitions.
- [Starlink fair-use documentation](https://starlink.com/legal/documents/DOC-1469-65206-75), freshly indexed text checked on 19 October, calls the network a finite resource that grows with added satellites. [Official capacity FAQ](https://starlink.com/en-bq/support/article/240ac933-68ce-00dd-d8ec-0d5bf5816f3d), fresh search result, says residential activations are not possible where the service region is at capacity. A geographic footprint is not unlimited simultaneous customer capacity; capacity can grow over time.
- [17 October launch update](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/satcom-row-starlink-says-india-infra-has-govt-approvals-ready-for-launch-126101700036_1.html), freshly opened, and a fresh launch-status search did not establish a completed Indian commercial launch or approved Direct-to-Cell launch. No unannounced Indian tariff is spoken.
- Avoid treating home-broadband customer growth as revenue growth, all villages as commercially viable, or global phone-service announcements as Indian authorization.
