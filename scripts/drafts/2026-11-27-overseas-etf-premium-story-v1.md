## English story for approval

Take a hypothetical Indian-listed overseas ETF. Its holdings are worth ₹100 per unit, but you pay ₹125.

That's a 25% premium.

Overseas investment limits can restrict fresh unit creation, allowing the traded price to pull away from NAV.

Now suppose the holdings' rupee value stays at ₹100, but the premium disappears and the ETF price falls to ₹100.

You've lost 20%, even though the holdings haven't fallen.

The overseas stocks were one part of your bet. The premium you paid was another.

## Progression and factual boundaries

Premium purchase → why extra units may not close the gap → hold the underlying rupee value constant → premium unwinds → return calculated on the investor's actual cost. This fulfils the hook, rather than claiming to know today's premium or when it will shrink. The 25% premium and 20% price loss have different denominators.

Hold the rupee NAV constant in the actual words. The hook is a hypothetical shorthand, not a universal rule about a flat Nasdaq. Do not imply premium disappearance is inevitable or that every overseas ETF trades above NAV.
