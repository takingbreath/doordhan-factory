## Verified consequential claims

- Company behind Claude has filed for a proposed IPO, not already completed it. Source 1 and S-1.
- Public offer is Class A: one vote per share. S-1 summary and governance discussion. Class N strategic holdings generally have no votes and can convert on qualifying transfers; avoid treating every existing security as Class A.
- Immediately before offering completion, a single Class F share is to be issued to Founder LLC. Initial sole members: Dario Amodei, Daniela Amodei, Tom Brown, Jack Clark, Jared Kaplan, Sam McCandlish and Christopher Olah. S-1 Founder Voting Structure. Formal entity name Anthropic Founders, LLC confirmed in charter Article V 5.1(q).
- Class F receives 50.1% of total voting power on matters submitted to stockholders generally, including elections other than Class T directors. No economic rights. S-1 summary and governance. This is not 50.1% equity ownership, profit entitlement or authority over every board decision.
- Additional issuance of Class A or other capital shares does not dilute the special voting power. S-1 governance-risk discussion. Ordinary shareholder votes alone cannot defeat Class F on a general vote controlled by this majority; class-specific consents and separate powers remain.
- LTBT generally elects/removes a majority of authorized directors through Class T. Class T does not otherwise vote on general matters and has no economic rights. Founder LLC and LTBT collectively can elect all directors. Board manages business and affairs; trust itself does not set daily strategy. S-1 and charter Article VII.
- PBC purpose and special governance are described as protecting the company's AI mission and balancing stockholder financial interests, affected parties and public benefit. Attribute rationale to the company; no claim it guarantees safety. S-1 governance and charter Article III.
- Sunset/redemption and transfer conditions exist. One authorized Class F share is a charter provision, not a mathematical impossibility of ever amending the structure. Charter Articles IV and V. Avoid permanent-control claims.
