## Interpretation versus established facts

- The explanatory bridge is ours: an unauthorized change in holdings can expose an investor to loss without a cash withdrawal. It follows from the reported swap and subsequent decline; it does not establish the attacker's profit or intention.
- The closing distinguishes successful authentication from consent to an order. It is an editorial principle, not a finding about which party failed a legal duty.
- “Barbaad” describes the reported harm colloquially. Do not accompany it with zero-balance graphics or imply that every account was wiped out. If that implication persists in delivery, change the hook to “Paise withdraw nahi hue. Phir portfolio mein loss kaise hua?” before final approval.

## Exclusions and checks still needed

- No confirmed aggregate-loss number is used. Complaint counts and company-identified affected accounts have different bases.
- Do not infer issuer complicity, a proven pump-and-dump, the attacker's identity or who received any trading benefit.
- Do not assert phishing or a prior data breach as the established cause, blame customers, or describe the company's account as a final forensic verdict.
- No compensation promise, regulatory-jurisdiction conclusion, specific platform recommendation or buy/sell instruction.
- Do not say the full sale amount exactly equals purchase cost; fees and residual cash complicate that comparison. This story gives neither transaction dollar total nor an exact reconciliation.
