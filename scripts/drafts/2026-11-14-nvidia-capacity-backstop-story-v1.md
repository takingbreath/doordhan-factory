## English story for approval

Nvidia sells chips to AI cloud companies that rent out computing power.

But with some partners, Nvidia has also promised to buy some of the capacity they cannot sell.

At 26 July, those commitments totalled $36 billion, typically lasting six years.

Why take that risk? The promise helps lenders finance more data centres—which can mean more Nvidia hardware sales. Nvidia can also earn a share of rental revenue.

If AI demand disappoints, though, Nvidia may have to pay for capacity it cannot fully use or resell.

That's the deal Huang's successor inherits: helping customers expand means taking on some of their demand risk. A chip sale can leave Nvidia tied to the buyer's business for years.

## Factual boundaries

- **$36 billion is a dated commitment total, not a loss, loan, cash already paid or bill due now.** It is not today's verified outstanding balance. Retain “at 26 July” in words and “26 July 2026” in visuals/source notes.
- “Some partners” is essential. This is not every Nvidia customer or every hardware sale. Nvidia receives computing services and can receive rental revenue; it is not simply giving the purchase price back.
- Buying capacity the clouds cannot sell is directly described in the filing's risk section. The risk of being unable to use/resell all committed capacity is conditional, not a claim that this has happened across the portfolio.
- Do not merge these cloud-service commitments with separate data-centre leases, lease-default guarantees, the SB Energy arrangement or equity investments. They have different terms and triggers.
- The commitments reduce as third parties or Nvidia's R&D consume capacity. Do not say the entire maximum must inevitably be spent or lost.
- No claim that Huang's departure caused these commitments, cancels them, proves customer demand is fake, or explains a particular share-price move. No allegations about private life or espionage enter the narrative.
