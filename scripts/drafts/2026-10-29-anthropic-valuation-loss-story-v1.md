## English story for approval

Anthropic reported an $80.7 billion loss for the first half of 2026. But that number doesn't tell you how much cash it burned.

To understand the loss, look at how it raised money. Some of its financing, including convertible notes, sits on its books as liabilities measured at fair value.

As Anthropic's enterprise valuation rose, those financing claims became worth more. The increase was recorded as an expense—even though that charge didn't involve an equivalent cash payment.

That revaluation accounted for $72.4 billion of the loss. Investors valuing the business more highly had made its reported loss bigger.

Does that mean the business was profitable? No. It still reported a $693 million operating loss over the same six months.

The useful question is what created the loss: running the business, or revaluing the claims on it? A bigger loss doesn't always mean a worse business.
