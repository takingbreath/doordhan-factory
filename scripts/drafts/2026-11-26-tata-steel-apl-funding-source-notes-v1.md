## Primary transaction records

Read the transaction framework, funding note and payment diagram. It gives the ₹28,000-crore business transfer consideration, share purchases from the promoter/public holders, initial acquisition financing and subsequent replacement with longer-term capital. The diagram distinguishes asset proceeds from promoter/open-offer payments. It also makes regulatory approvals/completion conditional. Long-term growth/debt-free aspirations are not accomplished results.

Read the initial approval and annexures for buying Tata Steel Colors and the tubes undertaking, subject to conditions and shareholder/regulatory approvals. This corroborates the buyer and the two businesses; it is not proof of completed payment.

[Tata Steel, 23 November board outcome SEC/1504](https://www.tatasteel.com/media/26679/ts-bsense-bm.pdf), especially PDF pages 1 and 5–6. Read the share purchase tranches, open offer and expanded-voting-capital basis. The formal filing states 26.38% on an expanded basis and up to 52.38% if the open offer is fully accepted; the presentation's 26.6% uses a different share-count presentation. No stake percentage is needed in the words. Do not infer control solely from buying the first block or assume full open-offer acceptance.

No net funding requirement is calculated by subtracting one headline consideration from another. The receipt, share acquisitions, potential open-offer outlay, financing mix and any subsequent capital injection must all be considered before claiming net cash/debt effects.

## Primary accounting basis for the inference

Ind AS 110, paragraphs B86(a)–(c) and B88, in the [Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Rules 2015 Gazette, government-hosted copy](https://thc.nic.in/Central%20Governmental%20Rules/Companies%20%28Indian%20Accounting%20Standards%29%20Rules%2C2015.pdf). Read the consolidation procedure: subsidiary assets/liabilities are combined with the parent's, internal balances eliminated, and consolidation begins on control. This is the basis for the conditional inference about external debt outstanding at a controlled APL Apollo. It does not establish legal recourse against Tata Steel or a precise future balance.
