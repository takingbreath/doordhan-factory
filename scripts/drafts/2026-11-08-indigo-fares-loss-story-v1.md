## English story for approval

In July–September, IndiGo's fare revenue per passenger-kilometre rose 34%. It carried fewer people, yet collected more ticket revenue.

But it still reported a ₹1,879 crore net loss.

The missing piece is the capacity being flown. A plane incurs costs even when some seats go unsold, and IndiGo's occupancy fell.

Across that whole capacity, the company's reported average revenue was ₹5.76 per available seat-kilometre, against costs of ₹6.22. That denominator includes empty seats.

Fuel and currency pressures were part of the squeeze. The passenger was paying more, but the airline was still struggling to cover its costs.

Higher fares helped absorb the cost shock. They still didn't close the gap.

## Narrative and factual scope

Higher fare yield and ticket receipts → surprising net loss → empty-seat/capacity denominator → like-for-like unit gap → cost pressure → payoff answering the traveller's question.

This describes consolidated Q2 FY27, reported 29 October, not today's Diwali fares or every domestic route. The higher fare yield is an average per revenue passenger-kilometre, not a universal ticket hike or an increase per available seat. The company-level revenue/cost comparison uses its reported measures, both net of finance income. It is an accounting comparison, not a cash-flow loss or an exact profit calculation for an individual flight.

Lower occupancy and elevated costs explain the relevant mechanism; no estimate of each factor's exact contribution is made. Do not assert that the fare increase itself caused all lost passengers, that all costs are fixed, or that empty seats alone caused the loss. The net loss actually narrowed year on year, so do not claim deteriorating headline net loss. The amount excluding forex was also a loss; do not attribute everything to an accounting currency entry.
