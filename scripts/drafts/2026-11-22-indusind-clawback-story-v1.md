## English story for approval

Sumant Kathpalia resigned as IndusInd's CEO in April 2025 after its derivatives accounting crisis.

But the bank kept him as an employee and suspended him during disciplinary proceedings.

On 20 November 2026, it terminated his employment and invoked clawback provisions dating back to March 2020, when he became CEO.

That reaches eligible pay already received, beyond simply stopping future bonuses.

The bank hasn't disclosed an amount or announced a completed recovery.

The incentive problem is that a bonus can be paid before the risks show up. Clawback is designed to keep eligible past rewards at stake even after a CEO leaves.

## Argument and delivery

Resignation appears to end the story → continued employment and inquiry complicate that assumption → current decision reaches back to earlier compensation → explain why paid rewards can remain conditional. The payoff is accountability across time, rather than a triumphant claim that shareholders have been repaid.

The mechanism has limits: applicable compensation terms matter; invoking provisions is not the same as obtaining repayment. The filing does not quantify recovery, identify individual awards or reproduce the inquiry findings. Do not call this six years of full salary repaid, a court judgment, a criminal conviction, or a personal obligation to repay the entire bank loss. Continued employment does not prove uninterrupted full salary or that he continued running the bank.

The actual filing also invokes malus, which concerns stopping unvested deferred remuneration. Source notes preserve this distinction. The story concentrates on recovering eligible previously paid/vested remuneration; it does not assert every compensation component is recoverable or restrict the bank's undisclosed action exclusively to cash bonuses.
