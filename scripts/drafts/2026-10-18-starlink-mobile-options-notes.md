## Confirmed facts and corrections

- Three NGSO GMPCS licensees are Starlink, OneWeb India and Jio Satellite Communications. Say Bharti-backed OneWeb rather than implying Airtel itself holds that particular authorization. The government's 8 October statement says all three remain at broadly the same regulatory stage, undergoing security assessment before seeking spectrum assignment. A licence is not a completed commercial launch, nor proof of authorization for every direct-to-phone architecture. [Ministry statement](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2320510&lang=1&reg=3); [minister naming the companies, Akashvani](https://newsonair.gov.in/india-does-not-allow-monopolies-in-any-sector-union-minister-jyotiraditya-scindia/).
- Airtel announced a Starlink agreement on 11 March 2025, explicitly alongside its OneWeb alliance. It covers exploring distribution, enterprise offerings and infrastructure collaboration, subject to authorization. OneWeb does not rule out working with Starlink. [Airtel release](https://www.airtel.in/press-release/03-2025/airtel-announces-agreement-with-spacex-to-bring-starlinks-high-speed-internet-to-its-customers-in-india/).
- Jio announced a Starlink broadband agreement on 12 March 2025, subject to SpaceX authorization. Public disagreements do not establish that this agreement has ended. Neither broadband distribution announcement proves an Indian Direct-to-Cell agreement. [Jio stock-exchange release](https://bsmedia.business-standard.com/_media/bs/data/announcements/bse/12032025/c053fe69-c8bf-4d4a-9f4e-873cb98f08e5.pdf).
- Vi announced a Direct-to-Device partnership with rival AST SpaceMobile on 18 June 2025. Do not present Vi as an unclaimed partner, or assert exclusivity without evidence. [Vi release](https://www.myvi.in/content/dam/vodafoneideadigital/StaticPages/PressReleases/2025/Press-release-Vi-and-AST-SpaceMobile-Announce-Partnership-for-Satellite-Connectivity.pdf).
- Direct-to-Cell satellites act as base stations in space. No nearby terrestrial cell tower or household Starlink dish is needed for the direct handset link. Starlink's documented partner model uses operators' licensed mobile spectrum and core-network integration; ground gateways/core infrastructure still matter. [SpaceX technical explanation](https://starlink.com/public-files/DIRECT_TO_CELL_FIRST_TEXT_UPDATE.pdf).
- Current US T-Satellite supports selected apps including WhatsApp voice/video, with compatible devices, outdoor sky visibility and limited capacity/speeds. This does not establish a replacement for dense urban/indoor terrestrial service or an Indian launch. [T-Mobile announcement](https://www.t-mobile.com/news/network/t-satellite-data-ready-app-expansion).
- India has been consulting on D2D using both mobile-satellite and terrestrial mobile spectrum. Industry submissions are proposals, not enacted permission. Do not claim the existing GMPCS licence alone lets Starlink use Indian mobile operators' frequencies. [TRAI consultation](https://trai.gov.in/consultation-paper-framework-satellite-communication-network-authorisation-and-assignment-spectrum); [Starlink submission](https://trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-05/Starlink_27052026.pdf); [AST submission](https://trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-05/AST_14052026.pdf).
- SpaceX's 8 October agreement to buy US 800 MHz spectrum remains subject to FCC approval. It supports ground-and-space mobile ambitions in the US, not automatic spectrum rights in India. [Grain announcement](https://graingp.com/grain-management-announces-definitive-agreement-to-sell-nationwide-800-mhz-spectrum-portfolio-to-spacex/).

## Business scenarios — analysis, not announcements

1. Partner with an Indian mobile operator: bring satellite coverage to its subscribers, subject to approvals. Faster access to spectrum, distribution and customer relationships; share economics. Partnership with a challenger could improve that challenger's coverage, but does not automatically supply dense urban capacity or force nationwide discounts.
2. Independent satellite mobile service: seek the appropriate Indian permissions and usable spectrum, with compatible devices. Satellites can bypass the local tower; they cannot bypass spectrum authorization. Technical coverage is different from capacity to carry mass-market traffic affordably.
3. Hybrid full mobile competitor: combine satellite reach with ground-based radio capacity and indoor coverage. Could build or lease sites, acquire capabilities or negotiate network access where permitted. Owning every tower is not required; substantial investment and time remain. No confirmed Indian Starlink plan for this route found.

The strategic tension is coverage versus capacity and independence versus shared economics. A partnership can reinforce an incumbent as readily as challenge it. Pressure on ARPU, cash flow or valuation requires genuine customer substitution or a change in competitive behavior; none follows from a licence count alone.
