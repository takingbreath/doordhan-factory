## English story for approval

On 5 November, foreign investors net-bought nearly ₹15,000 crore of Indian shares, according to provisional exchange data reported by Moneycontrol. The Sensex rose 2.6%. Later, the Nasdaq closed down 5.5%, with AI-linked stocks leading the sell-off.

Put those headlines together and it looks like AI money has found a new home.

But check the clock. India had already finished its sixth rising session before US regular trading opened. That later US plunge cannot explain an Indian rally that had already happened. An earlier rotation is still possible.

There were also different risks being priced. Analysts linked India's rally to relief from oil and bond yields, alongside earnings and foreign buying. The US sell-off reflected worries about the AI data-centre buildout.

That gives markets reasons to diverge without proving where the money travelled.

The foreign buying is real. What it doesn't tell us is whether the investors selling Nvidia were the ones buying India.
