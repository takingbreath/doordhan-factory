## English story for approval

SEBI's chairman says that from 1 November, fund houses' own apps and websites will offer direct plans by default. Anyone choosing regular must be told it costs more.

The fund's portfolio and manager are the same. The regular plan includes distribution costs in its expense ratio. That cost comes out of the fund's assets; it doesn't need to arrive as a separate bill.

So the change makes the cheaper route appear first. But a purchase-screen default doesn't rewrite your existing SIP. Existing regular investments aren't automatically converted into direct plans.

And changing old investments yourself is a separate transaction: a switch can bring capital-gains tax or an exit load, depending on the investment and holding period. Distributor assistance may also be something an investor values.

The distinction is simple: the new default changes what you're offered. It doesn't automatically change what you already own.
