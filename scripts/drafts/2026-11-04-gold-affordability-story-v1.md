## English story for approval

Gold eased from its January peak during July–September. But the new World Gold Council report shows why that didn't erase the affordability squeeze in India.

The gold content of jewellery purchases fell 20% from a year earlier. Yet the estimated rupee value of that demand rose 16%.

The missing comparison is last year's price. The quarter's average domestic gold price was still around 45% higher than a year before.

Buyers adapted. The report describes a shift towards lighter pieces, lower-carat gold and studded jewellery, reducing the fine-gold content of purchases.

These are market-wide figures, not a claim that every family's bill rose. But they explain how the rupee value can grow while less gold changes hands.

Cheaper than the peak can still mean less affordable than last year.
