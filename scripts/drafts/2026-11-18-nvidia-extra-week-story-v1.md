## English story for approval

Nvidia has forecast $130 billion of revenue next quarter, after reporting $115.8 billion.

That looks like roughly 12% growth.

But the next quarter has fourteen weeks. The one just reported had thirteen.

Put both on an average-revenue-per-week basis, and the forecast midpoint implies around 4% growth.

That's our simple comparison, not a separate company forecast.

The outlook still implies growth. But the larger revenue target also covers more selling time.

Before calling a bigger quarter faster growth, check whether the clocks match.

## Progression and factual limits

Higher sales target → apparent 12% quarterly increase → extra week changes the denominator → comparable average weekly calculation → identify it as our calculation on guidance → distinguish a bigger reporting total from a faster pace.

The exact inputs are $115.844 billion actual Q3 FY27 revenue and $130 billion at the Q4 guidance midpoint. Raw implied growth is about 12.22%; average revenue per week implies about 4.20%. Use “about 12%” and “around 4%,” with the forecast/actual labels intact. These are sequential comparisons, not year-on-year figures and not the latest quarter's reported 20% sequential growth.

Nvidia disclosed the extra week openly, including in its earlier fiscal-year note. Do not call it concealed, accounting manipulation or an exclusive discovery. Do not say its forecast is actually only 4%, that the business has stopped growing, or that demand itself grows 4%. Both growth measures are valid under their stated denominators.

This is a simple comparison of averages, not a seasonally adjusted demand model, a counterfactual allocation of sales to the extra week, or a prediction of equal shipments every week. The guidance is $130 billion plus/minus 3%; the 4% figure uses the midpoint, not a guarantee or the whole guidance range.
