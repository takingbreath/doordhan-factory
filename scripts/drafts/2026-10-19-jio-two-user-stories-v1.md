## Story 1 — current limits, future broadband overlap

Hook: contradiction between the satellite-war narrative and the allocation of IPO proceeds. Question: why disclose satellite competition when the present products are not close substitutes? Payoff: potential competition in future home-broadband growth, rather than a present mobile-network replacement.

### English dialogue

Jio's IPO isn't funding a satellite war with Musk. ₹27,500 crore is earmarked for debt repayment.

So why does Jio flag satellite competition?

Look at home broadband, where its subscriber base grew 55% in FY26.

AirFiber is expanding where fibre struggles to reach—the same places satellites make sense.

Today, Starlink's pricing and shared capacity limit the threat. Its dish isn't replacing a Jio SIM.

But if satellite economics improve, Jio could face a rival while that market is still opening up.

The risk is competition for tomorrow's customers.

## Story 2 — ARPU arithmetic versus realised monetisation

Hook: the supplied ₹48 illustration corresponds to approximately an IPO's worth of annual revenue. Rehook: that illustrative revenue is easier to model than collect. Payoff: tariff realisation/monetisation of existing scale. The comparison caveat is spoken, not hidden in credits.

### English dialogue

An extra ₹48 in monthly ARPU would mean roughly ₹31,000 crore a year for Jio—about the size of its IPO.

That's its reported gap with Airtel. Customer mixes differ, so this is an illustration, not guaranteed upside.

That revenue is easier to put in a spreadsheet than collect.

Jio has invested heavily in 5G, but unlimited 5G still comes bundled.

A tariff hike helps less if customers downgrade.

So watch how much of the next hike Jio actually retains.

The IPO question is how much more it can earn from the scale it already has.

### Delivery and visuals

Estimated 37–40 seconds at approximately 2.9 spoken words/second after numerical expansion; not audio-measured. Keep the conditional nature of the opening audible. On screen: June 2026 reported ARPU ₹215.6 (Jio) / ₹264 (Airtel India mobile); scopes differ. Show the arithmetic with a constant 53.33 crore customer base, not a revenue or profit forecast. The 5G point is bundling/monetisation, not a claim 5G earns nothing. No date or percentage for an unannounced hike.
