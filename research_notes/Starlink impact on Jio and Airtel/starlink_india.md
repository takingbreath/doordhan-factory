Research notes for Doordhan, compiled Sunday 18 Oct 2026 (IST). Research only: no hooks, scripts or views on any stock or IPO.
Conventions: every figure carries its outlet, publication date and URL. "(context)" marks a source published before 11 Oct 2026 (more than 7 days old). ## 1. Licence history, security conditions, gateways, and what is still pending (Oct 2026)

### Takeaway
Starlink already holds the licences: a DoT GMPCS authorisation (June 2025) and IN-SPACe authorisation for its Gen1 constellation (8 July 2025). It also holds provisional spectrum. It still cannot sell. As of mid-October 2026 it needs three things: the Home Ministry's second-stage security clearance; Cabinet approval of spectrum pricing, then formal assignment; and, for Starlink specifically, FDI approval. The government says the same security queue applies to Jio Satellite Communications and Eutelsat OneWeb.

### Cited Findings
**Licence timeline**
- Nov 2021: Starlink began pre-selling in India. DoT warned the public that it was not authorised and told it to stop; Starlink refunded "thousands of pre-orders". [Business Standard, 8 Oct 2026 (context)](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/starlink-india-entry-why-elon-musk-satellite-internet-launch-clearance-126100800416_1.html). The deposits were US$99, and Starlink gathered more than 5,000 applications before it was licensed. [ORF Expert Speak, Lt Gen M.U. Nair (Retd), 12 Oct 2026](https://www.orfonline.org/expert-speak/india-s-satellite-imperative-starlink-and-the-terms-of-entry)
- The GMPCS application was under consideration from Nov 2022. DoT issued a Letter of Intent in May 2025 and granted the GMPCS authorisation in June 2025. [Business Standard, 8 Oct 2026 (context)](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/starlink-india-entry-why-elon-musk-satellite-internet-launch-clearance-126100800416_1.html). MediaNama reported the permit on 7 Jun 2025, citing the Economic Times, without an exact grant date. It said IN-SPACe approval, spectrum and a security-compliance demonstration were still pending. [MediaNama, 7 Jun 2025 (context)](https://www.medianama.com/2025/06/223-starlink-india-gmpcs-license-dot-approval/)
- Licensing order: Eutelsat OneWeb (Bharti-backed) in 2021, Jio Satellite Communications in 2022, Starlink in mid-2025. [Broadcast and CableSat, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.broadcastandcablesat.co.in/satcom-spectrum-pricing-heads-to-cabinet-as-dot-finalizes-its-note/)
- IN-SPACe "granted authorisation to M/s Starlink Satellite Communications Private Limited, New Delhi (SSCPL)" for the "Starlink Gen1" constellation, dated 8 July 2025. It is valid for five years or until the end of Gen1's operational life, whichever is earlier. Gen1 is 4,408 satellites at 540–570 km. IN-SPACe said rollout is "subject to the stipulated regulatory provisions" and "requisite clearances, approvals and licences". [PTI via The Week, 9 Jul 2025 (context)](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/07/09/del69-space-ld-starlink.html)
- Gen2 constellation: Starlink re-applied to IN-SPACe after an earlier application was rejected (no date given). [ThePrint, 7 Oct 2026 (context)](https://theprint.in/tech/as-launch-awaits-clearance-starlink-says-20-gateways-built-indian-user-data-to-stay-in-india/3064053/)
- Provisional spectrum for trials came in Sept 2025, and Starlink then began security-related testing. In Aug 2025 UIDAI onboarded Starlink for Aadhaar authentication. [Business Standard, 8 Oct 2026 (context)](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/starlink-india-entry-why-elon-musk-satellite-internet-launch-clearance-126100800416_1.html)
- At India Mobile Congress 2026 the government confirmed that all three licensees hold provisional spectrum and are at a similar stage of approval, with launch contingent on Home Ministry (MHA) security clearance. [Broadcast and CableSat, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.broadcastandcablesat.co.in/satcom-spectrum-pricing-heads-to-cabinet-as-dot-finalizes-its-note/)
- A new authorisation regime under the Telecommunications Act, 2023, notified in June 2026, cancelled pending applications, so Amazon Leo (Kuiper) must reapply. Starlink, OneWeb and Jio Satellite keep their GMPCS licences until the 20-year term ends. [Communications Today, 9 Sep 2026 (context)](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/dcc-clears-satcom-spectrum-charges-amazon-leo-faces-relicensing/)

- Locations: Starlink named none. Its earlier plans covered gateways in Mumbai, Pune and Indore, "followed by other locations". [Business Standard, 8 Oct 2026 (context)](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/starlink-india-entry-why-elon-musk-satellite-internet-launch-clearance-126100800416_1.html)

**Security conditions (DoT, May 2025, for all GMPCS licensees)**
- These were issued as tightened GMPCS conditions in May 2025. They require a separate security clearance for each satellite gateway in India and the localisation of all lawful-interception facilities. They also add special provisions that mandate services to specific individuals, groups or areas during hostilities. [Business Standard, 5 May 2025 (context)](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/dot-tightens-security-conditions-satcom-licence-starlink-kuiper-125050501313_1.html)
- Requirements: [Business Standard, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/explained-why-starlink-s-india-launch-plans-have-raised-security-concerns-126101200434_1.html)
  - All Indian user traffic must pass through gateways located in India. Operators may not bypass them through inter-satellite links or mirror Indian traffic to overseas systems.
  - Network control and monitoring centres, data centres and Points of Presence must be in India, and DNS resolution must happen in India.
  - Operators must provide lawful interception and monitoring, and support metadata collection by DoT's Telecom Security Operations Centre (TSOC).
  - Operators must share user-terminal location with authorised agencies, enforce geofencing, and comply with directions to restrict or suspend service in specified areas or for designated users.
  - Land-based mobile terminals must report their location every 2.6 km of movement or every minute, whichever comes first.
  - Operators must support monitoring in designated border and coastal areas, prevent location spoofing, and stop unauthorised terminals from circumventing restrictions.
  - Operators must block websites prohibited by Indian authorities.
- Monitoring zones cover 50 km around international borders and the coastline. At least 20% of the ground segment must be indigenised within five years of launch. Operators must undertake not to copy or decrypt Indian telecom data abroad, and the conditions apply to all operators regardless of origin. [ORF, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.orfonline.org/expert-speak/india-s-satellite-imperative-starlink-and-the-terms-of-entry)
- The provisional licensing terms also require foreign operators to obtain security clearance for foreign nationals on their teams and to report regularly to law-enforcement agencies. Satellite-to-mobile connectivity "remains outside the current allocation, which covers fixed data and internet services". [Broadcast and CableSat, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.broadcastandcablesat.co.in/satcom-spectrum-pricing-heads-to-cabinet-as-dot-finalizes-its-note/)
- NavIC migration by 2029: UNVERIFIED. It appears only in search snippets of Inc42 and Business Standard pages I did not open, and another explainer called it "best-effort".
- Context cited by Business Standard (12 Oct 2026) from a Takshashila Institution report, "Starlink and Risks for India":
  - Security agencies have seized unauthorised Starlink hardware in the Andaman Sea, off Gujarat, along the Punjab border and in Manipur.
  - About 50,000 smuggled terminals were operating in Iran at the start of 2026.
  - On 15 Jan 2026 Starlink updated its Global Privacy Policy to allow customer data to be used to train AI models, with users opted in by default.
  [Business Standard, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/explained-why-starlink-s-india-launch-plans-have-raised-security-concerns-126101200434_1.html)

That contradicts Scindia, who says all three hold licences, and probably means final clearances. [The Week, 11 Oct 2026](https://www.theweek.in/news/sci-tech/2026/10/11/elon-musk-says-starlink-will-be-affordable-in-india-govt-officials-call-claims-on-licence-blatantly-false.html)

### Inferences
- The binding constraints are security clearance, the spectrum-pricing decision and, for Starlink only, FDI approval. The licence is not one of them. A Cabinet decision on pricing would not by itself switch the service on, because MHA clearance is a separate track with no published timeline.
- Several security conditions target features specific to Starlink's global design: inter-satellite laser links, foreign gateways and remote network control. That explains why in-country gateways and India-only routing are the centre of Starlink's public defence.

### Gaps
- The exact day of the GMPCS grant in June 2025 is not on any page I opened.
- No current, official list of gateway locations. Starlink's count (20) conflicts with trade press (about 10 plus a Mumbai hub).
- I did not open the DoT office memorandum of May 2025, so the NavIC wording and the full condition list are not from the primary text.
- I did not open the PIB text of the Ministry's 8 Oct 2026 statement; it is quoted via Business Standard and ANI.
- No official date or timeline for MHA clearance of any licensee.

## 2. Spectrum: TRAI's recommendation, DoT's response, the 2026 draft rules, current status, and what the telcos argued

### Takeaway
In May 2025 TRAI recommended administrative assignment at 4% of AGR (minimum ₹3,500 per MHz), plus ₹500 per urban subscriber a year and no upfront fee. DoT pushed back: it questioned the ₹500 urban charge and suggested 5% with a 1-point concession. TRAI reaffirmed its position in Dec 2025. On 3 Sep 2026, DoT's Digital Communications Commission (DCC) approved 5% of AGR (4% in notified remote zones), for five years plus two, and dropped the urban surcharge and the terminal subsidy. As of 16 Oct 2026 DoT is preparing the Cabinet note, the last policy step. The telcos (through COAI) called TRAI's price "unjustifiably low"; Starlink and Kuiper had asked for under 1%.

### Cited Findings
**TRAI, May 2025**
- Administrative (not auction) allocation, annual spectrum charge of 4% of AGR or ₹3,500 per MHz, whichever is higher, plus ₹500 per subscriber a year in urban areas. A separate 8% licence fee applies under DoT's authorisation terms. [Broadcast and CableSat (Mint copy), 6 Jun 2025 (context)](https://www.broadcastandcablesat.co.in/coai-to-govt-trais-satellite-spectrum-pricing-defies-level-playing-field-2/). No upfront charge; rural users exempt from the ₹500. [MediaNama, 7 Jun 2025 (context)](https://www.medianama.com/2025/06/223-starlink-india-gmpcs-license-dot-approval/)
- TRAI also recommended subsidising user terminals in unserved and underserved rural areas, through direct benefit transfer or the Digital Bharat Nidhi fund. It flagged one-time terminal costs of "typically ₹20,000 to ₹50,000" as a barrier. [Communications Today, 9 Sep 2026 (context)](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/dcc-clears-satcom-spectrum-charges-amazon-leo-faces-relicensing/)
- TRAI chairman Anil Kumar Lahoti: "Since satellite spectrum is a shared pool, the two (terrestrial and satellite) cannot be priced at par." He said the five-year term reflects a nascent sector. [Broadcast and CableSat, 6 Jun 2025 (context)](https://www.broadcastandcablesat.co.in/coai-to-govt-trais-satellite-spectrum-pricing-defies-level-playing-field-2/). Also: "It's not factually correct that satcom services are competing with terrestrial services." [TelecomTalk, 2 Jun 2025 (context)](https://telecomtalk.info/telcos-oppose-trai-satcom-spectrum-plan-unfair/995229/)
- Starlink and Amazon's Kuiper had pressed for under 1% of AGR with no add-on levies; neither TRAI nor DoT accepted that. [Broadcast and CableSat, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.broadcastandcablesat.co.in/satcom-spectrum-pricing-heads-to-cabinet-as-dot-finalizes-its-note/); [ThePrint, 7 Oct 2026 (context)](https://theprint.in/tech/as-launch-awaits-clearance-starlink-says-20-gateways-built-indian-user-data-to-stay-in-india/3064053/)

**DoT's response and back-reference**
- Sep 2025 (citing ET): DoT accepted no upfront payment and the 4% charge, but questioned the ₹500 urban charge as hard to "implement, verify, and audit". It planned to send the issue back to TRAI after the DCC. The ₹500 charge had been introduced "to address concerns from terrestrial network operators such as Jio, Airtel, and Vi". [Outlook Business, 3 Sep 2025 (context)](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/why-dot-rejected-trais-plan-to-charge-extra-500-for-urban-satcom-users)
- Dec 2025: TRAI reaffirmed 4% and the ₹500 urban charge, countering DoT's suggestion of 5% with a 1% discount for hard-to-connect regions. TRAI said geolocation binding can identify where terminals are, declined to keep BSNL's 1% AGR exception, and pressed again for terminal subsidies. [AngelOne, 9 Dec 2025 (context)](https://www.angelone.in/news/economy/trai-rejects-dot-proposal-to-hike-satellite-spectrum-fee-to-5-urban-rate-remains-500)

**DCC decision, 3 Sep 2026, and status now**
- The DCC, DoT's highest decision-making body, approved a 5% spectrum usage charge, reduced by one percentage point to 4% for government-notified hard-to-connect areas (border regions, hilly terrain, islands). Assignment is administrative, for an initial five years with an option of two more. The charge applies uniformly to GSO and NGSO operators offering fixed- and mobile-satellite services, with a minimum of ₹3,500 per MHz a year. The DCC rejected TRAI's ₹500 urban charge (on implementation grounds) and the terminal subsidy. [Communications Today, 9 Sep 2026 (context)](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/dcc-clears-satcom-spectrum-charges-amazon-leo-faces-relicensing/). ThePrint describes the same terms and says they need ratification by an inter-ministerial panel and the Cabinet. [ThePrint, 7 Oct 2026 (context)](https://theprint.in/tech/as-launch-awaits-clearance-starlink-says-20-gateways-built-indian-user-data-to-stay-in-india/3064053/)
- Combined levy with the 8% licence fee: 13% of AGR, or 12% in notified hard-to-reach areas. DoT "is preparing a Cabinet note", described as "the final policy step before the government can formally assign spectrum". Broadcast and CableSat's own analysis: the 5% rate "partly answers the level playing field argument". [Broadcast and CableSat, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.broadcastandcablesat.co.in/satcom-spectrum-pricing-heads-to-cabinet-as-dot-finalizes-its-note/)
- As of 8 Oct 2026 the 5% charge was "still awaiting Union Cabinet approval". [Business Standard, 8 Oct 2026 (context)](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/starlink-india-entry-why-elon-musk-satellite-internet-launch-clearance-126100800416_1.html)
- Scindia, 9 Oct 2026: TRAI and DoT are close to a final decision. "The fact that TRAI makes a recommendation doesn't mean that the announcement is made the next day." [MediaNama, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-satcom-licensees-security-clearance/). (given) "close to" a revenue-based framework: [Business Standard, 17 Oct 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/satcom-row-starlink-says-india-infra-has-govt-approvals-ready-for-launch-126101700036_1.html)
- Existing GSO players such as Hughes and Nelco, which serve mostly enterprise and government customers, have paid spectrum charges of 3–4% of AGR. [Broadcast and CableSat, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.broadcastandcablesat.co.in/satcom-spectrum-pricing-heads-to-cabinet-as-dot-finalizes-its-note/)

**The 2026 draft "Telecommunications (Spectrum Assignment by Administrative Process) Rules"**
- Published in the Gazette in mid-June 2026 with a 30-day comment window. The Week's reading of the draft:
  - Fixed annual fees rather than auctions.
  - VSAT commercial spectrum at 3–4% of AGR, BSNL's satellite phone at 1%, and a ₹1,000 application fee.
  - A separate security clearance before any consumer satellite broadband or phone service.
  - No interconnection with public networks "without explicit government permission".
  - BSNL and MTNL must reapply at market-linked prices.
  The Week framed the draft as affecting Starlink, OneWeb and Jio Satcom. [The Week, 22 Jun 2026 (context)](https://www.theweek.in/news/biz-tech/2026/06/22/india-satellite-communication-regulations.amp.html)
- Disagreement on scope: tele.net.in says the draft covers only GSO operators (VSAT, teleport, broadcasting), not NGSO operators such as Starlink, OneWeb or Kuiper. It says GSO operators keep 3–4% of AGR and that NGSO pricing was still being considered separately. [tele.net.in, 22 Jun 2026, updated 24 Jun (context)](https://tele.net.in/dot-releases-draft-framework-for-satcom-spectrum-assignment/). Broadcast and CableSat (16 Oct) supports that reading: the separate draft "retains that structure for VSAT and teleport operators". [Broadcast and CableSat, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.broadcastandcablesat.co.in/satcom-spectrum-pricing-heads-to-cabinet-as-dot-finalizes-its-note/)

**What Jio, Airtel, Vi and COAI argued**
- Joint COAI letter dated 29 May 2025 from Director-General S.P. Kochhar to Telecom Secretary Neeraj Mittal, copied to the TRAI chairman and DCC members: "The recommendation of spectrum usage charges—set at 4% of AGR—without any entry fee/upfront payment is inconsistent with the approach followed so far in case of administrative spectrum allocations, and is without any rationale, empirical analysis, international benchmarking or economic justification." The letter cites the "trillions of rupees" that terrestrial operators have spent at auctions, says satellite will compete with terrestrial services rather than complement them, and asks for a review committee. It claims Kuiper and Starlink's planned capacity of about 29 billion GB a month is greater than the operators' current traffic of about 23 billion GB a month. [Broadcast and CableSat (Mint copy), 6 Jun 2025 (context)](https://www.broadcastandcablesat.co.in/coai-to-govt-trais-satellite-spectrum-pricing-defies-level-playing-field-2/)
- The telcos called the pricing "unjustifiably low" and the process "non-transparent". They argued:
  - the proposed charge sits below what GSO VSAT services pay;
  - TRAI underestimated satellite capacity;
  - the Digital Bharat Nidhi should not subsidise satellite terminals;
  - Kenya is a precedent: it raised satellite licence fees by nearly 1,000% after NGSO operators gained share too quickly.
  [Telecom Review Asia, 3 Jun 2025 (context)](https://www.telecomreviewasia.com/news/policy-news/13588-indias-telcos-criticize-trais-satcom-proposals/); [TelecomTalk, 2 Jun 2025 (context)](https://telecomtalk.info/telcos-oppose-trai-satcom-spectrum-plan-unfair/995229/). Business Standard: "ad hoc", "based on incorrect assumptions". [Business Standard, 1 Jun 2025 (context)](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/telcos-slam-trai-satellite-spectrum-charges-as-unjustifiably-low-125060100523_1.html)
- (given) In Oct 2024 Reliance lobbied for auctioning satellite spectrum; the government chose administrative allocation. [Outlook Business, 14 Oct 2024](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/amp/story/explainers/mukesh-ambani-vs-elon-musk-war-over-satellite-spectrum-intensifies-as-jio-lobbies-hard-for-auction). (given) Jio's RHP says the administrative framework "may lower entry economics" for satellite rivals. [Business Standard, 15 Oct 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/jio-platforms-satellite-broadband-india-starlink-leo-spectrum-risk-126101500576_1.html)

### Inferences
- On the headline rate, the government moved towards the telcos: 5% against TRAI's 4% and Starlink's request for under 1%. It went against them on method (no auction) and by dropping the ₹500 urban surcharge that was meant to address their concerns.
- My calculation (ignores GST and the difference between AGR and subscription revenue): on a ₹3,000-a-month urban plan (₹36,000 a year), TRAI's ₹500 adds about 1.4 points, so TRAI's urban formula comes to about 5.4% against the DCC's 5%. On an ₹8,600-a-month plan the ₹500 adds about 0.5 points (about 4.5% against 5%). For urban users the two designs cost about the same; the DCC version costs more in rural areas outside the notified zones (5% against TRAI's 4%).

### Gaps
- I did not open TRAI's primary recommendation document or press release, so the exact May 2025 date and the full text are not confirmed.
- I did not open the Gazette text of the draft rules. A search snippet mentioned per-terminal fixed fees of ₹30,000 to ₹50 lakh: UNVERIFIED.
- Apart from the joint COAI letter, I found no separate on-record statements from Jio, Airtel or Vi on the 2025–26 pricing.
- No Cabinet meeting date.

## 3. Official capacity and speed statements (the "20 lakh users, 200 Mbps" claim)

### Takeaway
The quote is real. Minister of State for Communications Chandra Sekhar Pemmasani said in late July 2025, on the sidelines of a BSNL review meeting, that "Starlink can have only 20 lakh customers in India and offer up to 200 Mbps speed. That won't affect telecom services." He meant it as reassurance to incumbents, and officials said the figure reflects Starlink's capacity, not a policy cap. Other capacity numbers in circulation: PTI's "approximately 600-GBPS throughput over India" for Gen1, an unattributed 10,000–20,000 connections per Delhi-sized market, and COAI's 29 billion GB a month. They do not agree with each other.

### Cited Findings
- Pemmasani: "Starlink can have only 20 lakh customers in India and offer up to 200 Mbps speed. That won't affect telecom services". Also: "The upfront cost for satcom services will be too high, and the monthly cost may be around ₹3,000". He said this "on the sidelines of a Bharat Sanchar Nigam Limited (BSNL) review meeting", where he also said BSNL had finished its nationwide 4G rollout ("We want the market first. There are no tariff hikes planned"). - The prospectus does not single out India's delay as a material, India-specific risk. India is covered, if at all, by the generic language. This is an inference from absence, so it is not proof of how SpaceX weighs India.
- Satellite-to-mobile is a large part of SpaceX's pitch, but India's current satcom allocation covers fixed data and internet only (Section 1). The prospectus's mobile story therefore does not apply to India under current rules.

### Gaps
- I searched the S-1, the 424B4 and the 8 Jun FWP for India, Indian, Reliance, Jio, Airtel and Bharti. I did not search the UK and Japan FWPs or the confidential draft versions in full.
- I did not check whether Harrison is still on Reliance Jio's board as of Oct 2026; Jio's RHP director list was not checked.

## 9. Surprises: facts that cut against common claims

### Takeaway
Several widely repeated lines are wrong or incomplete:
- the "20 lakh cap" is not a rule;
- the "₹8,600 price" was disowned by Starlink;
- the "₹500 per urban user" fee was dropped;
- Starlink is licensed, and the hold-up is a security review that also covers Jio's and Bharti's ventures;
- SpaceX's own prospectus shows a director on Reliance Jio's board.

### Cited Findings
- **The "cap" is a capacity estimate, not a legal limit.** Officials indicated the 20 lakh figure "is tied to the company's current operational capacity, rather than any policy restriction". The minister said it at a BSNL review meeting to reassure incumbents: "That won't affect telecom services." [Business Today, 29 Jul 2025 (context)](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/starlink-limited-to-20-lakh-users-in-india-monthly-cost-around-rs-3000-telecom-minister-486720-2025-07-29). Vietnam, by contrast, has a real licence cap of 600,000 terminals. [Ookla, 12 Apr 2026 (context)](https://www.ookla.com/articles/low-orbit-high-stakes-starlinks-asia-pacific-performance-and-path-to-market)
- **The most-quoted "official" price is not official.** Starlink called the ₹8,600 + ₹34,000 figures "dummy test data" that "do not reflect what the cost of Starlink service will be in India". [Storyboard18, 9 Dec 2025 (context)](https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/no-pricing-announced-yet-starlink-denies-india-tariff-leak-calls-website-rates-test-data-85583.htm)
- **Starlink is licensed; the blocker is security clearance, which also holds up its rivals.** Starlink has a GMPCS licence (Jun 2025) and IN-SPACe authorisation (Jul 2025). Officials called Musk's licensing claim "blatantly false". [The Week, 11 Oct 2026](https://www.theweek.in/news/sci-tech/2026/10/11/elon-musk-says-starlink-will-be-affordable-in-india-govt-officials-call-claims-on-licence-blatantly-false.html). Bharti-backed OneWeb, licensed in 2021, is also still awaiting clearance. [Broadcast and CableSat, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.broadcastandcablesat.co.in/satcom-spectrum-pricing-heads-to-cabinet-as-dot-finalizes-its-note/). (given) Reliance says "Jio's own satellite services await clearances." [Business Standard, 17 Oct 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/satcom-row-starlink-says-india-infra-has-govt-approvals-ready-for-launch-126101700036_1.html)
- **Starlink has its own extra hurdle.** FDI approval for the wholly SpaceX-owned Indian unit was still pending as of 8 Oct 2026. [Business Standard, 8 Oct 2026 (context)](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/starlink-india-entry-why-elon-musk-satellite-internet-launch-clearance-126100800416_1.html). Its clearance was reportedly "effectively frozen in June after reports of its terminals operating in Iran". [Broadcast and CableSat, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.broadcastandcablesat.co.in/satcom-spectrum-pricing-heads-to-cabinet-as-dot-finalizes-its-note/)
- **The government is set to charge more than the regulator proposed, not less.** The DCC approved 5% of AGR against TRAI's 4% and Starlink's request for under 1%. It also scrapped TRAI's ₹500 urban fee, which had been meant to address the telcos' concerns. [Communications Today, 9 Sep 2026 (context)](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/dcc-clears-satcom-spectrum-charges-amazon-leo-faces-relicensing/); [Outlook Business, 3 Sep 2025 (context)](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/why-dot-rejected-trais-plan-to-charge-extra-500-for-urban-satcom-users)
- **The telcos' own letter contradicts the "Starlink is too small to matter" line.** COAI told the government that Starlink and Kuiper's planned India capacity (about 29 billion GB a month) is more than the three telcos' current traffic (about 23 billion GB). The government says Starlink can serve only 20 lakh users. [Broadcast and CableSat, 6 Jun 2025 (context)](https://www.broadcastandcablesat.co.in/coai-to-govt-trais-satellite-spectrum-pricing-defies-level-playing-field-2/); [Business Today, 29 Jul 2025 (context)](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/starlink-limited-to-20-lakh-users-in-india-monthly-cost-around-rs-3000-telecom-minister-486720-2025-07-29)

[SpaceX 424B4, 12 Jun 2026 (context)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026042639/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm)
- **Jio and Airtel are Starlink's partners as well as its rivals.** Both signed agreements in Mar 2025 to bring Starlink to their customers, subject to approvals. [Business Standard, 8 Oct 2026 (context)](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/starlink-india-entry-why-elon-musk-satellite-internet-launch-clearance-126100800416_1.html)

Latency is the weak point: 35–103 ms against 7 ms. [Ookla, 12 Apr 2026 (context)](https://www.ookla.com/articles/low-orbit-high-stakes-starlinks-asia-pacific-performance-and-path-to-market); [Ookla Speedtest Global Index, India](https://www.speedtest.net/global-index/india)

[tele.net.in, 22 Jun 2026 (context)](https://tele.net.in/dot-releases-draft-framework-for-satcom-spectrum-assignment/); [The Week, 22 Jun 2026 (context)](https://www.theweek.in/news/biz-tech/2026/06/22/india-satellite-communication-regulations.amp.html)

[ThePrint, 7 Oct 2026 (context)](https://theprint.in/tech/as-launch-awaits-clearance-starlink-says-20-gateways-built-indian-user-data-to-stay-in-india/3064053/); [Broadcast and CableSat, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.broadcastandcablesat.co.in/satcom-spectrum-pricing-heads-to-cabinet-as-dot-finalizes-its-note/)
- **Satellite to mobile phones is not in India's current allocation**, which covers fixed data and internet services. [Broadcast and CableSat, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.broadcastandcablesat.co.in/satcom-spectrum-pricing-heads-to-cabinet-as-dot-finalizes-its-note/)

### Inferences
- The public story ("oligarchs blocking Starlink" against "Starlink is too small to matter") leaves out the on-record mechanics: an MHA security review covering all three licensees, a Cabinet-pending price that is higher than TRAI's, and a Starlink-specific FDI approval. No opened source shows evidence that Reliance is behind the delay. The government denies bias, and Reliance says its own satellite services are also waiting.
- The capacity numbers in circulation span two orders of magnitude: PTI's 600 Gbps for Gen1, COAI's 29 billion GB a month, and the unattributed 10,000–20,000 connections per Delhi. Any claim about how many Indians Starlink can serve depends on which one is used.

### Gaps
- No primary document or official statement confirms the reported June 2026 Iran-linked freeze; it rests on Bloomberg via ThePrint and on Broadcast and CableSat.
- No source explains the basis of COAI's 29 billion GB a month or the 10,000–20,000-per-Delhi figure.
