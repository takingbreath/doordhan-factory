## 1. Country by country: launch dates, monthly prices, terminal cost and schemes, and price changes over time

### Takeaway
Across emerging markets, Starlink charges a fairly flat ~$30–65 a month for home service (≈ ₹2,900–6,200 today) plus a $180–500 kit (≈ ₹17,000–48,000), so it is a luxury in poor countries. Prices move both ways: down (kit cuts, rentals, Lite and Mini plans, US promotions) where there is spare capacity or slow uptake, and up (Nigeria +50%, Philippines +52% since launch, Malaysia, Jamaica, US, Brazil in 2026) where the network is full or the local currency has fallen.

### Inferences
- The South Asian neighbours (Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Bhutan) cluster at about ₹3,000–3,450 for Lite and ₹4,200–4,650 for Residential at today's rates, with kits at ₹17,000 (Bhutan Mini) to ₹36,000 (Standard). That fits the ₹2,500–4,500 range the caller cites from Jio's prospectus and makes ₹3,000–4,500/month plus a ₹17,000–35,000 kit the most likely Indian band, if Starlink prices India like its neighbours. Bhutan is the cleanest comparator because its currency is at par with the rupee.
- Against Jio's ₹214 ARPU (caller-supplied), even Bhutan's ₹3,000 Lite plan is about 14 times a typical Indian mobile bill before the kit. Bangladesh shows what happens at roughly 10 times local broadband prices: 3,469 subscribers after eight months (section 4).
- Starlink's pricing is set by capacity as much as by income: it cut prices or offered free and rented kit where it had spare capacity or weak uptake (Kenya 2024, US March 2026, Indonesia's launch promotion) and raised them where cells filled or currencies fell (Nigeria, Philippines, Malaysia, Jamaica, US and Brazil in 2026). An Indian launch price would therefore not stay fixed; it would likely be revised as dense cells fill.
- Price increases in 2026 came in several markets in the same months (US May–June, Brazil May, Philippines May, Malaysia June, Jamaica June), around SpaceX's June 2026 listing. The timing is consistent with a group-wide repricing, but no opened source says so.

### Gaps
- No official Starlink price page could be opened for any country; prices come from news outlets, regulators' notices and trackers, and several reseller pages disagree. Current Indonesian, Malaysian kit and Zambian prices are unverified.
- Launch dates are unverified for Zambia and Malawi (MACRA's 2022 licence is search-result only) and conflicting for Kenya (June or July 2023) and Nigeria (January or February 2023).
- I found no 2026 Bangladesh or Sri Lanka price change, and no Malawi update after October 2024.
- Whether Nigeria's NGN 57,000 and NGN 75,000 plans are two tiers or successive prices is not confirmed.

## 2. Measured speeds (Ookla 2025–2026), the US reference, and slowdowns as subscribers grew

### Takeaway
Ookla's data show a consistent pattern: where users pile into a fixed amount of local capacity, Starlink's median download falls to roughly 35–50 Mbps (Kenya, Nigeria, Zimbabwe, Indonesia, post-hurricane Jamaica), and where load eases or capacity is added it recovers to 90–130 Mbps or more (Malaysia, Japan, the US). Even in a healthy market like Brazil, speeds drop about 40% between the 2 a.m. peak and the 1 p.m. trough.

### Cited Findings

**US reference**
- Q1 2026 national medians: Starlink 127.39 Mbps down, 21.46 Mbps up, 39 ms latency (HughesNet 48.55 Mbps, Viasat 41.05 Mbps); 44.7% of Starlink users met the FCC's 100/20 Mbps benchmark in Q4 2025, up from 17.4% in Q1 2025; in H2 2025 the median download exceeded 100 Mbps in every state but Alaska (23 states in H2 2024) — [Ookla, 5 May 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/starlink-hits-new-us-highs).
- H1 2026: 17 states had at least half of Starlink users meeting 100/20 (none in H1 2025, five in H2 2025), but the big subscriber states lag: California 42.8%, Texas 31.4%, Florida 30.9%. Ookla says their larger sample volumes suggest "greater capacity demands and potential congestion" — [Broadband Breakfast, 15 Sep 2026](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/ookla-starlink-performance-surged-in-first-half-of-2026/), summarising [Ookla's H1 2026 report](https://www.ookla.com/research/reports/us-broadband-performance-report-h1-2026) (report page not opened).
- New Jersey, the most densely populated US state, saw its 25th-percentile download rise from 11.68 Mbps (H2 2024) to 77.64 Mbps (H2 2025) after newer high-capacity satellites — [Ookla, 5 May 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/starlink-hits-new-us-highs).
- "Because Starlink is a finite, shared resource, users' network speeds can fluctuate on a daily – or even hourly – basis"; US connections are faster in the middle of the night — [Ookla, 4 Feb 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/2025-global-satellite-broadband-performance-report).

**Global**
- Q3 2025 fastest Starlink medians: Latvia 187.30, Northern Mariana Islands 186.15, New Zealand 185.37, Azerbaijan 182.44, Portugal 180.18 Mbps; slowest: South Sudan 15.87, Madagascar 23.64, Liberia 26.74, Svalbard 27.80, Yemen 28.07 Mbps. Starlink had 97.1% of all satellite Speedtest samples; its top five markets by samples were the US, Mexico, Indonesia, Brazil and Canada — [Ookla, 4 Feb 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/2025-global-satellite-broadband-performance-report).
- UNVERIFIED (search summary of a Broadband Breakfast article, not opened): Europe's Q1 2026 median of 165.71 Mbps across 27 countries, up from 114.05 Mbps a year earlier.

**Asia-Pacific (Q4 2024 → Q4 2025)**
- Indonesia: unique device samples +33.9% while median download fell from 45.16 to 40.69 Mbps, which Ookla calls "demand outpacing available gateway capacity" — [Ookla, 12 Apr 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/low-orbit-high-stakes-starlinks-asia-pacific-performance-and-path-to-market).
- Malaysia: samples −27.2% while median download nearly doubled from 53.74 to 98.68 Mbps "as network load eased" (infrastructure upgrades may also have contributed) — same source.
- Philippines: samples −6.2% after the May 2025 price rise; download 42.68 → 53.27 Mbps — same source.
- Japan: 91.13 Mbps (Q4 2024) fell to 61.75 Mbps (Q1 2025), then recovered to 104.60 Mbps (Q4 2025), a pattern Ookla says mirrors Germany and Switzerland, "where speeds fell as demand grew, then recovered as network capacity increased" — same source.
- Bangladesh: 88.95 Mbps and 35 ms latency in Q4 2025, its second operating quarter, with a local gateway; Sri Lanka 140.68 Mbps but 103 ms; East Timor 157 ms, Mongolia 137 ms, Maldives 118 ms; Australia 162.47 Mbps; New Zealand 35 ms — same source.
- Philippines: Starlink averages about 55 Mbps against over 100 Mbps for fixed broadband, and slows in crowded areas — [Unbox, 18 Apr 2026](https://unbox.ph/news/starlink-philippines-market/).

**Sub-Saharan Africa (Q1 2024 → Q1 2026)**
- 16 of 23 countries exceeded 50 Mbps median download in Q1 2026; only Eswatini, Botswana and Senegal exceeded 100 Mbps. The East region fell steadily from 50.67 Mbps (Q1 2024) to 37.39 Mbps (Q1 2026); the Southern region peaked at 87.5 Mbps (Q3 2025) and fell to 59.58; Central peaked at 96.24 and settled at 75.3; West reached 54.6 Mbps — [Ookla, 15 Jun 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/starlink-ssa-q1-2026).
- Nigeria, Kenya, Zimbabwe and Madagascar are in the under-50 Mbps tier and "show signs of bandwidth bottlenecks. This aligns with the heavily populated or highly subscribed countries that forced Starlink to pause sign-ups"; Zambia's download fell 22% and Zimbabwe's 29% year on year; Nigeria held around 48–49 Mbps down, ~15 Mbps up, ~50 ms — same source.
- Kenya's latency fell from 289 ms to 53 ms after the Nairobi point of presence opened in January 2025 ([Ookla, 4 Feb 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/2025-global-satellite-broadband-performance-report)) and reached 39 ms in Q1 2026, the lowest in Africa ([Ookla, 15 Jun 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/starlink-ssa-q1-2026)).
- Kenya's median download was 34.55 Mbps in March 2026 against roughly 47 Mbps a year earlier (−26.5%); the article says the data don't establish that subscriber growth caused the drop — [TechTrendsKE, 6 Oct 2026](https://techtrendske.co.ke/2026/10/06/starlink-kenya-capacity-users-growth/) (its underlying data source is not named).
- Against terrestrial ISPs: Starlink leads on download in 22 of 23 markets (not Madagascar), but local ISPs beat it on upload in 10 of 23 and on latency in most; Ookla's analyst Karim Yaici notes that terrestrial ISPs keep "a clear advantage in latency and price" — [Ookla, 15 Jun 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/starlink-ssa-q1-2026).
- Nigeria: fixed ISPs doubled their median download from 16.51 Mbps (Q2 2024) to 33.97 Mbps (Q1 2026), "steadily closing the gap with Starlink"; Starlink's upload stayed at 11.31–15.70 Mbps and its latency fell from 76 to 50 ms, against 30–40 ms for ISPs; Starlink's "performance has fluctuated in recent quarters, possibly pointing to network congestion and capacity constraints" — [Ookla, 28 Sep 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/nigeria-connectivity-2026).
- Secondary coverage disagrees: one article lists Kenya at 172 Mbps, Nigeria 125 Mbps and Zambia 181 Mbps — [tech.africa, 16 Jun 2026](https://tech.africa/starlink-africa/). These conflict with Ookla's own article above and should not be used.

**Brazil and Jamaica (direct load evidence)**
- Brazil, Q1 2026: the median download peaked at 149.5 Mbps around 2 a.m. and fell to 90.6 Mbps by 1 p.m., "a nearly 40% drop in speed during peak business hours (and up to a 43% drop on weekdays)"; the peak-to-trough gap improved from 48.9% (Q3 2025) to 29.6% (Q2 2026); speeds "have dipped in recent months"; users cluster at 50–200 Mbps with "virtually no users consistently reaching gigabit speeds" — [Ookla, 29 Jul 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/satellites-at-scale-a-performance-audit-of-starlink-in-brazil).
- Jamaica: Starlink Speedtest samples rose 178% in the three months after Hurricane Melissa (late October 2025), and the median download "plummeted from over 100 Mbps before Melissa to below 50 Mbps in the months following"; after a June 2026 price rise it recovered to above 50 Mbps in May–July 2026 — [Ookla, 5 Aug 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/jamaica-starlink-data-speeds-drop-after-hurricane-melissa).

### Inferences
- The clearest natural experiments, Indonesia (more users, slower), Malaysia (fewer users, faster), Japan (dip, then recovery as capacity was added) and Jamaica (users nearly tripled, speed halved), all point the same way: Starlink speed in a cell depends on how many active users share it, and recovers only when users fall or capacity is added.
- Fresh launches look fast because the user base is tiny: Bangladesh posted 88.95 Mbps with a few thousand subscribers. Early Indian speed tests would likely be flattering in the same way; the relevant test comes after a few quarters of growth in dense cells.
- A ~40% daytime drop in Brazil, a market with more than a dozen ground stations, suggests Indian cities would see a similar or steeper daily swing, given India's evening-heavy residential usage. This is an inference, not measured.
- Latency is mostly a ground-station question (Kenya 289 → 39 ms), so the latency gap with Indian fibre is likely to be small if local gateways are built; throughput under load is the harder problem.

### Gaps
- Ookla's published articles give no 2025–2026 Starlink medians for Bhutan, Pakistan (not launched), Vietnam (excluded as a pilot), Zambia or Malawi in absolute terms; Kenya's figure comes from a secondary article.
- Ookla's own H1 2026 US report and the Europe Q1 2026 figures were not opened directly.
- No independent speed data for India were found; the only India figures in search results came from a commercial speed-test site and are not used.

## 3. Capacity limits: sold-out areas, waitlists, surcharges, pauses and caps, and what per-cell capacity implies for dense countries

### Takeaway
Starlink has repeatedly run out of capacity in emerging-market cities (Nairobi twice, Lagos and Abuja, Lusaka, Harare, all of Indonesia) and in parts of the US. It manages scarcity with four tools: sign-up pauses and deposits, one-time surcharges (up to $1,500 in the US; IDR 8–9.4m in Indonesia), price rises, and access only through costlier priority tiers. Analysts (MoffettNathanson's Craig Moffett; Vish Nandlall) argue the binding limit is capacity per spot-beam cell, which makes dense areas hard to serve even with V3 satellites.

### Cited Findings

**Pauses, waitlists and sold-out areas**
- Kenya: sign-ups in Nairobi and surrounding areas halted for network oversubscription, with the map showing "sold out" — [Connecting Africa, 28 Jan 2025](https://www.connectingafrica.com/connectivity/starlink-doubles-market-share-in-kenya-launches-ground-station). The late-2024 suspension was lifted in July 2025 after about seven months; new residential orders are again restricted in Nairobi, Kiambu, Mombasa, Machakos, Murang'a, Kirinyaga, Kwale and Kilifi, with prospective customers put on a waiting list and asked for a deposit — [TechTrendsKE, 6 Oct 2026](https://techtrendske.co.ke/2026/10/06/starlink-kenya-capacity-users-growth/).
- Nigeria: in September 2025 Starlink halted new residential orders in parts of Lagos and Abuja after reaching "design capacity limits", with waitlists and no fixed activation date; in February 2026 it reopened access only through the Business priority tier at NGN 159,000 a month (≈ $119), leaving residential users in the most congested areas "largely locked out" — [Space in Africa, 26 Aug 2026](https://spaceinafrica.com/2026/08/26/starlink-narrows-the-gap-to-under-13000-as-nigerias-isp-market-fragments-further-in-q2-2026/). Ookla also notes "periodic onboarding freezes" — [Ookla, 28 Sep 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/nigeria-connectivity-2026).
- Africa generally: sign-ups halted at times in Abuja, Lagos, Nairobi, Lusaka and Harare "due to capacity limitations"; V2 Mini launches are "helping to alleviate these constraints" — [Ookla, 15 Jun 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/starlink-ssa-q1-2026). Harare was oversubscribed and equipment sold out in just over two months after launch — [Connecting Africa, 4 Nov 2024](https://www.connectingafrica.com/connectivity/starlink-s-entry-into-zimbabwe-lowers-internet-tariffs).
- Indonesia: Starlink said its capacity was "sold out" nationwide and paused new customers and the activation of kits bought from retailers; Komdigi's Director General of Digital Infrastructure, Wayan Toni Supriyanto, said activation reopened on 22 July 2025 — [Antara, 22 Jul 2025](https://www.antaranews.com/berita/4983973/kemkomdigi-sebut-starlink-mulai-buka-layanan-aktivasi-pelanggan-baru). Demand surge fees of IDR 8–9.4m followed for high-demand gateway areas — [Ookla, 12 Apr 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/low-orbit-high-stakes-starlinks-asia-pacific-performance-and-path-to-market). UNVERIFIED: Komdigi's statements that Starlink was adding E-band spectrum and that a landing-rights evaluation preceded the reopening (Indonesian outlets in search results, not opened).

**US surcharges**
- Waitlists in several regions were replaced by one-time congestion charges: reports of $1,500 across Alaska (from Reddit users), $1,000 cut to $500 in the Pacific Northwest, $500 in western North Carolina and $250 where waitlists were recently lifted; Starlink offers free equipment with a 12-month commitment in areas with excess capacity — [SatelliteInternet.com, 27 Jan 2026](https://www.satelliteinternet.com/resources/starlink-congestion-surcharge/). The surcharge does not appear on SpaceX's public pages — [LiveEarthViewer, updated 8 Sep 2026](https://liveearthviewer.com/starlink/learn/how-much-does-starlink-cost).
- Vish Nandlall notes that Starlink broadband "already applies congestion charges in sold-out areas" — [RCR Wireless, 7 Oct 2026](https://rcrwireless.com/20261007/carriers/what-a-single-square-kilometer-tells-us-about-starlink-mobile-vish).

**Using price to shed load**
- Jamaica: Starlink raised monthly residential service from JMD 7,000 (USD 44.40) to JMD 7,450 (USD 47.25) from 18 June [2026]. Ookla's Kerry Baker suggests the rise may have been meant "to encourage customers to leave in order to regain capacity", adding "(It might be working.)"; speeds recovered to above 50 Mbps — [Ookla, 5 Aug 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/jamaica-starlink-data-speeds-drop-after-hurricane-melissa).
- Ookla's Affandy Johan: "Starlink's pricing responds to demand and capacity conditions in ways that fixed-line operators typically do not" — [Ookla, 12 Apr 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/low-orbit-high-stakes-starlinks-asia-pacific-performance-and-path-to-market).

**Caps by regulation**
- Vietnam limits Starlink to 600,000 terminals in its pilot phase (about 2.5% of the market) — [VnEconomy, 15 Aug 2026](https://en.vneconomy.vn/starlink-officially-launches-in-vietnam-prices-starting-at-43month.htm).
- UNVERIFIED: reports that India caps Starlink at 20 lakh (2 million) users (search results only, not opened).

**Capacity supply (company figures)**
- About 9,600 broadband and mobile satellites at 31 March 2026, mostly V2 Mini; V3 satellites, "designed to offer one Tbps of downlink capacity per satellite", deploy on Starship from H2 2026, up to 60 per launch, "a twenty-fold increase in Starlink downlink capacity deployed relative to a Falcon 9 launch". SpaceX also writes: "As network capacity increases and product performance continues to improve, we expect to compete increasingly in suburban and urban environments" — [SpaceX S-1, 20 May 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026036936/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm).
- More than 3,000 V2 Mini Optimized satellites launched in 2025 added more than 270 Tbps; each V3 Starship launch is projected to add 60 Tbps, "more than 20 times the V1.5 capacity added with each launch today" — [Starlink Progress Report 2025 (PDF)](https://starlink.com/public-files/starlinkProgressReport_2025.pdf).
- Cumulative network capacity "recently passed 600 Tbps"; Cloudflare saw Starlink's traffic grow 2.3x in 2025 — [Ookla, 4 Feb 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/2025-global-satellite-broadband-performance-report).
- V3 claims 10 times the downlink and 24 times the uplink of V2, but needs new user terminals, so "the adoption and effect on customer experience won't happen overnight" — [Ookla, 5 Aug 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/jamaica-starlink-data-speeds-drop-after-hurricane-melissa).

**Analysts on capacity per cell and density**
- Craig Moffett (MoffettNathanson): the main constraint is the capacity each hex cell's spot beam can support, not constellation size; Starlink can "comfortably" serve only the eighth and ninth US density deciles; as data use rises the density cut-off falls; expectations of disrupting suburban and urban broadband are "unreasonably high"; V3 takes about five years to roll out. Forecast: 14.81m consumer subscribers by end-2026 (3.16m US) and 97m by 2031 — [Light Reading, 13 Jul 2026](https://www.lightreading.com/satellite/even-with-bigger-birds-starlink-still-best-suited-to-low-density-markets-study).
- Vish Nandlall (Vish Nandlall Consulting), modelling Starlink Mobile in the US: estimated Ku/Ka broadband capacity of about 4–55 Mbps per km² of busy-hour load; a direct-to-cell beam of about 0.33 Mbps per km², enough for about 8 subscribers per km², so satellite alone carries a 10% mobile share only below about 82 people per km² (just above 1,000 per km² with a 250 m² antenna array) — [RCR Wireless, 7 Oct 2026](https://rcrwireless.com/20261007/carriers/what-a-single-square-kilometer-tells-us-about-starlink-mobile-vish). The article does not discuss India.

### Inferences
- Rough global arithmetic from company figures: capacity grew from roughly 330 Tbps to just over 600 Tbps in 2025 (+~80%) while subscribers grew from 4.4m to 8.9m (+102%; S-1). Capacity per subscriber therefore fell slightly in 2025, which fits the congestion seen in Kenya, Nigeria, Indonesia and Brazil. This is a global average and ignores where capacity sits.
- Taking Nandlall's broadband estimate of 4–55 Mbps per km² and assuming (my assumption) a busy-hour need of 2–5 Mbps per home, one km² could carry roughly 1–25 heavy-use homes at peak. Any Indian neighbourhood with thousands of homes per km² would fill a cell with a tiny share of households, so sign-up pauses, surcharges or priority-only access in Indian cities are the likely outcome, as in Nairobi and Lagos.
- Starlink's own S-1 language ("expect to compete increasingly in suburban and urban environments" as capacity grows) is a forward-looking company claim; Moffett's view is the opposite for dense areas. V3 timing (from H2 2026, about five years to roll out per Moffett, new terminals needed per Ookla) suggests little relief for Indian cities in 2026–27.
- The Vietnam 600,000-terminal cap and the reported Indian cap show regulators treating capacity as a national limit. Starlink's own behaviour (freezing whole cities) suggests the real limit is local, cell by cell.

### Gaps
- No opened source gives Starlink's own per-cell capacity (Gbps per hex cell) or its cell size; the figures above are analyst estimates. Mike Puchol's simulation (700 Mbps per cell on a 250 MHz channel) appeared only in search results and was not opened.
- No source says whether the Kenyan or Nigerian sold-out areas reopened by October 2026, apart from Nigeria's priority-tier route.
- The current status of US surcharges comes from trackers and Reddit-sourced reporting, not from SpaceX.

## 5. How incumbents responded

### Takeaway
Incumbents mostly answered with more speed for the same price and with partnerships, not price wars: Safaricom doubled fibre speeds twice (2024 and 2026) and pushed 5G routers into rural Kenya, while Airtel Africa, Vodacom, Globe, Robi and TelOne chose to resell or bundle Starlink. Lobbying for local-presence rules (Safaricom, Telkom Indonesia) and predatory-pricing complaints (Indonesia's satellite association) largely failed.

- US GEO satellite operators: HughesNet fell from 1.22m subscribers (2022) to 739,000 (Q4 2025) and Viasat from 590,000 to an estimated 159,000; both are "going on the defensive" with lower prices — [Ookla, 5 May 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/starlink-hits-new-us-highs).

### Inferences
- The Kenyan sequence is the closest precedent for Jio and Airtel: the dominant operator answered a tiny satellite rival with more megabits at the same price rather than price cuts, then out-added it many times over. Indian fibre and FWA operators would probably respond the same way: protect price, add speed, and push FWA where fibre doesn't reach.
- Jio and Airtel's distribution deals with SpaceX (section 6 context; Ookla, Apr 2026) follow the African pattern (Airtel Africa, Vodacom): incumbents resell Starlink for enterprise, backhaul and remote users and keep the mass market on their own networks.
- Telco-sold direct-to-cell at mobile price points (Globe P99) is a different channel from the dish product and would sit inside Indian operators' bundles, not compete with them.

### Gaps
- No PLDT, Smart, Converge or DITO response specific to Starlink was found; no Grameenphone response; no MTN Nigeria pricing response.
- No evidence was found of an incumbent cutting headline prices specifically because of Starlink; Zimbabwe's Liquid denied it, and the Kenyan responses were speed upgrades.

## 6. How regulators responded

### Takeaway
Regulators shaped Starlink's entry more than demand did: ownership rules (South Africa 30%, Namibia 51%, Thailand), security and lawful-interception terms (Sri Lanka, Pakistan, India), price approvals (Nigeria, Malawi), fee rises (Kenya) and hard caps (Vietnam's 600,000 terminals). Where governments treated satellite broadband as a priority (Bangladesh's NGSO framework in weeks; Vietnam allowing 100% foreign ownership), Starlink launched fast.

### Cited Findings
- Ookla's Affandy Johan: "Regulatory posture, not demand, determines where Starlink operates. Every active APAC market required security provisions, infrastructure commitments, or foreign ownership conditions before licensing was granted" — [Ookla, 12 Apr 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/low-orbit-high-stakes-starlinks-asia-pacific-performance-and-path-to-market).

**South Africa (ICASA)**
- Licensees of I-ECNS and I-ECS licences must be at least 30% owned by historically disadvantaged groups. A final policy direction recognising equity-equivalent investment programmes (EEIPs) was gazetted in December [2025]; in May 2026 ICASA said the Electronic Communications Act must change before EEIPs can count; a June 2026 parliamentary reply confirmed ICASA had "not received any licence applications from Starlink". Starlink has pledged at least $29m for free internet at 5,000 schools and $120m for local infrastructure — [ITWeb, 15 Jun 2026](https://www.itweb.co.za/article/no-starlink-licence-application-in-sa-yet/5yONPvErdlP7XWrb).
- On 12 May 2026 Communications Minister Solly Malatsi said the government would pursue legislative amendments so EEIPs can complement ownership requirements; ICASA's 13 May statement said full alignment "would require a legislative amendment to the current ECA" — [Advanced Television, 14 May 2026](https://www.advanced-television.com/2026/05/14/icasa-says-stouh-africa-needs-new-legislation-for-starlink/).

In December 2024 the NCC spokesman said he had "not been briefed" on the second NGN 75,000 rise — [Nairametrics, 29 Dec 2024](https://nairametrics.com/2024/12/29/starlink-nigeria-increases-subscription-price-again-to-n75000-per-month/).
- The NCC licensed six new ISPs earlier in 2026 — [Space in Africa, 26 Aug 2026](https://spaceinafrica.com/2026/08/26/starlink-narrows-the-gap-to-under-13000-as-nigerias-isp-market-fragments-further-in-q2-2026/).

**Kenya (Communications Authority)**

**Indonesia (Kominfo/Komdigi, KPPU)**

- The regulatory cases most like India's are Pakistan and Sri Lanka (security, interception and shutdown terms) and Vietnam (a numerical cap). In all three, terms took a year or more to settle.
- Price-approval regimes (Nigeria, Malawi) slowed Starlink's attempts to pass on currency losses but did not stop rises; if TRAI-style oversight applied to Starlink's tariffs in India, the Nigerian sequence (rise, pushback, reversal, re-rise within months) is the precedent.

### Gaps
- No Komdigi primary document on Starlink's Indonesian conditions was opened; Indonesian price regulation was only "considered" in June 2024 and its outcome is unknown.
- No opened source confirms what, if anything, regulators did about the 2025–2026 sold-out episodes in Kenya or Nigeria.
- Zambia's and Zimbabwe's licensing terms (Zimbabwe's local partner IMC is mentioned only in passing by Zimpricecheck) were not researched in depth.

## 7. Creative tactics and which ones worked

### Takeaway
The tactics that measurably moved numbers were cheaper entry hardware and financing (Kenya's $15 rental drove a 104.7% quarterly jump in satellite subscriptions), cheaper deprioritised tiers (Lite, 50 GB plans), and riding disasters with free service (Jamaica). Telco partnerships and community sharing extend reach without dishes. Price cuts and rentals also brought congestion, and several were later withdrawn (Kenya rental discontinued, Malaysia Lite removed).

### Cited Findings
- Hardware rental: Kenya's KES 1,950/month rental (August 2024) — [Connecting Africa, 22 Aug 2024](https://www.connectingafrica.com/connectivity/kenyans-can-now-rent-starlink-kits-for-15-per-month); satellite subscriptions then rose 104.7% in a quarter, "primarily due to Starlink's campaign that introduced affordable rental options" — [Connecting Africa, 28 Jan 2025](https://www.connectingafrica.com/connectivity/starlink-doubles-market-share-in-kenya-launches-ground-station); the rental has since been discontinued, as far as Ookla understands — [Ookla, 15 Jun 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/starlink-ssa-q1-2026). - Instalments: Kenya's six-month Mini instalment plan (January 2026), introduced as "uptake has recently shown volatility" — [Business Daily, 15 Jan 2026](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/technology/starlink-cuts-upfront-cost-with-instalment-payments-plan-5327602); Brazil sells kits in instalments through partner stores — [Exame, 15 Jun 2026](https://exame.com/tecnologia/examelab/starlink-no-brasil-vale-a-pena-em-2026-velocidade-estabilidade-e-preco-real/).

- Free disaster service: "When a community is impacted, Starlink provides free service to all customers in the affected area ... as well as to new customers who activate a kit for the first time during the crisis" — [Starlink Progress Report 2025](https://starlink.com/public-files/starlinkProgressReport_2025.pdf); in Jamaica, many who switched kept the service, making it "effectively ... a three-player broadband market" — [Ookla, 5 Aug 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/jamaica-starlink-data-speeds-drop-after-hurricane-melissa).
- Ground infrastructure as a selling point: the Nairobi PoP cut latency from 120 ms to 26 ms (local media cited by Connecting Africa) — [Connecting Africa, 28 Jan 2025](https://www.connectingafrica.com/connectivity/starlink-doubles-market-share-in-kenya-launches-ground-station).
- Public pressure through Musk's posts: on 7 March 2025 Musk posted "Starlink is not allowed to operate in South Africa, because I'm not black"; Starlink has neither complied with the ownership rule nor applied for a licence — [tech.africa, 9 Mar 2025, updated 7 Feb 2026](https://tech.africa/starlink-black-south-africa/). In Brazil, Starlink publicly called the asset freeze "illegal" but complied with the X block within a day — [Fortune/Bloomberg, 4 Sep 2024](https://fortune.com/2024/09/04/elon-musk-brazil-starlink-block-x).

### Inferences
- What worked: lowering the upfront cost (rental, instalments, Mini, promotions) produced the biggest bursts of sign-ups, but in Kenya it also filled Nairobi's capacity within months. Cheap entry and good speed in dense areas did not coexist.
- What didn't work: public pressure through Musk's posts. South Africa remains unlicensed 19 months after the March 2025 post, and Brazil's confrontation ended in compliance.
- Partnerships and community passes are Starlink's way to reach price-sensitive users without one dish per home. In India the analogous route is reselling through Jio and Airtel, and shared village or apartment connections, rather than mass individual subscriptions.

### Gaps
- No uptake data was found for Lite plans, Mini instalments or Starlink for Communities.
- Whether Starlink for Communities operates in any emerging market, and at what price, is not stated in the source opened.
- UNVERIFIED: Indonesian reports that Starlink was used for "RT/RW Net" neighbourhood resale and that a fair-use policy followed (headline seen in search results; page could not be fetched).

## 8. Where Starlink stalled, retreated or was blocked

### Takeaway
Starlink has stalled mainly on ownership and security rules (South Africa, Namibia, Thailand, Pakistan, a long wait in India) and retreated tactically on price and capacity (Malawi sales pause, Nigeria's 2024 price reversal, sign-up freezes, Kenya's rental withdrawn, Malaysia's Lite removed). Where it launched in poor, fibre-served markets at about $50, uptake was thin (Bangladesh).

- Bangladesh: 3,469 customers after eight months; resellers struggling; general users "are concentrated in areas with fibre coverage" (Shah Ahmedul Kabir, Bangladesh Satellite Company) — [Daily Star, 31 Mar 2026](https://www.thedailystar.net/business/economy/news/starlink-gains-just-3500-users-after-8-months-bangladesh-4139626).

- Malaysia: unique samples −27.2% as fibre expanded under the MYR 21bn JENDELA programme (target 7.5m premises by end-2025), leaving Starlink "a narrowing addressable market in peninsular coverage areas" — [Ookla, 12 Apr 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/low-orbit-high-stakes-starlinks-asia-pacific-performance-and-path-to-market); Lite later withdrawn — [Lowyat, 23 Jun 2026](https://www.lowyat.net/2026/396556/starlink-malaysia-removes-residential-lite-plan/).
- Kenya: kit price cuts and rentals in 2024; the rental later discontinued; Mini instalments added in 2026 amid "volatility"; repeated sign-up freezes (sections 1 and 3). Starlink grew only 2,717 subscribers in Q1 2026 — [TechCabal, 18 Jun 2026](https://techcabal.com/2026/06/18/starlink-grabs-headlines-but-safaricom-keeps-winning-broadband-users/).
- Indonesia: nationwide pause in July 2025 (reopened within weeks) — [Antara, 22 Jul 2025](https://www.antaranews.com/berita/4983973/kemkomdigi-sebut-starlink-mulai-buka-layanan-aktivasi-pelanggan-baru). A July 2026 article reports hundreds of customers complaining of bills of up to Rp 27m, without explaining the cause — [Bernas, 28 Jul 2026](https://bernas.id/nasional/news/tagihan-starlink-membengkak-hingga-rp-27-juta-pelanggan-keluhkan-biaya-2026-07-28) (thin sourcing; treat as unexplained).
- Brazil: no retreat, but the 2024 asset freeze and the X-block confrontation, which ended in compliance — [Fortune/Bloomberg, 4 Sep 2024](https://fortune.com/2024/09/04/elon-musk-brazil-starlink-block-x).

### Inferences
- Fibre is what stops Starlink: in Malaysia, the Philippines' cities and Bangladesh, cheaper fibre led to shrinking or thin Starlink use, while Starlink thrived where fixed options are weak (Nigeria, rural Brazil, Jamaica after the storm). India's urban fibre and FWA footprint puts most Indian cities in the first group.
- Starlink rarely cuts monthly prices to win share in poor markets; it cuts hardware cost and adds cheaper, deprioritised tiers instead. A sustained cut of Indian monthly prices towards Jio-like levels has no precedent in this evidence.

### Gaps
- No full list of countries where Starlink is unlicensed or banned was opened; Cameroon, Chad and others were not researched.
- No evidence was found of Starlink exiting a market after launch.

## 9. Surprises: facts that contradict "Starlink is premium-only" or "it always loses to cheap mobile data"

### Takeaway
In several low-income markets Starlink is cheaper or far better value than local fixed broadband (Bhutan, Zimbabwe, Ghana) and has become a top-two ISP (Nigeria) or a top net-adder (Brazil), often in cities rather than villages. Through telcos it is now sold at mobile price points (Globe's P99). The counter-evidence is equally strong: tiny shares wherever cheap fibre exists, and Ookla's view that it does not threaten mobile operators in Africa.

### Cited Findings
- Bhutan: Starlink's Nu 4,200 (≈ ₹4,200) unlimited plan at up to 110 Mbps compares with incumbents' ~Nu 4,000 leased lines capped at 8 Mbps and ~Nu 50,000 a month for 100 Mbps unlimited; BICMA says Starlink's Bhutan tariffs are lower than its global prices — [BBS, 5 Dec 2024](https://www.bbs.bt/?p=221487).
- Ghana and Zimbabwe: "Starlink's monthly plans are cheaper than those provided by ISPs" (fibre is cheaper in Nigeria, Kenya and Rwanda) — [Ookla, 15 Jun 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/starlink-ssa-q1-2026). In Zimbabwe at launch, local ISPs averaged about US$500 for unlimited internet (as reported; no operator named, and Liquid's own 2024 uncapped fibre tiers were $99–169) against Starlink's US$30 — [Connecting Africa, 4 Nov 2024](https://www.connectingafrica.com/connectivity/starlink-s-entry-into-zimbabwe-lowers-internet-tariffs).
- Shared dishes bring the cost to US$10 a household a month in a Harare low-income suburb — [MyZimbabwe, 7 May 2026](https://www.myzimbabwe.co.zw/technology/187394-us10-per-household-for-starlink-in-harare-ghetto-starlink-shakes-internet-market-zim-isps-forced-to-reduce-prices.html).
- Nigeria: second-largest ISP with 98,642 subscribers, still growing after a 50% price rise — [Space in Africa, 26 Aug 2026](https://spaceinafrica.com/2026/08/26/starlink-narrows-the-gap-to-under-13000-as-nigerias-isp-market-fragments-further-in-q2-2026/).
- Brazil: more Starlink subscriptions in highly urbanised areas (347,990) than in rural ones (213,291) in May 2026; second in net additions in January–May 2026, ahead of Claro — [Ookla, 29 Jul 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/satellites-at-scale-a-performance-audit-of-starlink-in-brazil).
- Africa: "While satellite coverage is often cited as a solution for the rural connectivity gap, the technology is primarily used as an alternative to high-speed terrestrial broadband service" — [Ookla, 15 Jun 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/starlink-ssa-q1-2026). Starlink's early Kenyan demand surged in urban centres — [TechCabal, 18 Apr 2025](https://techcabal.com/2025/04/18/safaricom-5g-router-versus-starlink-push-rural-kenya/).
- Indonesia was a top-five Starlink market by Speedtest samples in Q3 2025 — [Ookla, 4 Feb 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/2025-global-satellite-broadband-performance-report) — even though its entry plan cost more than twice the typical household internet spend — [Ookla, 12 Apr 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/low-orbit-high-stakes-starlinks-asia-pacific-performance-and-path-to-market).
- Customers who switched after a disaster mostly stayed: Flow lost 57,000 Jamaican residential subscribers and won back about a thousand — [Ookla, 5 Aug 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/jamaica-starlink-data-speeds-drop-after-hurricane-melissa).
- Mobile price points: Globe sells Starlink satellite-to-mobile from P99 (≈ $1.6) and includes it free on mid-tier postpaid — [GMA News, 8 Jul 2026](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/money/companies/994149/globe-sets-p99-entry-price-for-starlink-satellite-to-mobile-service/story/).
- Starlink's US entry price has at times been below its African target: $39 a month (March 2026 promotion) against an Africa target of about $50 — [Notebookcheck, 4 Mar 2026](https://www.notebookcheck.net/SpaceX-cuts-Starlink-residential-plan-price-to-39-month-ahead-of-IPO.1242274.0.html); [Techzim, 30 Oct 2024](https://www.techzim.co.zw/2024/10/starlink-suspends-new-kit-sales-and-subscriptions-in-malawi-due-to-currency-pricing-and-regulatory-issues-similar-problems-in-nigeria/). In the UK, its £35 100 Mbps plan is slightly cheaper than a similar BT offer over two years, according to New Street Research (as cited by Ookla's Mike Dano), who adds that Starlink's "pricing has been falling even as its speeds have been rising" — [Ookla, 4 Feb 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/2025-global-satellite-broadband-performance-report).
- SpaceX says it is deliberately trading ARPU for volume: "rather than prioritizing increases in ARPU", it expects ARPU to keep falling as it adds lower-priced plans — [SpaceX S-1, 20 May 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026036936/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm).
- Counter-evidence: Bangladesh's 3,469 users at ten times local broadband prices ([Daily Star, 31 Mar 2026](https://www.thedailystar.net/business/economy/news/starlink-gains-just-3500-users-after-8-months-bangladesh-4139626)); Kenya's 1.0% share ([CA Q4 FY2025/26](https://www.ca.go.ke/sites/default/files/2026-09/Sector%20Statistics%20Report%20Q4%202025-2026.pdf)); satellite in 2.2% of connected Philippine households ([Ookla, 12 Apr 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/low-orbit-high-stakes-starlinks-asia-pacific-performance-and-path-to-market)); and Ookla's Karim Yaici: satellite communications "do not threaten mobile operators because of high hardware costs, D2D device requirements, and poor indoor coverage" ([Ookla, 15 Jun 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/starlink-ssa-q1-2026)).

### Inferences
- "Premium-only" holds where fibre is cheap and widespread, but fails where fixed broadband is scarce, expensive or unreliable. In those places Starlink can be the cheaper option per megabit and wins urban as well as rural users. India's question is how much of its demand sits in those gaps (fibre-dark towns, villages, highways, islands, disaster-hit areas) rather than in fibre-served cities.
- Starlink did not beat cheap mobile data head-on anywhere in this evidence. It reached mobile price points only by selling through mobile operators (Globe, Airtel Africa), which is the channel Jio and Airtel already control in India.

### Gaps
- No market was found where Starlink's dish service undercuts mainstream mobile data on price per GB; I found no per-GB comparison in any opened source.
- Price comparisons with local ISPs in Ghana and Zimbabwe come from Ookla's summary statement and a 2024 article; no like-for-like 2026 tariff tables were opened.
