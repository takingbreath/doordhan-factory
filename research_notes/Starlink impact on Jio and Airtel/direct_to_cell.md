# Starlink direct-to-cell ("Starlink Mobile") and whether it can reach Jio's mobile business

Researched Sunday 18 Oct 2026 (IST). Conventions: "(context)" marks a source dated before 11 Oct 2026, so more than 7 days old. "UNVERIFIED" means I could not confirm the item on a page I opened. Rupee conversions are my own arithmetic, using the rate implied in Jio's own RHP (₹4,800 ≈ US$55, so about ₹87 per US$). Facts the brief supplied are used as given: 77.08% of Jio's operating revenue is prepaid mobile; Jio's ARPU is ₹214 a month for 42.3 GB (about ₹7.74/GB; IndMoney, from the DRHP); SpaceX listed in June 2026; Starlink, Jio Satellite Communications and Eutelsat OneWeb hold Indian satcom licences and all await security clearance and spectrum (Business Standard, 17 Oct 2026).

## 1. What Starlink Mobile is and can do as of October 2026: satellite generations, spectrum, capabilities, launch status

### Takeaway
"Starlink Mobile" is SpaceX's new name for Direct to Cell. Today it is a first-generation service of about 650 "V1 Mobile" satellites. It sells only through partner mobile operators and offers texting, light app data and over-the-top voice (WhatsApp-type calls), outdoors where the sky is visible. SpaceX now owns or is buying dedicated spectrum: 65 MHz from EchoStar for about $19.6B, plus 800 MHz low-band from Grain, announced 8 Oct 2026. It has FCC approval (6 Oct 2026) for up to 15,000 next-generation D2D satellites, and says it will start flying second-generation satellites in 2027 and offer a US carrier-style service from the end of 2027. I found no standalone Starlink phone plan in any market.

### Cited Findings
**What the product is (SpaceX's own filing, older than 7 days)**
- SpaceX's S-1 defines Starlink Mobile as "a service that provides cellular connectivity directly to everyday smartphones via satellites, supplementing terrestrial networks and substantially reducing mobile dead zones." — [SpaceX Form S-1, SEC, filed 20 May 2026 (context)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026036936/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm)
- Scale as of 31 Mar 2026: about 650 V1 Mobile satellites served about 7.4 million monthly unique devices across about 30 countries, through about 30 MNO partners on six continents. Those partners cover an area home to about 1.9 billion people. SpaceX began deploying the mobile constellation in January 2024. — [SpaceX S-1, 20 May 2026 (context)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026036936/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm)
- V1 capabilities: "light data, text messaging (SMS), and over-the-top voice services (e.g., WhatsApp and FaceTime)." Consumer focus is "initially... messaging for consumer subscribers in areas with limited or no terrestrial coverage, with a roadmap to support broader data, voice and IoT services." Elsewhere the S-1 says Starlink Mobile "is already commercially available for messaging in select markets." — [SpaceX S-1 (context)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026036936/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm)
- Business model: "We charge MNOs either a fixed fee or a per-mobile user fee-based amount, which is typically passed through to the customer via the carrier as an 'add-on' feature." SpaceX also earns revenue-sharing from MNO partners. Starlink Mobile revenue is reported inside "Enterprise & Government" revenue and is not broken out. — [SpaceX S-1 (context)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026036936/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm)
- Regulatory reach of Gen1: fully licensed in the US. Commercial Gen1 approvals (with at least one MNO partner) in "the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Japan, and Australia, as well as in several additional countries." Partnerships in over 30 countries "do not, by themselves, provide the regulatory approvals." — [SpaceX S-1 (context)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026036936/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm)
- Naming: "Starlink Mobile is the renamed direct-to-cell capability, separate from the broadband capability." — [Via Satellite, 5 Aug 2026 (context)](https://www.satellitetoday.com/finance/2026/08/05/spacex-outlines-starlink-mobile-plans-in-first-earnings-report/)

**Satellite generations (V1 Mobile, V2 Mobile, and the confusingly named V3 broadband)**
- On the Q2 2026 call (4 Aug 2026), SpaceX President Gwynne Shotwell said: "We're going to start flying the next-generation Starlink mobile satellite, which is confusing. It's actually the second version as opposed to the V3, which is the broadband... We're going to start flying those next year." — [Motley Fool transcript, 11 Aug 2026 (context)](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/11/spacex-spcx-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)
- S-1 plan: deploy "next-generation mobile V2 Mobile satellites in 2027." Starship is expected to carry "approximately 50 mobile satellites per launch." Gen2 aims at "broadband data and IoT connectivity" and "5G connectivity." V3 is the broadband satellite, "designed to offer one Tbps of downlink capacity per satellite," with up to 60 per Starship launch. — [SpaceX S-1 (context)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026036936/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm)
- Shotwell on scale: today's system "uses about 5 megahertz of bandwidth, and we operate that through local telco providers." With 65 MHz of EchoStar spectrum and "probably 10x the number of satellites," the next generation would be "100x better. 10 x 10 better than what we've got right now." — [Motley Fool transcript, 11 Aug 2026 (context)](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/11/spacex-spcx-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/); same quotes in [Via Satellite, 5 Aug 2026 (context)](https://www.satellitetoday.com/finance/2026/08/05/spacex-outlines-starlink-mobile-plans-in-first-earnings-report/)
- Ookla's account: SpaceX describes the first-generation constellation of about 650 D2D satellites as complete, with second-generation satellites "targeted for 2027 carrying roughly 20 times the throughput." — [Ookla Research, reprinted by Communications Today, 29 Jul 2026 (context)](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/?p=417644)
- On 6 Oct 2026 the FCC authorised SpaceX to deploy 15,000 NGSO satellites for D2D in Very Low Earth Orbit (nine shells, 326–335 km). They provide supplemental coverage from space (SCS) and MSS in the US, and MSS and direct-to-cell outside the US. The FCC deferred some spectrum requests, including MSS in 2020–2025 MHz. SpaceX's authority in the EchoStar spectrum depends on the deal's second step, expected on or about 30 Nov 2027. — [Via Satellite, 7 Oct 2026 (context)](https://www.satellitetoday.com/connectivity/2026/10/07/fcc-grants-spacex-approval-for-15000-direct-to-device-satellites-in-mss-spectrum/)
- Speed claim for V2: Notebookcheck reports that V2 DTC satellites "will enable 5G/LTE speeds of up to 150 Mbps." The article gives no primary source for this, so it is UNVERIFIED. — [Notebookcheck, c. Aug 2026, date not shown (context)](https://www.notebookcheck.net/SpaceX-tips-Starlink-Mobile-carrier-launch-date-and-coverage-with-dish-base-stations.1360525.0.html)

**Spectrum holdings**
- EchoStar deal, signed 7 Sep 2025: initial consideration $17.0B for 50 MHz of AWS-4 and H-Block (2000–2020, 2180–2200, 1915–1920 and 1995–2000 MHz) plus international authorisations and ITU priorities. An amendment on 5 Nov 2025 added up to 15 MHz of unpaired AWS-3 for $2.6B more. The total is about $19.6B: about $11.1B in SpaceX stock and up to $8.5B to pay off EchoStar debt. SpaceX will also lend the trust about $3.0B for EchoStar debt service through Nov 2028. The FCC approved the transfer on 12 May 2026, and final closing is expected on or about 30 Nov 2027. — [SpaceX S-1 (context)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026036936/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm). Note on figures: many outlets still cite "$17 billion," the September 2025 headline; the S-1 total is $19.6B.
- Handset limits on that spectrum, per the S-1: the EchoStar frequencies are standardised for terrestrial 5G (3GPP n66/n70), "But the 5G NR-NTN bands for these same frequencies, such as n252 and n256, are not currently supported by RF front-end hardware... in any commercially available mobile devices." Also: "We do not have direct contractual arrangements with handset manufacturers." Internationally, "Until device manufacturers incorporate support for our international spectrum bands into future handsets, we will be unable to offer 5G-like direct-to-consumer service on our own international spectrum." The near-term international strategy therefore depends on "MNO spectrum partnerships on a market-by-market basis." — [SpaceX S-1 (context)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026036936/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm)
- Gen2 abroad: SpaceX has "filed applications in nearly every country in which we intend to operate our Gen2 service, and approvals have been granted in a limited number." It also warns that "some countries have signaled... they are considering ignoring or diminishing ITU priority," including weighing "whether the operator originates from the country." The S-1 names no countries. — [SpaceX S-1 (context)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026036936/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm)
- New low-band deal, 8 Oct 2026: SpaceX will acquire 14 MHz of paired spectrum in the 800 MHz band from Grain Management. Financial terms were not disclosed, and the deal needs FCC approval. Grain had bought the block from T-Mobile; that deal closed in August for $2.9B cash plus spectrum. SpaceX's statement: the low band "will provide a coverage layer that ensures Starlink Mobile's signal penetrates through obstacles, such as walls, and can provide service to customers' devices even when they are in buildings." SpaceX says most existing phones already support the band, and that Starlink Mobile will be "the first network operator to deploy both satellite and terrestrial spectrum." — [Via Satellite, 8 Oct 2026 (context)](https://www.satellitetoday.com/connectivity/2026/10/08/spacex-moves-to-acquire-nationwide-low-band-spectrum-for-starlink-mobile/)
- Grain deal price conflict: SatNews says "Financial media reported the transaction value at approximately $8 billion in cash, though official corporate filings did not formally disclose" terms. It quotes Elon Musk: "This is the last critical piece of the spectrum puzzle needed for SpaceX to provide complete phone coverage in America." — [SatNews, 11 Oct 2026](https://satnews.com/2026/10/11/spacex-acquires-grain-managements-800-mhz-spectrum-portfolio-to-launch-standalone-starlink-mobile-carrier/). MarketBeat also cites "reports indicating a purchase price near $8 billion." — [MarketBeat, c. 16 Oct 2026](https://www.marketbeat.com/articles/spacexs-spectrum-deal-hits-telecom-stocks-but-gives-tower-reits-a-new-opportunity/). SpaceX and Grain have disclosed no price.

**Plan to be a full US carrier (launch status)**
- SpaceX's own post on X, 8 Oct 2026: "We announced an agreement to acquire a nationwide low-band spectrum license portfolio that will pave the way for @Starlink to become a major mobile carrier in the US." — [SpaceX on X, 8 Oct 2026 (context); read through X's embed endpoint](https://x.com/SpaceX/status/2108291133025698301)
- Shotwell named AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile ("roughly between them, $600 billion a year"): "I anticipate us to be able to acquire quite a few of their customers because I think our service will be better... We will start to fly the satellites next year, and we will start providing service end of next year." — [Motley Fool transcript, 11 Aug 2026 (context)](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/11/spacex-spcx-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)
- On building ground infrastructure, Shotwell said: "you could put a base station, a cellular base station on the... gear that holds a Starlink broadband [dish]... little femtocells around the country." She declined to give capex. Musk said SpaceX could deploy "a large number of sort of small stations. Essentially, they're really just Starlink dishes that also provide connectivity in the mobile spectrum bands." — [Motley Fool transcript (context)](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/11/spacex-spcx-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)
- FindCheapBroadband: "SpaceX has not announced an exact standalone Starlink Mobile retail launch date for either country [US or UK]." Its late-2027 US window is its own estimate. — [FindCheapBroadband, checked 10 Oct 2026 (context; consumer guide, lower tier)](https://findcheapbroadband.com/blog/when-will-starlink-mobile-launch-us-uk/)

**Check of the viral reel ("Elon Musk has entered the mobile telecom network industry" with "Starlink Mobile")**
- True: "Starlink Mobile" exists and is live through about 30 carriers (S-1 above). SpaceX publicly said on 8 Oct 2026 that it intends to become "a major mobile carrier in the US" (X post above). Shotwell said service from next-generation satellites starts "end of next year," meaning end-2027 (transcript above).
- Not true yet: no country has a standalone Starlink-branded SIM or phone plan. Every live service is sold by a partner operator as an add-on or inclusion (see Q2). India has no direct-to-device framework at all (see Q4).

### Inferences
- For Indian viewers, the accurate framing is "a coverage add-on sold through telcos today; a US carrier ambition for late 2027." It is not "a new Indian mobile network."
- The binding constraints are spectrum and handsets as much as satellites. The S-1 concedes that current phones do not support SpaceX's own 2 GHz NTN bands, and that outside the US it must rent spectrum from local operators market by market. In India the near-term route therefore runs through an Indian telco partner.
- The 800 MHz Grain deal is a US-only asset: an FCC licence for US airwaves. It does nothing for India.

### Gaps
- SpaceX has not disclosed Starlink Mobile users after 31 Mar 2026 (7.4M monthly unique devices) in the sources I opened. A widely shared blog figure of "16 million unique users" is UNVERIFIED and conflicts with the S-1.
- The exact date of the rebrand from "Direct to Cell" to "Starlink Mobile" was not found. The S-1 of 20 May 2026 already uses the new name.
- A blog (5gworldpro, 22 Jul 2026) attributes to Starlink VP Mike Nicolls at MWC a goal of about 1,200 V2 satellites, with contiguous global coverage within six months of starting deployment. UNVERIFIED; no primary source found.

## 2. Partners and consumer prices: T-Mobile T-Satellite, other carriers, standalone Starlink plans

### Takeaway
Carriers sell Starlink Mobile as a cheap add-on or give it away free. T-Mobile charges US$10 a month, O2 UK £3, and Globe in the Philippines from PHP 99 (about US$1.65). It is free with top plans at T-Mobile and Rogers, free messaging at all three Japanese operators, and free trials at Airtel Africa and Banglalink. SpaceX sells no standalone phone plan, and the US carriers have refused to host a Starlink MVNO.

## 3. Measured performance and limits: coverage, speeds, capacity per beam, line of sight

### Takeaway
Independent data show a narrowband, outdoor, coverage-gap service. An academic measurement study (an arXiv preprint) estimated about 3 Mbps per beam outdoors. One user measured 1.7 Mbps down, about 200 kbps up and over 200 ms latency. Texts take 5–30 seconds. Coverage is weaker near the equator (relevant to South Asia), and usage is far lower than carriers expected.

### Cited Findings
- Crowdsourced measurement study (Universidad Politécnica de Madrid authors; US data Oct 2024–Jul 2025): median RSRP was 24 dB lower than terrestrial networks. "We estimate the expected performance of the announced DS2D mobile data service to be around 3 Mbps per beam in outdoor conditions," possibly expandable "up to 18 Mbps in the future, depending on key regulatory and business decisions." — [arXiv 2506.00283, v8 dated 23 Nov 2025 (context)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00283). BGR summarises the same research as "4 Mbps" rising "closer to 12 Mbps," probably from an earlier version; the arXiv abstract is the primary source.
- Single-user benchmark off Kauai, Hawaii: "a downlink of 1.7Mbps, an uplink of about 200kbps, and latency of more than 200ms." — [BGR, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.bgr.com/2283260/starlink-direct-to-cell-phone-network-explained/)
- ISPreview describes the platform as "a fairly basic (c.5Mbps) and app-specific 4G remote roaming style service." Only about 650 of about 11,130 Starlink satellites are D2C. — [ISPreview, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.ispreview.co.uk/index.php/2026/10/opensignal-map-shows-where-uk-mobiles-connect-to-starlinks-d2d-satellites.html)
- Field test (Backpacker, Moto Edge 2024): messages sent "in a matter of 5 to 10 seconds" under open sky, "15 to 30 seconds" under tree cover or in a car. Non-T-Mobile users "need an unlocked phone and an empty secondary eSIM." — [Backpacker, 7 Jan 2026 (context)](https://www.backpacker.com/gear/t-satellite-backpacking-test/)
- Ookla signal data, Jan 2025–Jun 2026: "Only 15.5% of Philippine signal scans reached −118 dBm or better... just over half the share recorded in Australia, Japan, and New Zealand," because Starlink's D2D satellites fly in 43° and 53° inclined orbits "that concentrate coverage at mid-latitudes and thin it toward the equator" (Manila sits at 14°N). Today's services "run on narrow slices of the host operators' mobile spectrum, shared by every user in a satellite's beam." — [Ookla via Communications Today, 29 Jul 2026 (context)](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/?p=417644)
- Ookla usage data: detected users averaged 25.7 scans a month in Australia and 23.0 in New Zealand, against about 4 in the UK and 29 in Canada. Japan's detected base in June 2026 was about half its July 2025 level. Use rises with outdoor seasons and disasters: Globe opened free access to more than 700,000 subscribers after the 8 Jun 2026 Mindanao earthquake, and more than 150,000 connected. — [Ookla via Communications Today (context)](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/?p=417644)
- Opensignal's D2D map: every point is a phone that connected since Jan 2026. "A connection does not necessarily mean a message or data was sent." The map is updated monthly. — [ISPreview, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.ispreview.co.uk/index.php/2026/10/opensignal-map-shows-where-uk-mobiles-connect-to-starlinks-d2d-satellites.html)
- Operator usage evidence:
  - T-Mobile CEO Gopalan: "Most of the usage we're seeing is in national parks... we're seeing a lot less usage than we were originally thinking." T-Mobile does not disclose T-Satellite customer numbers. — [Light Reading, 29 Apr 2026 (context)](https://lightreading.com/satellite/starlink-d2d-use-is-less-than-expected-says-t-mobile)
  - AT&T's Stankey says satellite reaches "about 2% of traffic it can't cover." — [Via Satellite, 24 Jul 2026 (context)](https://www.satellitetoday.com/connectivity/2026/07/24/telco-ceos-shut-down-the-idea-of-satellite-mvno-say-leo-isnt-a-threat-to-broadband/)
- Line of sight: T-Mobile and Rogers both limit the service to outdoor areas "where you can see the sky." — [T-Mobile](https://www.t-mobile.com/coverage/satellite-phone-service); [Rogers](https://www.rogers.com/satellite) (both accessed 18 Oct 2026). SpaceX's stated reason for the 800 MHz purchase is to get signals "through obstacles, such as walls." — [Via Satellite, 8 Oct 2026 (context)](https://www.satellitetoday.com/connectivity/2026/10/08/spacex-moves-to-acquire-nationwide-low-band-spectrum-for-starlink-mobile/)
- SpaceX's own risk disclosure: Starlink services "may be less competitive in certain markets, including dense urban areas where terrestrial fiber and wireless networks may offer higher capacity, lower cost, or more consistent performance." — [SpaceX S-1 (context)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026036936/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm)

### Inferences
- Rough capacity arithmetic, my own, using the study's 3 Mbps per beam and Jio's 42.3 GB per user per month. A 3 Mbps beam running flat out 24x7 for 30 days carries about 972 GB, roughly what 23 average Jio users consume. This is an upper bound: it ignores overheads, and actual demand is uneven. It shows why today's D2C cannot substitute for Indian prepaid data volumes. Shotwell's claimed "100x" for Gen2 is a system-level figure (10x satellites times more spectrum), not per user.
- Southern India lies at tropical latitudes comparable to the Philippines (Ookla cites Manila at 14°N), so the geometry finding (weaker signal toward the equator) would probably apply there until Gen2 arrives. This is an inference; Ookla did not measure India, where the service is not permitted.

### Gaps
- I found no PCMag, The Verge or Light Reading speed test of T-Satellite data. Ookla's APAC report measures signal strength and usage, not throughput. No independent per-beam capacity figure for V2 exists, because V2 is not in orbit.

## 4. India: do the rules allow satellite D2D, which bands, has Starlink applied, and what are Indian players doing

### Takeaway

## 7. Surprises: facts that contradict "Starlink is only a dish product" or "Starlink will replace your SIM"

### Takeaway
Both popular framings are wrong. Starlink already reaches millions of ordinary phones through carriers, including next door in Bangladesh and through Airtel's own African business, and is buying spectrum to become a US carrier. But every live service still rides on a telco's SIM, works only outdoors, and carries a few Mbps. India has no rules allowing it yet.

**Against "Starlink will replace your SIM"**
- Every live service needs a partner operator's SIM or eSIM (T-Mobile for non-members needs "an empty secondary eSIM"; Banglalink needs a Banglalink SIM). It works outdoors "where you can see the sky," for text and select apps. — [Backpacker, 7 Jan 2026 (context)](https://www.backpacker.com/gear/t-satellite-backpacking-test/); [T-Mobile, accessed 18 Oct 2026](https://www.t-mobile.com/coverage/satellite-phone-service); [Via Satellite, 9 Oct 2026 (context)](https://www.satellitetoday.com/connectivity/2026/10/09/veons-banglalink-to-launch-starlink-mobile-in-bangladesh/)
- Measured capacity is about 3 Mbps per beam outdoors. — [arXiv 2506.00283 (context)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00283)
- T-Mobile reports "a lot less usage than we were originally thinking." — [Light Reading, 29 Apr 2026 (context)](https://lightreading.com/satellite/starlink-d2d-use-is-less-than-expected-says-t-mobile)
- SpaceX's own filing says current phones do not support its 2 GHz NTN bands. Abroad it cannot offer "5G-like direct-to-consumer service on our own international spectrum" until handsets change, and it may be "less competitive in... dense urban areas." — [SpaceX S-1 (context)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026036936/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm)
- In India, D2D is "not permitted... due to the absence of a regulatory framework." Starlink's own TRAI filing proposes D2D over telcos' IMT spectrum with open access for "all suitable carriers." Starlink's India VP says "Starlink isn't here to replace India's terrestrial networks." — [TelecomTalk, 14 Nov 2025 (context)](https://telecomtalk.info/dot-trai-direct-to-device-satellite-service/1001639/); [Starlink India submission to TRAI, 27 May 2026 (context)](https://trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-05/Starlink_27052026.pdf); [TelecomTalk, 8 Oct 2026 (context)](https://telecomtalk.info/imc2026-starlink-setup-over20-gateway-sites-india/1012624/)
- Starlink cannot yet launch even its dish service in India: security clearance and spectrum are pending (the brief, citing Business Standard, 17 Oct 2026). MHA reportedly froze clearances in June 2026. — [MediaNama, 20 Aug 2026 (context)](https://www.medianama.com/2026/08/223-starlink-india-satellite-network/)

### Inferences
- A fair one-line summary of the evidence: Starlink is already a phone network in about 30 countries, but only as a backup layer inside your existing telco's plan. The version that could compete with a SIM is a US plan for late 2027, and India has not yet written the rules that would allow even the backup version.

### Gaps
- No public data show how many Indian phones could technically attach to Starlink V1 satellites, which work with LTE handsets, if the service were permitted.
