## 1. Bharti Airtel's latest reported quarter (Q1 FY27, Apr–Jun 2026): mobile, postpaid, 4G/5G, homes, enterprise, premiumisation, and the Airtel–Jio ARPU gap

### Takeaway
Airtel's latest reported quarter as of 18 Oct 2026 is Q1 FY27 (quarter ended 30 Jun 2026, reported 4 Aug 2026). Mobile ARPU was ₹264 against Jio's ₹215.6, so Airtel earns about ₹48 (22%) more per user. Airtel had 30.0 million postpaid users (8% of 376.5 million mobile customers), 14.7 million Homes customers and ₹5,665 crore of Airtel Business revenue. Its stated strategy is premiumisation through postpaid, plus "fibre first" in about 400 cities, with fixed wireless (FWA) used only where fibre can't reach.

### Inferences
- Airtel's premium franchise sits in mobile postpaid (30 million users, about 8% of its base) and urban fibre homes. Neither is a natural target for a rooftop dish: satellite doesn't replace a phone line, and in about 400 fibre-served cities 1 Gbps fibre is cheaper (see section 5).
- Airtel is deliberately retreating from cheap FWA and from homes outside fibre reach. That shrinks its overlap with the segment where satellite looks most like a substitute: rural and semi-urban homes beyond fibre.
- Airtel's average Homes bill (₹523) is falling. Its home base is mid-market on average, not a premium base that Starlink at ₹3,000 or more could skim at scale.

At end-Aug 2026 India had 1,101 million broadband connections. Only 48.6 million were wired and 19.3 million fixed-wireless, so about 68 million fixed connections in all. Jio leads wired (14.81 million), 5G FWA (9.42 million) and UBR FWA, where it is the only reporter (5.37 million). Airtel has 11.44 million wired and 4.03 million 5G FWA. About 9,000 villages still have no mobile signal at all.

### Inferences
- Computed from Analysys Mason's ~20% penetration and ~60 million home connections: India has about 300 million households, and **about 240 million (~80%) have no fixed broadband**. In rural India about 94% have none.
- The group with no mobile signal at all (about 9,000 villages, ~1.4%) is small in household terms. The much larger pool for satellite is rural and semi-urban homes that have 4G or 5G but no fibre. There the incumbent home product is FWA, which is majority-rural for 5G and dominated by Jio.
- Airtel's fixed-line base is about 99% urban (1.3% rural). Jio's is 7.4% rural and BSNL's 32.9%. In fixed lines, rural exposure runs BSNL first, then Jio, then Airtel.

### Gaps
- No official TRAI or DoT household fixed-broadband penetration figure. No official count of villages without 4G as of 2026; only "no mobile" (8,985) and scheme progress are available.
- TRAI's 19.34 million "fixed wireless" category includes satellite but does not break it out. About 0.52 million sits outside 5G and UBR FWA (Wi-Fi, WiMax, satellite and others).

## 3. Bharti's satellite interests: Eutelsat/OneWeb stake, OneWeb India licence and status, ground network, what Mittal and Airtel say about customers

### Takeaway
Bharti Space owns 17.88% of Eutelsat, behind the French State's 29.65% (Eutelsat filing, Nov 2025), though Business Standard (8 Oct 2026) still calls Bharti the largest shareholder. OneWeb India has its GMPCS licence and IN-SPACe authorisation, has done its security demonstrations and holds provisional test spectrum, but no commercial permission yet. Mittal says the ground network has been ready for three years. The customers publicly lined up are government and enterprise users on land, at sea and in the air (via Tata's Nelco), plus disaster recovery, not homes. Separately, Airtel owns about 33% of Hughes Communications India, India's largest VSAT operator.

## 4. Analyst views on cannibalisation, addressable-market estimates, and who is more exposed

### Takeaway
Analysts are close to unanimous, though mostly from 2025, and the newest view is Jio's own commissioned report. Dish-based satellite won't scale in mainstream urban broadband. It fits remote and rural homes, enterprise, government, maritime and aviation. The only quantified India estimates found are old or indicative: Deloitte's $1.9 billion satellite-broadband market by 2030, and the minister's 20 lakh (2 million) user cap for Starlink. No named analyst was found saying whether Airtel or Jio is more exposed.

### Inferences

- **Affluent rural and semi-urban homes beyond fibre:** this is the most plausible consumer pool. The incumbent product there is FWA: 5G FWA is 51.9% rural, and Jio holds about 79% of 5G-plus-UBR FWA. So the consumer overlap leans towards Jio.
- **Enterprise, government, maritime, aviation, defence, mines and rigs (today's VSAT market):** the incumbents are HCIPL (Airtel 33%), Nelco and BSNL. - **Price war:** a mass consumer price war looks constrained by the 20 lakh cap (if it still applies), the kit cost and capacity. A narrower fight is more plausible: introductory pricing aimed at FWA price points (₹599–899) in rural and semi-urban areas, and bidding for enterprise and government contracts among the satellite players (Starlink, OneWeb, Jio–SES or Jio's own LEO plan).
- **Unserved places:** about 9,000 no-signal villages and border areas are a real but small pool. Analysts and officials point to institutions (defence, states, NDMA) as early customers there.

- Jio: 77.08% of operating revenue comes from prepaid mobile recharges. — [IndMoney (via desk file)](https://www.indmoney.com/blog/ipo/jio-ipo-starlink-threat)

### Inferences
- Computed: Airtel's revenue-earning mobile customers (376.5 million) are about 71% of Jio's 533.3 million customers. Yet Bharti Group's access AGR is about 90% of Jio's (₹29,803 crore against ₹33,226 crore). That gap, plus the ₹48 ARPU gap, is the measurable premium mix. Caveats: Bharti Group AGR also covers Bharti Hexacom and wireline; TRAI's subscriber counts (Airtel 491.6 million) include about 86 million low-revenue M2M SIMs.
- Airtel's premium users are mostly mobile postpaid and urban, so satellite home broadband does not touch most of them.

### Gaps
- Jio's postpaid subscriber count or share, and the share of high-ARPU users at either company: not found on any page opened.

## 7. Vi and BSNL: satellite plans and partnerships, as they bear on who loses customers

### Takeaway
Vi's satellite plan is direct-to-phone with AST SpaceMobile (announced Jun 2025). It faces a spectrum-rules problem, and no 2026 approval or launch was found. BSNL already sells satellite phones and a direct-to-device (D2D) service on Viasat's geostationary L-band satellites, and has a small VSAT business. Neither is a dish-broadband competitor to Starlink. BSNL's rural fixed-line base and Vi's rural-heavy mobile base are the most rural customer bases.

### Cited Findings
- **Vi with AST SpaceMobile:** AST "will design, manufacture, and manage the satellite constellation, while Vi will handle spectrum operations, integrate terrestrial networks, and manage regulatory access". Planned segments are consumer mobile, enterprise and IoT. Vi says satellite is "a complement to terrestrial connectivity". — [Telecom Review Asia, 2025 [ctx]](https://www.telecomreviewasia.com/news/technology-news/13780-vodafone-idea-ast-spacemobile-bring-d2d-satellite-connectivity-to-india/)
- Experts say Vi's spectrum "was assigned for a terrestrial network and cannot support a satellite-based network, according to the rules in place". One quote reads "No provision exists for leasing terrestrial IMT spectrum to satellite play[ers]" (truncated in the fetched page). — [Light Reading, 2025 [ctx]](https://www.lightreading.com/satellite/vodafone-idea-and-ast-spacemobile-s-d2d-plans-may-face-regulatory-snag-in-india)
- **Vi's position:** 193.1 million customers in Q1 FY27, "the first quarter of positive net subscriber addition since merger"; ARPU ₹177. — [TelecomTalk, Aug 2026 [ctx]](https://telecomtalk.info/airtel-reliance-jio-vodafone-idea-arpu-q1fy27/1010651/). Its TRAI mobile base is 199.57 million (Aug), 46.3% rural (computed), the most rural of the four; it has only 754,111 wireline lines. — [TRAI PR 125/2026 [ctx]](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-09/PR_No125of2026_0.pdf)

BSNL "also offers Direct-to-Device (D2D) service in India... with Viasat's geostationary L-band satellites". — [Deccan Herald, 9 Jul 2026 [ctx]](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/technology/bsnl-launches-satellite-phone-in-india-for-rs-134-lakh-all-you-need-to-know-4067786)

## 8. Surprises: facts that cut against the assumption that Starlink competes mainly with Jio

### Takeaway
On the consumer home side, the assumption broadly holds: the plausible overlap is rural and semi-urban FWA, which Jio dominates. But several facts point the other way. Airtel's group is already India's satellite incumbent, Bharti has its own LEO stake, and the enterprise and government segments that analysts expect Starlink to win first are Airtel-linked.

### Cited Findings
- **Airtel is the incumbent in Indian satellite connectivity today.** It owns about 33% of Hughes Communications India — [NSE filing, 5 Jan 2022 [ctx]](https://archives.nseindia.com/corporate/BHARTIARTL_05012022090755_VSATdealupdate.pdf). HCIPL has 67.64% of India's 1,84,579 VSAT users; JIO-VSAT has 0.11% (Infotel Satcom has 1.40%; its link to Reliance is UNVERIFIED) — [TRAI QPIR, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-10/QPIR_12102026.pdf). Bharti Space owns 17.88% of Eutelsat (OneWeb) — [Eutelsat, 25 Nov 2025 [ctx]](https://www.eutelsat.com/system/files/2025-11/DOC_Investor_Amendment-URD-2024-25_EN_251125.pdf). The enterprise, government, maritime and aviation segments expected to adopt satellite first are where Bharti-linked businesses already compete.
- **The existing satellite market is small and shrinking.** VSAT users fell 8.28% in one quarter (Mar–Jun 2026). — [TRAI QPIR](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-10/QPIR_12102026.pdf)
- **Airtel's home base is almost entirely urban.** Only 163,579 of its 12.28 million wireline lines (1.3%) are rural, against 7.4% for Jio and 32.9% for BSNL (computed) — [TRAI PR 125/2026 [ctx]](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-09/PR_No125of2026_0.pdf). Gopal Vittal says "95% odd of the market is in 400 cities", where Airtel has fibre — [Airtel transcript, 5 Aug 2026 [ctx]](https://assets.airtel.in/static-assets/cms/investor/docs/quarterly_results/2026-27/Q2/Earnings-Call-Transcript.pdf). That is the zone where satellite is least competitive, so Airtel's premium home users are not the obvious target.
- **Airtel is stepping back from the segment most like satellite.** Its homes strategy is to "pivot to fiber" and use FWA "only... where fiber is not accessible" after churn problems from low entry pricing. Net additions fell to 473K from 1,135K. — [Airtel transcript [ctx]](https://assets.airtel.in/static-assets/cms/investor/docs/quarterly_results/2026-27/Q2/Earnings-Call-Transcript.pdf); [Airtel IR Pack [ctx]](https://assets.airtel.in/static-assets/cms/investor/docs/quarterly_results/2026-27/Q1/Quarterly-IR-Pack-Bharti-Airtel-Consolidated.pdf). By contrast, 5G FWA is 51.9% rural and Jio has about 79% of 5G-plus-UBR FWA — [TRAI PR 125/2026 [ctx]](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-09/PR_No125of2026_0.pdf). This supports the Jio-overlap view for homes, but because Airtel chose to retreat.
- **Airtel's average home bill is falling.** Homes ARPU went from ₹537 to ₹523 YoY. A ₹3,000+ Starlink plan would be 5.7 times or more the average Airtel home bill (computed), so "skimming Airtel's premium homes" lacks a large premium-priced base to skim. — [Airtel IR Pack [ctx]](https://assets.airtel.in/static-assets/cms/investor/docs/quarterly_results/2026-27/Q1/Quarterly-IR-Pack-Bharti-Airtel-Consolidated.pdf)
- **Who flags the risk:** Jio's RHP lists satellite broadband as a key risk — [Business Standard, 15 Oct 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/jio-platforms-satellite-broadband-india-starlink-leo-spectrum-risk-126101500576_1.html). Airtel's Q1 FY27 earnings call never mentions satellite, Starlink or OneWeb (text search) — [Airtel transcript [ctx]](https://assets.airtel.in/static-assets/cms/investor/docs/quarterly_results/2026-27/Q2/Earnings-Call-Transcript.pdf)
- **Jio's own documents disagree on the Starlink price.** The commissioned Analysys Mason report quotes the US entry plan at about ₹4,500 (about US$50) — [Analysys Mason, 17 Jun 2026 [ctx]](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Analysys%20Mason%20-%20Industry%20Report%20for%20Jio%202026-06-17.pdf). The RHP, as reported, says about ₹4,800 (about US$55) — [Business Standard, 15 Oct 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/jio-platforms-satellite-broadband-india-starlink-leo-spectrum-risk-126101500576_1.html)
- **BSNL is not the most rural mobile operator.** Its mobile base is the least rural of the four: 33.0% (Aug 2026, computed) or 32.12% (June 2026), against Vi's 46.3% or 46.72%. In fixed lines, BSNL is the most rural. — [TRAI PR 125/2026 [ctx]](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-09/PR_No125of2026_0.pdf); [TRAI QPIR](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-10/QPIR_12102026.pdf)
- **Bharti is not Eutelsat's largest shareholder.** Eutelsat's filing puts the French State first at 29.65% and Bharti at 17.88%, although Indian coverage (Business Standard, 8 Oct 2026) still calls Bharti the largest. — [Eutelsat [ctx]](https://www.eutelsat.com/system/files/2025-11/DOC_Investor_Amendment-URD-2024-25_EN_251125.pdf); [Business Standard [ctx]](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/sunil-mittal-responds-to-musk-says-eutelsat-oneweb-also-awaiting-approvals-126100800646_1.html)

### Inferences
- "Starlink vs Jio" is accurate for rural and semi-urban home broadband. "Starlink vs Bharti" is the more accurate frame for enterprise, government and remote-site satellite connectivity, the segments analysts expect satellite to win first. There Starlink competes with Bharti's OneWeb stake and Airtel's HCIPL joint venture.
- The evidence does not support Starlink as a bigger threat to Airtel's premium mobile users. Those are postpaid and urban, and dish broadband cannot replace a phone. Direct-to-cell is judged years away from disrupting mobile (JM Financial expert, 2025).

### Gaps
- No quantified estimate exists of how many HCIPL, Nelco or OneWeb enterprise sites Starlink could take, or of OneWeb's planned India pricing.
