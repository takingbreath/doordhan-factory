## 1. Revenue split by segment and product (RHP/DRHP), and the IndMoney "77.08% prepaid mobile" figure

### Takeaway
The RHP does not split revenue by product. Jio Platforms reports one operating segment, and the only product-type split it discloses is the share of revenue from "pre-paid connectivity services". That share was 79.35% (FY24), 78.57% (FY25), 77.08% (FY26) and 76.37% (Q1 FY27), and it is falling.

IndMoney's 77.08% is the correct FY26 number, but the RHP calls it "pre-paid connectivity services", not "prepaid mobile". The newest figure is 76.37%.

### Cited Findings
- **No product split (RHP fn. 75, p. 200, "Our Business"):** "The Company operates as a single operating segment. Except for digital connectivity (which constitutes substantially all revenue), standalone revenue contributions for individual products are not separately disclosed as they are bundled with or subsumed within connectivity services." The restated accounts say the same: "the Group has single segment as per the requirements of Ind AS 108" (Note 43, p. 428). — [RHP, Jio Platforms, 14 Oct 2026](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)
- **Prepaid share, exact RHP wording (Risk Factor 3, p. 33; also pp. 32, 135):** "Our revenue from pre-paid connectivity services constituted 76.37%, 77.88%, 77.08%, 78.57% and 79.35% of our consolidated Revenue from Operations for the three months ended June 30, 2026 and 2025, and Fiscals 2026, 2025 and 2024, respectively." Reliance Retail Ltd (RRL), a subsidiary of the promoter, is "the sole distributor of our pre-paid connectivity services". It buys recharge vouchers "on a principal-to-principal basis" and resells them. The RHP treats this as a stand-alone risk ("Any disruption in our arrangement with RRL would adversely impact our business"). — [RHP, 14 Oct 2026](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)
- **The DRHP gave only the FY26 figure:** "revenue from pre-paid connectivity services constituted 77.08% of our consolidated Revenue from Operations for Fiscal 2026". — [DRHP, 19 Jun 2026](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/Registration_19062026154852_DRHP.pdf)
- **What IndMoney says:** "77.08% of total operating revenue comes from pre-paid mobile connectivity recharges", sourced to the "JIO DRHP", with no year stated. It also gives ARPU ₹214 a month, 42.3 GB, 42.60% of fixed broadband (2.71 crore homes) and 77.49% of 5G/UBR FWA (1.29 crore AirFiber homes). — [IndMoney, last updated 13 Oct 2026](https://www.indmoney.com/blog/ipo/jio-ipo-starlink-threat)

### Inferences
- **What "prepaid" covers.** "Pre-paid connectivity services" is broader than prepaid mobile: JioFiber and AirFiber also sell prepaid plans. The RHP does not say whether prepaid home-broadband recharges are inside the 77.08%. The accurate phrasing is "77.08% of FY26 revenue from operations came from pre-paid connectivity services, all sold through Reliance Retail".
- **No sourced product split exists.** Any split into prepaid mobile, postpaid, JioFiber, AirFiber, enterprise, devices and cloud is not disclosed. A source giving such ₹ crore figures is estimating.
- **Where the satellite-exposed lines sit.** Everything the RHP lists as satellite-exposed (Section 3) would mostly fall in the non-prepaid 22.92% (₹33,666 crore in FY26), unless home-broadband prepaid sits in the prepaid share. That pool also holds postpaid mobile, enterprise mobility, device sales and digital services. The non-prepaid pool is growing faster (about 22% CAGR against about 14%).

### Gaps
- No ₹ or % split for prepaid mobile, postpaid mobile, JioFiber, AirFiber/UBR, enterprise, digital services, cloud/AI or devices exists in the RHP or DRHP (fn. 75 confirms this). The RHP does not define "pre-paid connectivity services" further.

## 2. Customers by product, ARPU, data per user, rural vs urban

At 30 Jun 2026 Jio had:
- 533.3 million customers, 503.6 million of them on mobile broadband and 284.9 million on 5G;
- 28.6 million fixed-broadband homes: 14.6 million JioFiber (wired) and 14.0 million JioAirFiber.

ARPU went ₹181.7 (FY24 exit quarter) → ₹206.2 → ₹214.0 → ₹215.6 (Q1 FY27). Data use went 28.7 → 33.6 → 42.3 → 43.7 GB a month. The RHP gives no enterprise customer count. On geography it gives only that about 46% of data used on the network is rural.

### Inferences
- Total customers (533.3m) minus mobile broadband (503.6m) leaves 29.7 million. The RHP does not say what they are; it is close to the 28.6 million fixed-broadband base, but that is not confirmed.
- **The exposed base is small.** The base satellite broadband could target (28.6m homes) is about 5% of Jio's customer count. It is also the fastest-growing part, and AirFiber is almost half of it.
- FY25's tariff hike shows in the table: net additions fell to 6.4m (from 42.5m) and churn rose to 1.81%. The RHP says the hike "contributed to higher customer churn" (Section 6).

### Gaps
- No enterprise customer count, enterprise ARPU or fixed-broadband ARPU is disclosed.
- No quarterly ARPU series beyond the exit quarters above; the RHP's ARPU and 5G charts are images.
- No rural vs urban split of customers or revenue.
- Q2 FY27 (Jul–Sep 2026) results were not found as of 18 Oct.
- Upstox (9 Oct) calls ₹215.6 "Q2" ARPU; the RHP shows it is Q1 FY27 (three months to 30 Jun 2026). — [Upstox, 9 Oct 2026](https://upstox.com/news/business-news/latest-updates/akash-ambani-s-most-affordable-player-remark-for-jio-after-airtel-revises-postpaid-plans/article-201586/)

## 3. Which revenue lines dish-based satellite broadband or satellite direct-to-phone could touch, and how big each is

### Takeaway
The RHP names exactly what it thinks satellite could hit: "JioFiber, JioAirFiber, UBR-based fixed wireless access, rural broadband, and enterprise and government connectivity solutions". It does not mention mobile.

These lines serve 28.6 million fixed-broadband homes (5.4% of customers) plus an undisclosed enterprise base. Their revenue is not disclosed. The only hard upper pool is the 22.92% of FY26 revenue (₹33,666 crore) that was not prepaid connectivity, which also contains other businesses.

Direct-to-phone would in principle touch mobile connectivity, the bulk of the ₹1,13,219 crore prepaid pool. But Jio already covers more than 99% of the population, D2D is not yet authorised in India, and the RHP does not mention D2D at all.

### Cited Findings
- **Lines the RHP names as exposed (Risk Factor 6, p. 35):** "Satellite and non-geostationary orbit ("NGSO") connectivity providers may offer services that directly compete with or substitute our JioFiber, JioAirFiber, UBR-based fixed wireless access, rural broadband, and enterprise and government connectivity solutions, particularly in coverage-gap and underserved areas where terrestrial network deployment is capital-intensive or logistically challenging." — [RHP p. 35](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)
- **Size of those lines in customers:** 28.6 million JioFiber + JioAirFiber customers (14.6m wired, 14.0m AirFiber), 43.32% of India's 66.1 million fixed-broadband connections (30 Jun 2026). No enterprise or government customer count is given. — [RHP pp. 169–170](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)
- **Size in revenue:** not disclosed; the RHP says product revenue is "bundled with or subsumed within connectivity services" (fn. 75). Non-prepaid revenue was ₹33,666 crore (22.92%) in FY26 and ₹9,257 crore (23.63%) in Q1 FY27 (derived). — [RHP pp. 33, 131, 200](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)
- **Market size in the RHP:** India's fixed-broadband market is projected to grow from ₹340–350 billion (₹34,000–35,000 crore, about US$4 billion) in FY2026 to ₹920–930 billion by FY2031, as customers rise from 66.1 million to 150.4 million (Analysys Mason). — [RHP p. 167](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)
- **Coverage, which limits the mobile overlap:** "As of June 30, 2026, our wireless broadband network covers more than 99% of the population". — [RHP p. 128](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)
- **D2D rules are not settled (context, older than 7 days):**
  - TRAI began examining D2D in April 2026, in two variants: "D2D service via satellite by using MSS spectrum, and... D2D service via satellite by using IMT spectrum". — [TelecomTalk, 9 Apr 2026](https://telecomtalk.info/trai-is-planning-for-direct-to-device/1006144/)
  - DoT held informal consultations with Apple and Google, who flagged smartphone hardware limits and the need to follow global standards. Commercial D2D "may use terrestrial mobile spectrum bands... requiring country-specific regulatory approvals and coordination with telecom operators". — [Business Standard, 18 May 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/technology/tech-news/india-s-d2d-ambitions-depend-on-hardware-and-global-standards-explained-126051800677_1.html)
- **Jio's own plan includes D2D:** its 1,600-satellite proposal lists "Direct-to-device calling for rural areas" among its uses. — [TelecomTalk, 8 Oct 2026](https://telecomtalk.info/imc2026-jio-plans-satellite-network-connect-600000villages/1012635/); [Livemint, 8 Oct 2026](https://www.livemint.com/technology/jios-1-600-satellite-leo-network-23-ground-stations-remote-area-coverage-and-more-11791457201362.html)
- **Hardware barrier:** a satellite dish costs $300–500. — [IndMoney, updated 13 Oct 2026](https://www.indmoney.com/blog/ipo/jio-ipo-starlink-threat)
- **Starlink's India price signals (context, Dec 2025):** Starlink's India site briefly showed ₹8,600 a month; the company removed it within hours and called it a "technical glitch". ET reported plans to charge retail customers ₹2,500–3,500 a month. Official pricing awaits approvals and spectrum. Musk said Starlink "will complement existing telecom operators, as its low-earth-orbit network cannot serve densely populated regions" (Business Standard's paraphrase). — [Business Standard, 10 Dec 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/starlink-india-launch-monthly-plans-retail-pricing-satellite-internet-125121000198_1.html)
- **Musk, Oct 2026:** "if Starlink is too expensive in India, no one will buy it, so SpaceX will need to make pricing affordable." No India price was given. — Desk background file (Outlook Business 10 Oct; Reuters via Deccan Chronicle 11 Oct; not re-opened for these notes)

### Inferences
**Dish-based broadband overlap (main exposure)**
- The overlap is JioFiber, JioAirFiber/UBR, rural broadband and enterprise/government links. In customers it is 28.6m homes (about 5.4% of 533.3m) plus an undisclosed enterprise base.
- **Illustrative run-rate (UNVERIFIED; my assumptions, not a disclosure).** If all 28.6m homes paid the confirmed entry prices of ₹599–699 a month before GST (Section 6), home broadband would earn about ₹20,600–24,000 crore a year. That is about 14–16% of FY26 revenue from operations.
- On the FY26 average base (about 22.3m, the mean of about 17.5m in Mar 2025 and 27.1m in Mar 2026), it would be about ₹16,000–18,700 crore (11–13%).
- These sit uneasily with the non-prepaid pool (₹33,666 crore), which also holds postpaid mobile, enterprise and digital services. So either effective broadband ARPU is lower (discounts, bundles, free months), or some home-broadband revenue is inside the prepaid share. Treat any "home broadband = X% of revenue" figure as an estimate.

**Direct-to-phone overlap (theoretical, near-term small)**
- Mobile connectivity is the pool D2D could touch: about ₹1,13,219 crore of prepaid revenue in FY26 (77.08%), plus undisclosed postpaid.
- With terrestrial coverage above 99% of the population, D2D would mainly serve coverage gaps (the uncovered <1%, maritime, border areas, disasters). The RHP does not size that. D2D in India still awaits a spectrum and authorisation framework, and Jio plans its own D2D calling.

**Price gap**
- Starlink's US entry plan as the RHP quotes it (₹4,800) is about 22× Jio's ₹215.6 ARPU and about 6.9× the ₹699 JioFiber plan.
- The RHP's African range (₹2,500–4,500) is about 3.6–6.4× the ₹699 plan.

### Gaps
- No disclosed revenue for fixed broadband, enterprise or government connectivity, rural broadband or postpaid mobile, and no disclosed fixed-broadband ARPU.
- No source sized Jio's population or revenue outside terrestrial coverage.
- No official Starlink India price exists; Starlink's Indian direct-to-cell plans and spectrum were not researched in depth here.

## 4. What the RHP says about satellite, Starlink, LEO, NGSO, direct-to-device and administrative spectrum allocation (exact text)

### Takeaway
"Starlink" appears exactly twice in the 591-page RHP: in one price example and its footnote. The core text is Risk Factor 6 (p. 35), which in the RHP names satellite in its title, cites the draft 2026 administrative-allocation rules and lists the Jio products satellite could substitute. None of that was in the June DRHP.

The RHP contains no instance of "direct-to-device", "D2D", "direct-to-cell", "non-terrestrial", "OneWeb", "Kuiper", "Eutelsat", "JioSpaceFiber", "MEO" or "1,600 satellites".

### Cited Findings
- **Risk Factor 6, title (RHP p. 35):** "6. If we are unable to respond to changes in technologies, including for instance, more competitive satellite-based broadband services and the evolving demands of our customers, we may face technological obsolescence, which would have a material adverse impact on our business, financial condition and results of operations." — [RHP p. 35](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)
- **Risk Factor 6, satellite paragraph (RHP p. 35), verbatim:** "Further, as per the Analysys Mason Report, satellite-based connectivity has been touted as the potential next disruptor in digital connectivity technology. Unlike terrestrial networks where a slice of spectrum is exclusively locked to one operator, satellite spectrum can be considered as a shareable resource managed internationally by the International Telecommunication Union. In India, satellite spectrum is currently being assigned administratively and there is no auction for satellite spectrum. The GoI has also published the draft Telecommunications (Spectrum Assignment by Administrative Process) Rules, 2026, to regulate the administrative allocation of spectrum under the Telecommunications Act, 2023. This administrative allocation framework may lower entry economics for satellite-based broadband service providers, including operators of low earth orbit and non-geostationary satellite orbit constellations, when compared to existing digital connectivity players, which are required to acquire spectrum through auctions. Satellite and non-geostationary orbit ("NGSO") connectivity providers may offer services that directly compete with or substitute our JioFiber, JioAirFiber, UBR-based fixed wireless access, rural broadband, and enterprise and government connectivity solutions, particularly in coverage-gap and underserved areas where terrestrial network deployment is capital-intensive or logistically challenging. Accordingly, there can be no assurance that the competitive advantages our JioFiber, JioAirFiber, and UBR-based fixed wireless access services currently enjoy vis-à-vis this technology will continue in the future. If satellite solutions scale faster than expected, become more cost effective, or are adopted by competitors to address coverage gaps and enterprise or government use cases, our current offerings may become less competitive and require additional investments and increased costs. While we are in the process of developing satellite constellation-based connectivity solutions, and would seek satellite spectrum under the administrative allocation of spectrum and may enter into strategic partnerships for the same, we cannot assure you whether we would be able to deploy our satellite-based connectivity solutions in a timely manner, or at all, or in a manner which is competitive with alternative offerings in the market, or procure necessary regulatory approvals in time, or at all." — [RHP p. 35](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)

- **The disclosure grew between June and October.** The satellite risk now has its own title, and the administrative-allocation and substitution paragraph is new. ## 5. Jio's own satellite assets: JioSpaceFiber, the Jio–SES joint venture, Jio Satellite Communications' licence, and the 1,600-satellite LEO plan

### Takeaway
In the accounts, Jio's satellite business is tiny:
- Jio Satellite Communications Ltd (JSCL) earned ₹34.3 crore in FY26 (0.02% of revenue), all of it apparently billed to RJIL.
- "Satellite infrastructure" capex fell from ₹81.2 crore (FY24) to ₹3 crore (FY26).
- The SES joint venture (Jio Space Technology Ltd, 51:49) is carried at ₹4.1 crore.

JSCL holds a unified licence including GMPCS and VSAT, valid until March 2042, but awaits security clearance and spectrum. The 1,600-satellite LEO constellation was shown at IMC on 8 Oct as a proposal. It is not in the RHP, and its cost is not disclosed.

## 9. Surprises: facts that contradict "Jio is just a SIM company" or "Starlink will kill Jio"

### Takeaway
Both narratives are wrong in specific ways.

**Against "just a SIM company":**
- Jio is India's largest home-broadband provider, with 28.6 million homes and 43.32% share.
- Its non-prepaid revenue is growing about 22% a year.
- Its parent company earns ₹16,167 crore a year from digital services.

**Against "Starlink will kill Jio":**
- The RHP itself limits the satellite threat to home and enterprise broadband in coverage gaps.
- Its mobile network covers more than 99% of people.
- Starlink's quoted prices are 3.6–22 times Jio's.
- The market priced the feud for about two days.

**Uncomfortable for Jio's own position:**
- Its satellite business is nearly zero in the accounts.
- It strengthened its satellite risk disclosure after the DRHP.

### Cited Findings
- **"Prepaid" is not "prepaid mobile", and it is shrinking.** The RHP's 77.08% (FY26) is "pre-paid connectivity services", all sold through one related distributor (RRL), and fell to 76.37% in Q1 FY27 from 79.35% in FY24. — [RHP p. 33](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)
- **No company-disclosed home-broadband share of revenue exists.** "Standalone revenue contributions for individual products are not separately disclosed". — [RHP fn. 75, p. 200](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)
- **Jio is India's largest fixed-broadband provider:** 28.6m homes, 43.32% share, about 1.9× the second player, 61.73% of net additions in Q1 FY27. JioAirFiber alone has 78.62% of 5G/UBR FWA. — [RHP pp. 169–170](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)
- **The products named as satellite-substitutable** (JioFiber, JioAirFiber/UBR, rural broadband, enterprise/government) are where Jio is growing fastest: fixed broadband +55.19% in FY26. — [RHP pp. 35, 215](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)
- **The satellite risk wording was strengthened after the DRHP.** The DRHP (19 Jun) risk title did not mention satellite. The RHP (14 Oct) title names "more competitive satellite-based broadband services" and adds the administrative-allocation, "lower entry economics" and substitution passages. The quoted Starlink US price rose from ~₹4,500 (Feb 2026 website) to ~₹4,800 (Aug 2026 website). — [DRHP](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/Registration_19062026154852_DRHP.pdf); [RHP p. 35, 168](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)
- **Jio wants the same rule it calls a rival's advantage.** It "would seek satellite spectrum under the administrative allocation of spectrum", the framework it says "may lower entry economics" for LEO/NGSO rivals. — [RHP p. 35](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)
  - Background: in October 2024 Reliance argued for auctions (Outlook Business, 14 Oct 2024, cited in the desk background file; not re-opened here).
- **Jio's satellite business barely registers in the accounts.** JSCL revenue was ₹34.3 crore (0.02%) in FY26, apparently all billed to RJIL. Satellite capex fell from ₹81.2 crore (FY24) to ₹3 crore (FY26). The SES JV is carried at ₹4.1 crore. The 1,600-satellite plan is absent from the RHP. — [RHP pp. 43, 261, 291](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)
- **"Direct-to-device" never appears in the RHP**, yet Jio's own IMC proposal includes "direct-to-device calling for rural areas". — [RHP](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf); [TelecomTalk, 8 Oct 2026](https://telecomtalk.info/imc2026-jio-plans-satellite-network-connect-600000villages/1012635/)
- **Coverage is near-universal**, more than 99% of the population on wireless broadband, which limits where satellite-to-phone adds anything. — [RHP p. 128](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)
- **Rural matters a lot to Jio:** about 46% of data used on its network is rural, and 36% of Q1 FY27 home-broadband additions were rural. Rural is where satellite pitches itself. — [RHP p. 203](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)
- **An independent analyst favours Starlink within satellite.** IDC's Simon Baker says Starlink would "likely... dominate" Indian consumer satellite broadband if opened now. That cuts against "Jio's satellites will beat Starlink" (but is about the satellite niche, not broadband overall). — [India Today, 9 Oct 2026](https://www.indiatoday.in/technology/news/story/is-elon-musk-taking-on-ambani-to-win-starlink-race-in-india-before-jio-catches-up-experts-say-it-is-a-reason-3013128-2026-10-09)
- **The market shrugged.** The GMP rose 37% from 7 to 18 Oct. Bharti Airtel rose 5.2% from 6 to 16 Oct. RIL's moves on feud days came against a falling market and a 26% year-to-date decline. — [InvestorGain](https://www.investorgain.com/gmp/jio-platforms-ipo/2306/); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/RELIANCE.NS/history/); [Fortune India, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.fortuneindia.com/markets/ipo/bharti-airtel-vs-jio-ipo-nomura-sees-valuation-gap-narrowing-as-tariff-hikes-loom/164287)
- **The analyst risk flagged most is tariffs, not Starlink.** Nomura's concern is that Jio passed through only 49% of the 2024 hike (Airtel 69%) because of "the highest proportion of non-data users". — [Fortune India, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.fortuneindia.com/markets/ipo/bharti-airtel-vs-jio-ipo-nomura-sees-valuation-gap-narrowing-as-tariff-hikes-loom/164287)
- **Much of the parent's digital-services revenue is billed to its own telecom arm.** Standalone revenue was ₹16,167 crore in FY26, of which ₹6,830 crore came from RJIL. — [RHP pp. 132, 393](https://myjiostatic.cdn.jio.com/cms/assets/iv3/Red%20Herring%20Prospectus.pdf)

### Inferences
- **For the writer.** The RHP's own numbers show the exposed business is home and enterprise broadband: about 5% of customers, the fastest-growing segment, and a revenue line Jio does not disclose. Mobile is mostly insulated.
- **Two sentences that sit side by side in the RHP:** satellite "is not likely to be able to compete with FWA on pricing", and the disclosure that satellite could "directly compete with or substitute" JioAirFiber.

### Gaps
- No primary-source figure for home-broadband revenue, enterprise revenue or the population outside coverage.
- No primary evidence links the RHP's stronger satellite language to the Musk feud. The RHP is dated 14 Oct and Musk's posts began on 7 Oct; that is timing only.
