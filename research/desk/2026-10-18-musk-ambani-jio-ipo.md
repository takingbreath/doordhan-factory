# Musk vs Ambani, linked to the Jio IPO: research for Akhilesh's pick (Sun 18 Oct 2026)

Research only (desk rule, 2026-10-08): facts, sources, what others made, the curiosity gap. No hooks or lines.
Tracker: `musk-ambani-starlink` (marked picked) and `jio-ipo`.

## The link in one paragraph
In its own IPO prospectus, filed with SEBI on Thu 15 Oct, Jio Platforms lists satellite broadband as one of the key risks to its business. It even quotes Starlink's prices: about ₹4,800 a month in the US and ₹2,500–4,500 in Africa. Jio's average customer pays about ₹214 a month. The prospectus also warns that India's way of handing out satellite spectrum (administratively, not by auction) "may lower entry economics" for rivals like Starlink. That is the same rule Reliance lobbied against in October 2024. Then, in the week before the IPO opens, the world's richest man spent eight days publicly calling Mukesh Ambani "Prime Minister Ambani". Reliance answered on Fri 16 Oct, and Musk replied "Much appreciated." The issue opens Wed 21 Oct.

**Where things stand now:** All three satellite players (Starlink, Jio Satcom and Airtel-backed OneWeb) hold licences, and none can sell yet. Each needs (1) a security clearance and (2) spectrum. Spectrum waits on a revenue-based pricing framework, which, Scindia says, TRAI and the Department of Telecommunications are "close to" deciding. Amazon's Kuiper has shown interest. Nobody has published evidence that Reliance is behind the delay, and the government denies any bias (Business Standard, 17 Oct).

## 2. The link: what Jio's own IPO prospectus says about satellites (RHP filed 15 Oct)
From Business Standard, 15 Oct, quoting the RHP:
- Satellite broadband is flagged as "a potential disruptor" and "one of the key risks to its business".
- Jio's argument: satellite "is not likely to be able to compete with FWA on pricing... For example, Starlink's entry level fixed broadband plans in the USA are priced at ~₹4800 (~US$55) per month, and in most African countries are priced at Rs 2500–Rs 4500 (US$28–50) per month." Satellite may matter only "for specialised use cases in remote or hard-to-reach locations".
- The spectrum risk: satellite spectrum is given out administratively, while terrestrial spectrum is auctioned. "This administrative allocation framework may lower entry economics for satellite-based broadband service providers, including operators of low earth orbit (LEO) and non-geostationary satellite orbit (NGSO) constellations, when compared to existing digital connectivity players, which are required to acquire spectrum through auctions."
- On its own products: "there can be no assurance that the competitive advantages our JioFiber, JioAirFiber, and UBR-based fixed wireless access services currently enjoy vis-à-vis this technology will continue... If satellite solutions scale faster than expected, become more cost effective, or are adopted by competitors... our current offerings may become less competitive and require additional investments and increased costs."
- On its own satellite plan (1,600 LEO satellites, separate from the Jio–SES joint venture): it "cannot assure you whether we would be able to deploy our satellite-based connectivity solutions in a timely manner, or at all".

Exposure (Multibagg, 16 Oct, from the RHP): Reliance Jio Infocomm, the telecom arm, made 87.74% of Jio Platforms' FY26 revenue, 94.88% of EBITDA and 93.76% of profit.

How much satellite can actually touch (IndMoney, from the DRHP; analysis, not the company's own words):
- 77.08% of operating revenue comes from prepaid mobile recharges, which satellite can't compete for. A satellite dish costs $300–500 of hardware and needs a clear view of the sky.
- Jio's ARPU is ₹214 a month for 42.3 GB, about ₹7.74 a GB.
- The overlap is home broadband: Jio has 42.6% of fixed broadband (2.71 crore homes) and 77.49% of 5G fixed wireless (1.29 crore AirFiber homes).
- The other side: SpaceX's connectivity revenue was $7.55 billion in H1 2026 (+49%), with Starlink subscribers doubling from 6 million to 12 million. SpaceX itself listed in June 2026 (Benzinga).

## 3. The Jio IPO: terms (RHP dated 14 Oct)
- **Size and structure:** price band ₹1,065–1,119; 27 crore fresh shares, all new, no existing shareholder selling. That is about ₹30,213 crore at the top of the band, India's biggest IPO, ahead of Hyundai India's ₹27,870 crore (Oct 2024). (Chittorgarh; Business Standard, 17 Oct)
- **Valuation:** about ₹10.3 lakh crore at the top of the band, short of the roughly ₹11 lakh crore it was earlier said to seek (Business Standard). Reuters reported $106–108 billion. The new shares are about 3% of the company. P/E is about 33 on FY26 earnings at the top of the band (Chittorgarh).
- **Where the money goes:** ₹27,500 crore to repay borrowings of Reliance Jio Infocomm; the rest is for general purposes (Chittorgarh). Borrowings were ₹70,781 crore at Mar 2026 and ₹61,372 crore at Jun 2026.
- **Dates:** anchor book Mon 19 Oct; open Wed 21 to Fri 23 Oct; allotment Mon 26; listing Wed 28 Oct (tentative), on NSE and BSE.
- **Retail:** lot 12 shares = ₹13,428 at the top of the band; at least 35% of the net issue goes to retail, up to 50% to QIBs, at least 15% to HNIs.
- **Reliance shareholders:** a quota for RIL shareholders (bids up to ₹2 lakh). PTI reported ₹2,500 crore for holders as of 14 Oct, and ₹500 crore for employees (tracker note, 15 Oct), with an employee discount of ₹54.
- **Ownership:** RIL's stake goes from 66.43% to 64.48% after the issue (Chittorgarh).
- **Financials (restated consolidated, ₹ crore):**

  The FY26 EBITDA margin is 51.91%. Revenue grew 16% and profit 15% in FY26.
- **Customers:** 533.3 million at 30 Jun 2026 (Chittorgarh, from the RHP).
- **Grey market:** GMP ₹250 on InvestorGain's page, last updated 18 Oct; it was ₹148 two weeks earlier, so that's about 22% over ₹1,119. Earlier: ₹225 (IIFL, 16 Oct) and ₹206 (Outlook Money, 15 Oct). GMP is unofficial dealer talk, not a forecast.

## 4. The curiosity gaps
- Jio's own IPO document, the one millions will read before applying, names Starlink and quotes its price. The company says it isn't worried, but it put Starlink in the risk section anyway.
- ₹214 a month (Jio's average customer) against ₹4,800 a month (Starlink in the US, as Jio's prospectus quotes it). So why is a company that size worried about Starlink at all?
- The rule Jio's prospectus says helps Starlink (spectrum handed out, not auctioned) is the rule Reliance lobbied against two years ago, and lost.
- Musk spent eight days calling Ambani "Prime Minister". Then, five days before India's biggest IPO, he wrote "Much appreciated." What changed?
- Three companies hold satellite licences and none can sell. The one whose boss shouts loudest, Starlink, is behind Airtel's OneWeb in the queue, by Mittal's account.

- **No call on the IPO:** not "apply", not "skip", not "listing gain pakka". GMP only as "grey market says", if at all; it is unofficial. No price levels for RIL.
- **Musk's claims are accusations:** "monopoly", "oligarchs" and "Prime Minister Ambani" are his posts. The government calls them "baseless and misconceived", and there is no evidence that Reliance caused the delay. Show the posts as screenshots, quoted exactly.
- **Real people:** Musk and Ambani appear only as licensed stills, their own real footage or screenshots of their posts. No AI face or voice, and no words they didn't say (CLAUDE.md rule 4). Mittal's and Reliance's lines are quotable as said.
