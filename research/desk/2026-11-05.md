## 2. Stockal: Indians' Apple and Nvidia shares were sold without their consent and swapped for Chinese microcaps; losses over ₹100 crore

Hot lane (new; first reported by Moneycontrol on Tue 3 Nov, company statements Wed 4 Nov) · HOT · **9/10** (momentum 3/3, stake 1/1, shareability 2/2, gap 2/2, known name 1/2) · tracker id `stockal-unauthorised-trades`

Window: About 12 Nov: regulator and police steps, and DriveWealth's response, will keep turning it.

### What's happening
- Stockal (owned by Borderless Investing) lets Indians buy US shares; its US broker and custodian is DriveWealth. On 15 Oct, during US market hours, someone logged into 26 customer accounts with valid credentials. In 13 of them, large-cap US holdings were sold and the money used to buy thinly traded microcaps. No money was withdrawn (Borderless statement, BusinessLine, 4 Nov).
- One investor's complaint: 10 holdings sold on 15 Oct for $335,768 (about ₹3.19 crore): 150 Apple, 234.8 Tesla, 93 Palantir, 80 Amazon, 40 Alphabet, 80 AMD, 400 Nvidia and others. All of it bought 3 lakh shares of Zhibao Technology, a Nasdaq-listed Chinese digital-insurance company, for $315,681, plus a $3,000 commission. He learnt of it by email on 18 Oct. He had used Stockal since 2020, introduced through HDFC Securities (India Today, 4 Nov).
- Nearly 30 investors have complained; preliminary losses exceed ₹100 crore based on two investors' disclosures, per Moneycontrol (via India Today). Another investor told NDTV Profit she and her husband lost close to $3.2 million. An FIR and complaints to regulators have been filed.
- Borderless says it found no breach of its own systems and suspects social engineering using data from 'another company's data breach several weeks earlier' (unconfirmed); help to customers is 'in good faith', not an admission of liability. HDFC Securities says its partnership with Stockal ended in 2024 and it isn't named in the FIR (BusinessLine, 4 Nov).

### The curiosity gap
- Someone sold a man's Apple, Tesla and Nvidia shares and bought 3 lakh shares of a Chinese company he had never heard of, and nobody took any money out. Why would a thief do that?
- Your US shares sit with a broker in America you've probably never heard of (DriveWealth). When something goes wrong, who in India answers for them?
