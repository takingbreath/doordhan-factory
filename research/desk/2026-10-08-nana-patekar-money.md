## In one paragraph
A boy who earned ₹35 a month painting film posters became the first character actor in Hindi cinema to ask for ₹1 crore a film. He died worth an estimated ₹50–80 crore, still living in a 750 sq ft 1BHK in Andheri. He gave up a ₹1.5 crore car to put the money into farmers' families instead. No investments, stock holdings or startup stakes of his turn up anywhere. His money went into land (a 27-acre Pune farm, a Goa house) and into the Naam Foundation.

## 1. Where the money came from
- **Childhood: ₹35 a month.** At 13, after his father's business was lost (one account says an acquaintance fraudulently transferred it into his own name), he painted film posters and zebra crossings for ₹35 a month and one meal a day. [reported]

  - Sources disagree on details: he walked 8 km or 16 km a day. A "₹15 for zebra crossings" figure appears in one Moneycontrol headline but on no page I could open, so don't use it.
- **Theatre: ₹50 a show,** while his wife Neelkanti supported him financially. [reported] Koimoi, 8 Oct (link above); Times of India headline, 8 Oct.
- **The ₹1 crore moment.** Paresh Rawal (to The Lallantop) said Nana was the first character actor in Hindi cinema to ask for ₹1 crore a role: "Heroes did not dare to ask for it; Nana Patekar asked and received it." [old: April 2025]

- **Money he refused:** he told Rohit Shetty not to pay him for his Golmaal Again cameo and to give the fee to an orphanage ("Tujhse paise lunga?"). [reported] NDTV and India Today, 8 Oct (Rohit Shetty's account).

## 3. The ₹1.5 crore car
- In 2015 he had saved about ₹1.5 crore for a new car. After a TV report on the family of a farmer who had died by suicide, he dropped the purchase. He meant to give ₹15,000 each to 1,000 farmers' families. [reported]
- In August 2015 he handed out ₹15,000 cheques to 62 families in Vidarbha. Business Today says early aid was ₹15,000–25,000 per widow or family.
- In his own retelling the amount is "₹1–1.5 crore". Much of the story comes from his interviews and a writer's (Ranbir Pushp's) recollection.

- Context [old]: Maharashtra recorded 3,228 farmer suicides in 2015 (Indian Express, via the Daily Star).

## 5. Where the money went: giving
- **Naam Foundation** (founded Sept 2015 with Makarand Anaspure). [self-reported] https://naammh.org/
  - 77,17,000 farmers benefited;
  - 50,908 disaster-relief families;
  - 4,957 widows supported;
  - 198 rivers rejuvenated across 2,187 villages;
  - 7.04 crore cubic metres of silt removed;
  - 385 farm ponds;
  - ₹102.5 lakh in aid to the families of army martyrs.
- **Funding:**
  - Individuals and corporate CSR: partners shown include Tata Motors (Sumant Moolgaokar Development Foundation), BASF India, NHPC, Bharat Forge, Tech Mahindra, Michelin, Komatsu.
  - Donations qualify for 80G tax deduction.
  - It reportedly raised ₹80 lakh on day one and over ₹6.5 crore in two weeks (YourStory, Feb 2016 [old]: https://yourstory.com/2016/02/nana-patekar).
  - No audited income figures found; the MCA filings of the 2021 company would have them.
- **His own giving:**
  - Business Today says he gave award money and film fees to drought relief "often without publicity". No totals.
  - ₹42 lakh in relief for families hit by cross-border shelling in Rajouri and Poonch (J&K). [reported]
  - "Aarogya cards" (health cards) for 500 farmers' widows in Marathwada. [reported] Moneycontrol headline, 8 Oct.

## 6. The curiosity gap
- **"₹80 crore, and he lived in a 1BHK."** The wealth number and the way he lived don't match. Every tribute today runs one or the other, rarely the money logic connecting them.
- **"The best investment he ever made was a ₹1.1 lakh flat."** It's now worth about ₹3 crore, while the ₹1.5 crore he could have spent on a car went to farmers instead. A money channel can set the two side by side: an asset he kept versus a depreciating asset he refused.
- **"No stocks, no startups."** In a week when every celebrity seems to be in a startup or an IPO (Jio's IPO is this month), the richest thing about him was land and a foundation. For our audience that's a quiet "what would you do with ₹1.5 crore?"
- **"₹35 a month to ₹1 crore a film."** The rise itself is the most-covered angle today (NDTV, News18, Outlook Luxe all headline "₹35 a month to crores"). It's saturated, so the gap is in the contrast above, not in the rise.
