## 2. eMudhra crashed 20% after a researcher found its secret security keys were tiny numbers. One of them was the number 4

Hot lane (new; stock mover, 20% lower circuit) · HOT · **9/10** (momentum 3/3, stake 1/1, shareability 2/2, gap 2/2, known name 1/2) · tracker id `emudhra-key-4`

Window: Thu 22 Oct to about Tue 27 Oct, when Google is due to remove eMudhra's root from Chrome (Inc42). Next turns: the root-cause report, MeitY's investigation, and Google's decision on the remaining root.

### What's happening
- eMudhra is a Bengaluru certificate authority: one of the companies your browser trusts to vouch that a website is real (the padlock). In India its digital signature certificates are used to file on GST, MCA and income-tax portals (Inc42).
- On Sat 17 Oct a researcher reported that the private keys behind eight eMudhra intermediate certificates could be reconstructed from the public certificates. They were small whole numbers: 4, 56, 66, 115, 155, 156, 230 and 237 (Inc42; The Register). Scott Helme recovered them on his laptop in less than a second (The Register).
- Browsers moved fast: Firefox blocked them within hours, then Chrome, Apple and Microsoft (Inc42, OfficeChai). Google is removing the root 'emSign ECC Root CA – G3' from the Chrome Root Store on 27 Oct and reviewing the rest (Inc42). About 30,000 customer certificates have to move (OfficeChai).
- The stock: 20% lower circuit on Wed 21 Oct, ₹540 to ₹432 on the NSE; market value from about ₹4,470 crore to about ₹3,580 crore, roughly ₹900 crore in one session (OfficeChai). BSE low ₹431.55 (Inc42).
- eMudhra's side: a flawed script in a key-generation ceremony on 30 April 2025 made a second set of weak keys that staff used by mistake; nothing was stolen, no data breached, none of the eight issued certificates to end users; the affected global TLS business is about 1% of revenue (Inc42). MeitY says the impact on Indian sites was very limited and has formed an investigation team; CERT-In warned the code-signing keys could make malware look trusted (Inc42).

### The curiosity gap
- The company whose job is guarding the internet's padlock used secret keys like 4 and 56. A proper key is a number over 100 digits long. How does a listed company end up with a password a child could guess?
- The business that broke is about 1% of eMudhra's revenue, but the share fell 20% in a day. Why does a 1% mistake cost a fifth of the company?
- The same company signs the digital signatures people use to file GST and income-tax returns. The government says those weren't affected. So what exactly did investors run from?
