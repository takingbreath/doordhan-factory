## 1. Swiggy fell as much as 17% on Friday, its worst day ever, to a record low near ₹180, less than half its ₹390 IPO price, as Instamart's loss widened to ₹895 crore

Window: Sun 25 Oct to about Wed 28 Oct. Markets reopen Mon 26 Oct; the next turn is whether the fall continues and what Swiggy's management says.

### What's happening
- Swiggy reported Q2 FY27 results after market hours on Thu 22 Oct. The consolidated net loss narrowed to ₹922 crore from ₹1,092 crore a year earlier, but rose from ₹792 crore the quarter before. Revenue rose 36% to ₹7,571 crore (BusinessLine; CNBC-TV18).
- Food delivery is the profit engine: adjusted EBITDA hit a record ₹341 crore, up from ₹240 crore (BusinessLine).
- Instamart, the quick-commerce arm, is the problem. Its adjusted EBITDA loss widened to ₹895 crore from ₹849 crore a year earlier and ₹778 crore in June (BusinessLine; India Today).
- Blinkit, by contrast: order value ₹20,940 crore, more than three times Instamart's ₹6,594 crore, with an adjusted EBITDA profit of ₹184 crore. It has 2,751 dark stores against Instamart's 1,240. Blinkit added 308 stores in the quarter, Instamart 69 (BusinessLine).
- Swiggy's own letter to shareholders: 'Competitive intensity in the sector remains high, and we do not expect it to ease in the near term' (BusinessLine).
- The stock on Fri 23 Oct: down as much as 16% after the results (CNBC-TV18, midday, ₹179.7, its biggest one-day fall since February 2025). CNBC-TV18's close report: 'Swiggy plunged 17%, marking its biggest single-day fall'. Earlier snapshots differed: India Today had an intraday low of ₹184.90 (−12.49%) at 9:50 am. The close report is the newest figure.
- The IPO price was ₹390 (CNBC-TV18). At ₹179.7 the stock is about 54% below it (our arithmetic). It was down more than 50% in 2026 by Friday, while Eternal (Zomato) is up 7% this year (CNBC-TV18). About ₹9,000 crore of market value went in a day (Economic Times).

## 2. The RBI Governor says an upper-layer company like Tata Sons 'is required to list'. India's best-known private company may have to sell shares to the public, and its owners are split

Hot lane (new; RBI, famous name) · HOT · **8/10** (momentum 2/3, stake 1/1, shareability 1/2, gap 2/2, known name 2/2) · tracker id `tata-sons-listing`

Window: Sun 25 Oct to about Sun 8 Nov. Next turns: the Tata Sons board's response, and any RBI deadline.

### What's happening
- At the NDTV World Summit on Fri 23 Oct, RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra said: 'As long as it is an NBFC within our definition, it is a CIC, it has access to funds, direct or indirect, it's required to be registered, and if it is in the upper layer, then it is required to list' (Millennium Post, PTI).
- He named Tata Sons as an example of a large CIC that must register 'because of the systemic risks', but declined to rule on it specifically ('We do not answer questions relating to any specific regulated entity', The Week).
- Background: Tata Sons was put in the RBI's upper layer in September 2022, which gives three years to list. In March 2024 it repaid its debt and applied to surrender its registration. Last month (September 2026) the RBI rejected that application (Millennium Post; Goodreturns).
- The owners are split. The Tata Trusts collectively hold a majority and have said they have not agreed to a listing; Noel Tata opposes one. The Shapoorji Pallonji group holds about 18.4% and backs the RBI's directive (Outlook Business, 21 Oct).
- The SP group wants Tata Sons to review a plan to unlock at least ₹25,000 crore from its stake to repay debt (Outlook Business, citing ET).
- Tata Sons owns the group's companies, among them TCS, Tata Motors, Tata Steel and Tata Power (Goodreturns).

### The curiosity gap
- Tata is the most trusted name in Indian business, and it has never been listed. Why is the RBI forcing the holding company onto the stock market, and why are the Tatas fighting it?
- A listed Tata Sons would let anyone buy a piece of the parent of TCS and Tata Motors. Who gains, and why are Ratan Tata's trusts against it?

## 3. Jio lists on Wednesday. Its biggest revenue stream is sold through one distributor, its own sister company Reliance Retail, and the Jio brand belongs to RIL

Deep lane (via The Ken, 22 Oct; for Wednesday's Jio listing) · HOT · **8/10** (momentum 1/3, stake 1/1, shareability 2/2, gap 2/2, known name 2/2) · tracker id `jio-reliance-retail-ken`

Window: Sun 25 Oct to Wed 28 Oct (listing day), then about a week. Allotment Mon 26 Oct.

### What's happening
- The Ken (22 Oct): 'Jio and Reliance Retail are tied at the hip. That's fine as long as the Ambanis are one big happy family.' Headline only; we did not read past the paywall. The facts below are from the RHP as reported in open coverage.
- Reliance Retail is the sole distributor of Jio's prepaid service, which was 77% (₹1.13 lakh crore) of Jio's ₹1.47 lakh crore consolidated revenue in FY26 (Fortune India, from the RHP, 15 Oct; context, older than a week).
- Airtel, by contrast, sells through 8,863 independent dealers and distributors, none owned by Airtel (Fortune India, citing Airtel's FY26 annual report).
- Reliance Retail also signs up customers, does identity checks, collects payments and handles marketing. Jio paid ₹4,374 crore of selling and distribution expenses to group companies in FY26 (Fortune India).
- Related parties supplied about 17.5% of Jio's FY26 capex excluding spectrum, and 36% in the June 2026 quarter. Group firms own about 1.74 lakh of the 3.6 lakh towers Jio uses and supply about 98% of its fibre (Fortune India).
- The Jio trademark belongs to RIL, which licenses it to Jio and currently charges no royalty (Fortune India).
- The RHP itself flags the risk: if the arrangement broke down, Jio would have to build its own distributor network across India (Fortune India).
- This week: the IPO closed on Fri 23 Oct with more than 1.35 crore applications, the first IPO anywhere past 1 crore (Business Today, 23 Oct, sent yesterday). Allotment Mon 26 Oct, listing Wed 28 Oct (tracked as jio-ipo).

### The curiosity gap
- 1.35 crore people applied for Jio. How many know that 77% of its revenue comes through one shop, and that shop is owned by the same family?
- The Jio name isn't Jio's: RIL owns the brand and charges nothing for it, for now. What happens to Jio's profits if that ever changes?
