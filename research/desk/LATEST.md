## 1. Tata Sons' board will take up whether India's biggest group goes public on Mon 30 Nov, after Noel Tata got the 26 Nov meeting pushed back and the RBI told Tata Sons to follow its listing rule

Hot lane (new turn on a story retired on 8 Nov: the RBI's 16 Nov letter and the board meeting moved to Mon 30 Nov) · FLAT · **8/10** (momentum 1/3, stake 1/1, shareability 2/2, gap 2/2, known name 2/2) · tracker id `tata-sons-board-nov30`

Window: Post by Sun 29 Nov, before the board meets Mon 30 Nov; the result is a ready follow-up that day. Tata Sons must also hold its delayed AGM by 31 Dec.

### What's happening
- Tata Sons postponed its board meeting from 26 to 30 Nov after Noel Tata objected that directors had not got the seven days' notice the company's practice requires; the papers were uploaded on 22 Nov, four days ahead (Tribune/ANI, 23 Nov; BigGo Finance, 24 Nov).
- The RBI's letter of 16 Nov, replying to a Tata Sons letter of 6 Oct, told the company to 'comply with all applicable regulations/guidelines/directions' for upper-layer NBFCs, 'including the requirement of listing by your company' (Tribune/ANI).
- The rule: an upper-layer NBFC must list within three years of being identified (paragraph 43 of the 2025 NBFC governance directions). Tata Sons was first classified as one in September 2022 (Tribune/ANI), so the three years ran out in September 2025.
- Agenda for 30 Nov: a proposal to call an extraordinary general meeting, the half-year results, and a review of loss-making businesses including Air India (BigGo Finance).
- The fight: the board voted 4-1 on 17 Sep to reappoint N Chandrasekaran for five years; Noel Tata has written seven letters since, calling it illegal, and opposes listing. The Sir Ratan Tata Trust alone holds 23.56% of Tata Sons (BigGo Finance).
- Context only (older than a week): ICICI Direct estimated in March 2024 that a 5% float would make a Rs 55,000 crore IPO on a Rs 11 lakh crore valuation, India's largest ever; Tata Trusts own 65.9% and the Mistry family 18.4%.

### The curiosity gap
- India's most trusted business name is being told by the RBI to list, and the man who chairs its biggest owner doesn't want it to. Why would anyone refuse what would be India's biggest IPO?
- The deadline ran out in September 2025 and Tata Sons is still private. What does the RBI actually do to a company that ignores a listing rule?
- If it lists, ordinary investors could own a piece of the company that owns TCS, Tata Motors and Titan. What would that piece cost, and who decides on Monday?

## 2. SEBI is prosecuting Ketan Parekh, the broker of the 2001 market scam, for not paying back Rs 27 crore from a front-running case, days after a court refused him a 91-day holiday in nine countries

### What's happening
- On Fri 20 Nov the special SEBI court in Mumbai refused Ketan Parekh's plea to travel for 91 days (20 Dec to 20 Mar) to nine countries: Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia, the UAE, Norway, Sweden, Finland, Denmark and Switzerland (BusinessToday, 23 Nov).
- Special judge RM Jadhav: it 'would not be expedient in the national interest and economy of the country' to allow it. Parekh's earlier permitted trips ran six to twenty days (BusinessToday).

The offence carries one month to 10 years in jail, a fine of up to Rs 25 crore, or both (The Open Press, 24 Nov).
