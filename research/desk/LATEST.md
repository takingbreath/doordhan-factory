## 3. A draft CAG audit found 81% of India's 28,865 hallmark-licensed jewellers weren't inspected in 2024-25, while a newspaper sting got about 20-carat gold hallmarked as 22-carat

Deep lane (Moneylife, 22 Nov; draft CAG audit; Dainik Bhaskar's rhenium sting) · HOT · **8/10** (momentum 3/3, stake 1/1, shareability 2/2, gap 1/2, known name 1/2) · tracker id `bis-hallmark-cag`

Window: To about 4 Dec (tracker window). It turns again if BIS responds or the final CAG report is tabled.

### What's happening
- A draft audit by the Comptroller and Auditor General found about 81% of licensed jewellers did not get their prescribed annual inspection in 2024-25: 5,444 of 28,865 were inspected. Over five years, 83.25% were left out (Aryan Age, 21 Nov).
- The same draft says BIS fell 43.7% short of its monitoring target for the Assaying and Hallmarking Centres where purity is tested; BIS cited staff shortages and remote areas, and CAG rejected that (Aryan Age; Gulf News, 20 Nov).
- The sting (Dainik Bhaskar, reported by Gulf News): 1 g of rhenium mixed into 7.83 g of 24-carat gold; the finished 9.58 g ornament was hallmarked 22-carat. Estimated real gold about 81.73% (about 20-carat) against the 91.6% that 22-carat requires; the showroom machine read 93.70% and found no rhenium. Estimated loss about ₹14,700 per tola at ₹1.5 lakh gold.
- Moneylife published its own investigation on Sun 22 Nov: 'Is your hallmarked gold really 22-carat?'
- Caveat in the coverage: the rhenium result is one sample; it doesn't show hallmarked gold in general falls short.

### The curiosity gap
- The hallmark is the one thing Indians trust when they buy gold. Who checks the people who stamp it? In 2024-25, for four jewellers in five, nobody did.
- A ₹1.5 lakh tola of 'hallmarked 22-carat' gold can carry about ₹14,700 less gold, and the showroom machine can't tell. How?
- Gold just fell 33% from January, and everyone is talking about buying the dip. Is the dip even gold?
