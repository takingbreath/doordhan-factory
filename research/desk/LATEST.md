## 1. Jio's IPO: India's biggest ever, priced well below the hype

Hot lane · HOT · **8/10** (momentum 3/3, stake 2/2, shareability 1/2, gap 1/2, clarity 1/1) · tracker id `jio-ipo`

Window: Live until listing (before 30 Oct). Next news beats: the price band, the opening (week of 19 Oct), listing day.

### What's happening
- Reliance's digital arm, Jio Platforms, plans to list on the stock market before 30 October. The issue may open in the week of 19 October.
- On 6 October Bloomberg reported that Jio will seek about ₹11 lakh crore ($114 billion). At one stage it had been valued at $130-170 billion, and a report two days earlier still said $143-146 billion. The reports link the lower price to the market sell-off that has hit IPO pricing, including NSE's downsized listing.
- It is up to 27 crore new shares (about 2.93% of the company), worth about $3.4 billion (₹32,000-32,500 crore) at that valuation. All of it is new money, mostly to repay debt.
- It is still India's biggest IPO. The record is Hyundai Motor India's ₹27,870 crore (Oct 2024); LIC raised ₹21,000 crore (2022).
- At $114 billion Jio would be India's third most valuable listed company, after Reliance and Bharti Airtel.
- The business: 50.6 crore mobile customers (39% of the market). FY26 profit after tax ₹30,053 crore (+15%), revenue ₹1,46,885 crore (+14.5%).
- Reliance shareholders get a reserved quota, per the draft prospectus. The record date isn't announced.

### The curiosity gap
- Everyone pays Jio every month, and almost nobody knows what the company earns from them. The ₹30,000 crore profit is the reveal: a company you top up every month makes that much.
- Why did India's biggest IPO get cheaper before it even listed? Talk of $170 billion became $114 billion. Business Today's short on the cut is its best-performing Jio video (31.6× the channel's usual).
- It's a brand everyone knows, in today's news. That's the shape of both our breakouts (Policybazaar, Snapdeal).

## 3. NSE warns: your Nasdaq ETF can cost far more than what's inside it

Deep lane (via Reuters) · COLD · **6/10** (momentum 0/3, stake 2/2, shareability 2/2, gap 2/2, clarity 0/1) · tracker id `intl-etf-premium`

Window: Evergreen for weeks; tracked until 22 Oct.

### What's happening
- On 7 October NSE told investors to be careful with some international ETFs. They trade at substantial premiums to their NAV (the value of what they hold). BSE warned the same on 25 September.
- Why: Indian mutual funds have an industry-wide $7 billion limit on overseas investment and a separate $1 billion limit for overseas ETFs. Both are full, so no new units can be created, and demand pushes the price above what the units hold.
- The premium averaged 15-20% over the past year and jumped from early September. Motilal Oswal NASDAQ Q50 ETF traded at a 235% premium on 18 Sep (context, older than a week).
- The risk: the premium can collapse, for example if the limits are raised or the ETF price-band rules change from 1 April 2027. The Nasdaq can rise while the ETF falls.

### The curiosity gap
- How do you lose money on the Nasdaq when the Nasdaq goes up? Buyers think they own Apple and Nvidia. Above NAV, they partly paid for a place in a queue.
- Why can't Indian funds just buy more US shares? Because a $7 billion cap is full. Most viewers have never heard of it.
- The herd angle: everyone piled into US tech and paid extra for the privilege.
