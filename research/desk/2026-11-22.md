## 1. Silver has crashed about 65% from January's ₹4.2 lakh record to under ₹1.5 lakh a kg, while Indians bought 57% more silver ETFs in October than in September

## 2. IndusInd Bank fired its former CEO Sumant Kathpalia and will claw back his pay back to March 2020, after the ₹1,960 crore derivatives hole that cost shareholders 27% in a day

Hot lane (new; Moneylife and the exchange filing, Fri 20 Nov) · RISING · **9/10** (momentum 2/3, stake 1/1, shareability 2/2, gap 2/2, known name 2/2) · tracker id `indusind-kathpalia-clawback`

Window: This week: Monday's trade is the first reaction, and the amount to be recovered has not been disclosed yet (a second turn when it is).

### What's happening
- IndusInd Bank's board, acting as the disciplinary authority, terminated former MD and CEO Sumant Kathpalia's employment with immediate effect at its meeting on Fri 20 Nov, and approved the invocation of malus and claw-back provisions with effect from 24 March 2020, the date he was first appointed MD and CEO (exchange filing, reported by ANI/Tribune and ETV Bharat).
- The decision follows an inquiry report dated 18 Nov 2026 by a former Bombay High Court judge. The filing gave no amount to be recovered (Tribune; ETV Bharat).
- Background: Kathpalia resigned as MD and CEO on 29 April 2025, taking moral responsibility for accounting discrepancies in the derivatives portfolio, but the board kept him on as an employee 'until further intimation'; he was suspended from 18 June 2025 (Tribune).
- The hole: the bank disclosed the derivatives discrepancies on 10 March 2025 and its shares fell about 27%; Grant Thornton put the cumulative hit at ₹1,959.98 crore, and the bank posted its worst-ever quarterly loss of about ₹2,329 crore for Q4 FY25 (context, 2025 reports). In May 2025 SEBI's interim order asked five senior officials, Kathpalia among them, to disgorge about ₹20 crore for alleged insider trading (context).
- Malus and claw-back let a bank withhold unpaid and take back already-paid variable pay; RBI's pay rules for bank CEOs require them. A YouTube channel notes Friday was IndusInd's strongest intraday move since February 2021 (Nifty data from yesterday's desk: +8.59%), but no page we opened ties the rise to this news.

### The curiosity gap
- In India, top bosses who leave after a scandal usually keep the money. Here a bank wants six years of its ex-CEO's pay back. How much, and can it actually get it?

## 3. EPFO will let retirees take their PF as a monthly income while the rest keeps earning 8.25%, cut the minimum balance to 10% and the final-withdrawal wait from 12 months to 2

Rule (hot on YouTube; The Ken, 21 Nov, 'EPFO to let you keep your PF with it and draw a monthly income. Ask how it earns the rate first') · HOT · **7/10** (momentum 3/3, stake 1/1, shareability 1/2, gap 0/2, known name 2/2) · tracker id `epfo-swp-pf-pension`

Window: About a week, until EPFO notifies the rules and a start date (none announced); then it is news again.

### What's happening
- EPFO's Central Board of Trustees, at its 240th meeting on 19 Nov, approved a voluntary Systematic Withdrawal Plan: retiring members can take their PF in monthly, quarterly or half-yearly instalments instead of one lump sum (Business Today; Onmanorama, 20 Nov).
- The balance left with EPFO keeps earning the EPF rate, 8.25% now. Options: the whole corpus in instalments, part lump sum and the rest in instalments, or only the interest while the principal stays. If the member dies, the nominee can take the rest or keep the instalments (Onmanorama).
- About 6.32 lakh members over 55 with more than ₹5 lakh in PF are expected to benefit first (Business Today; Onmanorama).
- Also approved: the minimum PF balance that must stay falls from 25% to 10% (members under 55 can take up to 90%), and the wait for final withdrawal after losing a job falls from 12 months to 2 (Business Today). No operational date has been announced.
- The rate angle: 8.25% on PF against SBI's one-year FD at about 6.25% and October retail inflation at 6.09% (context, 12-15 Nov). The Ken's 21 Nov piece (paywalled, headline only) asks how EPFO earns that rate.

### The curiosity gap
- Your PF can now pay you like a pension at 8.25%, when a bank FD pays about 6.25% and inflation is 6.09%. How does EPFO afford a rate the banks don't pay?
- You can soon take out 90% of your PF instead of 75%. Is that a gift, or an invitation to empty your retirement?
