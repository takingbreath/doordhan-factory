# Playbook: produce (after the script is approved)

_This is the path that has worked so far. Change it whenever you find a better way, and note what you changed in registry/episodes.md._

Work in work/<episode-slug>/. Outputs go to outputs/<episode-slug>/.

1. Shot list: map every approved line to a shot. The template's shot pattern is a starting point, not a script. Default: talking shots on Wan 2.7 (close-ups), scenes without dialogue on Seedance 2.5 or local stills and cards. Choose differently when you have a reason. Reusable clips (registry "clips", e.g. Sarika's tagline ident) cost nothing.
2. Cost plan: estimate credits per shot with the rates in registry `_costs_credits`, or preflight with `get_cost: true` on the generate call (free). Over the budget in CLAUDE.md: ask first.
3. Voices: generate each line. Sarika and Gupta: Higgsfield `generate_audio` with text2speech_v2, variant elevenlabs, voice_type element and the registry voice_id (about 0.15 credits a short line). Voices that live on ElevenLabs: `tts_elevenlabs.py --voice <name>`. Then prep_line.py (tempo 1.15 for anchors and presenters; --phone for callers). Check the printed duration, loudness (-16) and pauses; check the words before spending video credits on a mispronounced line: with the ElevenLabs connector, creative_create_asset_upload → put_upload.py → creative_finalize_asset_upload (with a flow_id) → creative_transcribe_audio (eleven_scribe_v1) → creative_get_flow_run_status; or with an API key, `transcribe.py raw.mp3 --expect "<line>"`.
4. Tracks: make_track.py per talking shot (lead 0.5 s, because Wan's first second barely moves the lips; start the shot 0.38 s into the clip. Hold 0.1–0.4 s, whole seconds). Trim each line at its last word first. If it says a shorter hold saves a second, take it unless the hold carries a reaction. Upload each track to Higgsfield: media_upload, then pipeline/put_upload.py with the returned URL and content type audio/mpeg, then media_confirm.
5. Keyframes: every character on screen wears a new outfit this episode (the wardrobe rules in the format file; check registry `wardrobe_log`). Generate that outfit's close-up (and desk or location frames), plus any other new ones, with gpt_image_2_5, the style reference and the sheets; for Sarika's wardrobe use seedream_v4_5. Download, view, and check the text, the face against the sheet, and that it reads as a real 90s outfit rather than AI glamour.
6. Video: submit at most 3 jobs at a time. Talking shots: wan2_7 720p, start_image + audio_references. Non-talking: seedance_2_5 480p. Prompts: describe the shot, the performance and "keep the exact analog look of the start image; no new text, no subtitles". Wan acts out every attitude word: for talking shots use the format's steady-performance sentence (Sarika's is in formats/samachar.md), and never "glamorous", "playful", "tilt", or "hair moves". If a preset recommendation comes back, resubmit with `declined_preset_id` (registry style.declined_preset_id; it fired again on the 2026-09-25 test).
7. Download every result and run `qc.py CLIP --track TRACK --box <mouth box> --expect "<line>"`. It includes headcheck: a CHECK there (a wobbling head) means reshoot with the steady-performance prompt; it is the "hot and cheap" failure Akhilesh flagged. Find the mouth box once per keyframe with `lipcheck.py --face-crop` and save it in registry `qc.mouth_box_480`. Then look at the contact sheet and at full-size frames of the face and any text. check_match can't see a wrong-speed track, so also look at mouth frames at a known word against the edit's audio. If something keeps failing, change the approach rather than re-rolling the same prompt; ask Akhilesh when the budget or a creative call is at stake.
8. Graphics: logo cards (inset_card.py), title/montage (montage.py), any slates or SMS inserts (build locally). Always add the hook as a big lower-third headline strip for the first 3 seconds (docs/script-bible.md, "The first 3 seconds"). Use a bold font, and on a cloud machine install fonts with `sudo apt-get install -y fonts-noto-core` so Devanagari renders. Note the cover frame (eye contact plus hook text) in the post kit.
9. Assemble: write work/<slug>/edit.json, run assemble.py. Cut points come from speech_map.py (or transcribe.py word times) on each generated clip. Give montage parts `"lufs": -17` so the sting sits just under the voices; every other part is levelled to -16 automatically. Pick the look in edit.json (`"look": "broadcast"` by default, `"vhs"` for the teleshop); preview any clip first with `look.py CLIP OUT --preset <name>`, which also writes a before/after frame.
10. Hold it to the quality bar in CLAUDE.md: look at the joins sheet, run speech_map on the final (no word cut, no dead gap), check the first frame shows the subject, and check the length is 20–30 seconds. Then present: send the final with SendUserFile (it reaches his phone through Remote Control), with length, credits spent, what you checked, and anything you're unsure about.
11. Log the episode in registry/episodes.md, and each character's outfit in registry `wardrobe_log`. Add its row to the strategy book with `python3 pipeline/scoreboard.py add-reel --id E<n> ... --bets <what it carried> --credits <spent>` (docs/strategy.md); the strategist links it to the Instagram post and judges the bets on it.
