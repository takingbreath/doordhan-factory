# Playbook: produce (after the script is approved)

_The creator format (Rashmi at home, modern look) follows formats/creator.md and assets/edits/nana01_build.py instead of assemble.py's old-TV finish; what makes it read as real is in docs/realism.md._

_This is the path that has worked so far. Change it whenever you find a better way, and note what you changed in registry/episodes.md._

**`/produce-video` runs this playbook end to end** (.claude/skills/produce-video/SKILL.md, with prompts.md and gotchas.md). For a Sahayata Kendra episode the edit and graphics start from `assets/edits/sk03_build.py` and `assets/edits/sk03_gfx.py` (E7). Check a new machine with `python3 pipeline/produce_check.py`.

Work in work/<episode-slug>/. Outputs go to outputs/<episode-slug>/.

1. Shot list: map every approved line to a shot, then plan the shot mix.
   - **Sizes:** one wide, mediums, one close.
   - **Talking share:** no more than **55%** of the runtime a face talking to camera; the rest is cutaways, reactions and inserts without dialogue.
   - **Eyeline rule:** no character looks at an off-frame object. A look at the cat, the phone or anything else is either a cut (he looks off-camera, then a cutaway of the object) or the object is in the same frame with an end image (step 4).
   - **Talking shots** are straight to camera or eyes down, with no object handling; the builder writes "exactly one blink and one small eye shift, nothing more" into every talking prompt.
   - **Continuity:** give each shot its set and framing, so consecutive shots of one framing chain.

   Then: The template's shot pattern is a starting point, not a script. Default: talking shots on Wan 2.7 (close-ups), scenes without dialogue on Seedance 2.5 or local stills and cards. Choose differently when you have a reason. Reusable clips (registry "clips", e.g. Sarika's tagline ident) cost nothing.
2. Cost plan: estimate credits per shot with the rates in registry `_costs_credits`, or preflight with `get_cost: true` on the generate call (free). Over the budget in CLAUDE.md: ask first.
3. Voices: generate each line over the ElevenLabs API, the default (Akhilesh, 2026-09-30): `tts_elevenlabs.py --voice <name> --text-file LINE.txt --out raw.mp3` for every character whose registry entry has an `elevenlabs_voice_id` (Sarika, Gupta and Pandey do). The key is in the git-ignored `.env`; a session without one uses the connector's `creative_generate_speech` with the same voice id. A character with no ElevenLabs voice stays on Higgsfield: `generate_audio` with text2speech_v2, variant elevenlabs, voice_type element and the registry voice_id (about 0.15 credits a short line). Sarika's and Gupta's Higgsfield elements are kept in the registry under `higgsfield_fallback`. Then prep_line.py (tempo 1.15 for anchors and presenters; --phone for callers). Check the printed duration, loudness (-16) and pauses; check the words before spending video credits on a mispronounced line: `transcribe.py raw.mp3 --expect "<line>"` (scribe_v2, with word times); or, in a session without the key, the ElevenLabs connector: creative_create_asset_upload → put_upload.py → creative_finalize_asset_upload (with a flow_id) → creative_transcribe_audio (eleven_scribe_v1) → creative_get_flow_run_status. Then `verify_line.py LINE.wav --script "<line as sent, with tags>" --transcript "<Scribe's text>" --episode work/<slug>/lines.json --line-id <id> [--cap 0 for formats without a cap]` on every line, and rerun the voice until it passes: must-say numbers and terms heard, no tag spoken aloud, last word intact and not hard-cut, no sound after the last word (it prints where to trim), lead silence, and the running reel estimate against the cap. It is free; nothing video-side starts before every line passes.
4. Tracks and shot specs: `shot_spec.py build --line LINE.wav --id <id> --keyframe <id> --character <name> --set <set> --framing <setup> [--chain-from <previous shot's spec>] --acting "<one note per beat>" --out work/<slug>/specs` does this step. The builder enforces the continuity rules:
   - **Chaining:** a shot of the same character, set and framing as the one before starts from that clip's last clean frame (mouth closed, eyes open, preflight pose and size, identity vs the sheet); judge resets it to the keyframe if face_consistency fails.
   - **Looks and reaches:** `--look-at` or `--reach-for` with `--target-side` and `--target-in-frame` asks for an end image (make it, then `shot_spec.py endcheck SPEC END.png`), and refuses an off-frame target.
   - **Moves:** `--end-pose` (a change of position) needs start and end images.
   - **No-dialogue shots:** `--silent SECONDS` builds a cutaway, reaction or insert.

   Tracks in detail: the build and writes the generate_video params: it trims a tail sound, splits lines over 6 s of speech at a pause (talking shots are capped at about 6 s: Wan drifts about -8 ms/s, and E5's 11 s shot crossed the lip-sync limit at 9 s; pass --allow-long only where lipsync_lag has passed longer shots of this character and framing), adds the 0.5 s lead-in, the still-start sentence and 0.3 s of headroom. By hand: make_track.py per talking shot (lead 0.5 s, because Wan's first second barely moves the lips; start the shot 0.38 s into the clip. Hold 0.1–0.4 s, whole seconds). Trim each line at its last word first. If it says a shorter hold saves a second, take it unless the hold carries a reaction. Upload each track to Higgsfield: media_upload, then pipeline/put_upload.py with the returned URL and content type audio/mpeg, then media_confirm.
5. Keyframes: run `preflight_keyframe.py KF.png --profile <character>` on every talking keyframe (face and mouth size, head pose: pitch over 18 degrees or roll over 10 warned, mouth closed for a still start, Pandey's heavy lids; `--ocr X Y W H --expect "TEXT"` on any board or logo) and `face_consistency.py SHEET.png KF.png` against the character sheet (PASS >= 0.55). Every character on screen wears a new outfit this episode (the wardrobe rules in the format file; check registry `wardrobe_log`). Generate that outfit's close-up (and desk or location frames), plus any other new ones, with gpt_image_2_5, the style reference and the sheets; for Sarika's wardrobe use seedream_v4_5. Download, view, and check the text, the face against the sheet, and that it reads as a real 90s outfit rather than AI glamour.
6. Video: submit at most 3 jobs at a time. Talking shots: wan2_7 720p, start_image + audio_references. Non-talking: seedance_2_5 480p. Prompts: describe the shot, the performance and "keep the exact analog look of the start image; no new text, no subtitles". Wan acts out every attitude word: for talking shots use the format's steady-performance sentence (Sarika's is in formats/samachar.md), and never "glamorous", "playful", "tilt", or "hair moves". If a preset recommendation comes back, resubmit with `declined_preset_id` (registry style.declined_preset_id; it fired again on the 2026-09-25 test).
7. Download every result and run `shot_spec.py judge SPEC.json CLIP.mp4`: it runs lipsync_lag.py (SyncNet, with a landmark-correlation fallback) and decides ACCEPT, ACCEPT_WITH_SHIFT (a constant offset: put its audio_shift_ms on the part in edit.json), one automatic RETRY (weak lips: stronger articulation; drift: a split), then ASK. Also run `qc.py CLIP --track TRACK --box <mouth box> --expect "<line>"`. It includes headcheck: a CHECK there (a wobbling head) means reshoot with the steady-performance prompt; it is the "hot and cheap" failure Akhilesh flagged. Find the mouth box once per keyframe with `lipcheck.py --face-crop` and save it in registry `qc.mouth_box_480`. Then look at the contact sheet and at full-size frames of the face and any text. check_match can't see a wrong-speed track, so also look at mouth frames at a known word against the edit's audio. If something keeps failing, change the approach rather than re-rolling the same prompt; ask Akhilesh when the budget or a creative call is at stake.
8. Graphics: logo cards (inset_card.py), title/montage (montage.py), any slates or SMS inserts (build locally). Always add the hook as a big lower-third headline strip for the first 3 seconds (docs/script-bible.md, "The first 3 seconds"). Use a bold font, and on a cloud machine install fonts with `sudo apt-get install -y fonts-noto-core` so Devanagari renders. Note the cover frame (eye contact plus hook text) in the post kit.
9. Assemble: write work/<slug>/edit.json, run assemble.py. Finish defaults (2026-09-29):
   - VHS motion at 0.65 under the look;
   - voice room on every part with sound (`"room": false` on music, stings, montage, a phone caller, or a mix treated in its own build);
   - recorded office ambience at −36 dB (`"ambience": null` if the build has its own bed).

   It also writes `<out>_prefinish.mkv`, which the gate uses for lip sync. A 67 s reel takes about 6 minutes. Then: Cut points come from speech_map.py (or transcribe.py word times) on each generated clip. Give montage parts `"lufs": -17` so the sting sits just under the voices; every other part is levelled to -16 automatically. Pick the look in edit.json (`"look": "broadcast"` by default, `"vhs"` for the teleshop); preview any clip first with `look.py CLIP OUT --preset <name>`, which also writes a before/after frame.
10. Look at it yourself: the joins sheet, the first frame shows the subject, speech_map on the final (no word cut, no dead gap), the length against the format (20–30 s for news formats; Sahayata Kendra has no 30 s cap). The build writes work/<slug>/qc_manifest.json (assets/edits/sk03_build.py's `write_qc_manifest` is the example: every line's placement, its talking segments and clip spans, the character sheets, the dialogue stem, the repairs file and the rebuild command); an assemble.py-only edit writes it by hand, giving talking parts a `"part"` index so offset repairs go into edit.json.
11. **Last step, every time: the QC gate.** No final reaches Akhilesh or Naveen without it. Get Scribe's transcript of the exact final (`transcribe.py`, or the connector route, as in step 3), `python3 pipeline/qc_gate.py bind-transcript FINAL.mp4 <text file>`, then `python3 pipeline/qc_gate.py work/<slug>/qc_manifest.json`. It runs, in order: the script diff against the frozen approved script, lipsync_lag on every talking segment of the pre-finish edit (before the VHS pass and the look, which lower SyncNet's confidence) against the reel's audio, face_consistency of each talking clip against its character sheet, verify_line's end / tail / lead checks on each line as placed in the mix, loudness (target ±1 LU, true peak ≤ -1 dBTP) and length/format. A constant lip-sync offset, a hard-cut line end and a wrong lead are repaired automatically, once: it writes the repairs file, rebuilds and re-checks (if the repair changed the sound, bind a fresh transcript of the rebuilt final and run it again; that rerun does no further repairs). Anything else blocks, and the summary names the check, the clip in work/<slug>/qc/ and the fix: fix it and run the gate again, or ask Akhilesh; only his (or Naveen's) say-so waives a row: `qc_gate.py waive FINAL lipsync:P6 --by "Akhilesh: <his words>"`.
    Present only on PASS: send the final with SendUserFile together with `<final>_qc.png` (the one-screen summary: every check pass, repaired or failed), plus length, credits spent and anything you're unsure about. The gate is enforced, not advisory: a video under outputs/ without a PASS stamp for that exact file is refused by the SendUserFile hook (.claude/settings.json) and by put_upload.py (the emailed Higgsfield link). Changing the file after the gate voids the stamp.

After he approves (not part of producing): log the episode in registry/episodes.md, and each character's outfit in registry `wardrobe_log`. Add its row to the strategy book with `python3 pipeline/scoreboard.py add-reel --id E<n> ... --bets <what it carried> --credits <spent>` (docs/strategy.md); the strategist links it to the Instagram post and judges the bets on it.
