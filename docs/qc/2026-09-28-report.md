## 1. lipsync_lag.py
**Method.**
- **Primary:** SyncNet (pretrained syncnet_v2), 2 s windows hopping 1 s.
- **Fallback:** mouth openness from MediaPipe Face Landmarker (inner-lip gap / mouth width), cross-correlated with `common.envelope` over ±500 ms.
- **Sign:** + means the sound leads.
- **Verdict:** FAIL if a confident window is outside −125..+45 ms. A window counts as confident if SyncNet's confidence is at least 1.5, a face is found in at least 80% of its frames, and at least 40% of it is speech.
- **Calibration checks:**
  - shifting a clip's audio by ±200 ms moved both methods by exactly ±200 ms;
  - SyncNet resolves 40 ms steps (one frame at 25 fps);
  - the fallback agrees with SyncNet to within 80 ms on 74% of readings (median gap 37 ms) and gave no reading on 8 of 43.

**Findings.**

**Wan's baseline** (29 clips checked against their own audio): offset median −40 ms (range −160 to 0); **drift median −8.5 ms/s** (interquartile range −12.7 to −3.3). So a talking shot that starts at −40 to −80 ms crosses −125 ms after roughly 6–10 s. That's the evidence behind the 6 s cap.

**Repair path.**
- **Constant pattern:** the tool prints the fix as `audio_shift_ms` (shift = measured offset + 40, landing mid-window). `assemble.py` applies it per part. For a custom edit like the Kendra's build.py, the same fix is the clip's `src_in`. Tested on today's a488b60a: −160/−200 ms became −40/−120 ms, PASS.
- **Drift:** it's reported with "split the line", because no shift can fix it.
- **Weak lips:** a steep "drift" in a clip judged against its own audio, or low confidence. It gets "regenerate from a tighter keyframe".

**Recommended thresholds:** keep −125/+45 ms as specified. With 40 ms steps that means −120 passes and −160 fails, +40 passes and +80 fails. Against your judgement it caught everything you noticed and raised no false alarm on material you approved, apart from the mild v4 P4 tail.

- **P6 ("…ka?") ends in a hard cut** (the level only 4.3 dB below speech at the file end). P2's file too. C1, P3 and P4 end without a fade (WARN, 9–11 dB). Our tight trims clip the last vowel's decay. Nobody noticed on a TV-speaker mix, but it's a real defect: re-export untrimmed, or add a 30 ms fade.
- **Duration:** E5 comes out at an estimated 57 s against a 30 s cap (the Kendra runs with `--cap 0`). The estimate crosses 30 s at P3.
- **Recommended thresholds:**
  - end drop below 6 dB = FAIL, 6–12 dB = WARN (good lines 22–58 dB; the cut line −1 dB);
  - a tail is a 60–400 ms sound at least 150 ms after the last word that's quiet, or cut off by the end of the file;
  - lead 0–0.35 s for raw lines, 0.4–0.6 s for Wan tracks.

## 4. preflight_keyframe.py
Calibrated on 14 E5 keyframes and 31 clip start frames (`keyframe_calibration.txt`).
- **Face size doesn't separate good lips from weak ones in our range.** All our talking faces are 16–33% of the frame height, and the weak-lips Gupta frame (20%) matches one that synced fine. So face and mouth size are floors: warn below 16% / 60 px, fail below 12% / 45 px.
- **Head pose matters more.** The two E5 v2 lip-sync fails started pitched down 29° with a −15° roll (the two-shot) or turned −17° (T4v2). Thresholds: pitch warn 18 / fail 26; roll 10 / 16; yaw 20 / 32. The E5 v1 three-quarter frames at −29° yaw with a level head synced fine.
- **Pandey's heavy lids:** lid closure was 0.00–0.02 on the "lifeless" v1 keyframes and 0.09–0.19 on the approved ones, so the rule is ≥ 0.06. The v1 front keyframe **FAILs** and the approved one **PASSes**: your "lifeless" note would have been caught before any video.
- **OCR:** reads the new board ("…DOORDHAN -SAHAYATA KENDRA", match 0.83, PASS) and fails the old "Grahak hi Bhagwan hai" master (0.28).
- **Limitation:** E4's weak-lips Gupta frame passes preflight. Nothing in a still predicts that failure; only lipsync_lag on the clip catches it.

## 5. face_consistency.py
AuraFace-v1 embeddings (Apache 2.0; InsightFace's buffalo weights are non-commercial, so not used). Calibrated on Pandey, Sarika and Gupta across sheets, keyframes and clips (`face_consistency_calibration.txt`):
- **Same character:** 0.66–0.93 (45 pairs).
- **Different characters:** 0.10–0.34 (75 pairs).
- **Thresholds:** PASS ≥ 0.55, WARN 0.45–0.55, FAIL < 0.45. The worst frame of a clip counts.
- **Side finding:** the field reporter in E3, E4 and today is the same person (0.73–0.81), so Gupta has stayed consistent.
- **Caveat:** our material has no example of a character's face drifting mid-clip, so the FAIL side is calibrated on different characters, not on drift.

## 6. shot_spec.py
- **`build`**, tested on E5 lines:
  - P2 gets its tail trimmed automatically and splits at the "Loan hai?" pause (6.05 s + 2.65 s of speech);
  - P4 splits 5.4 + 5.45 s;
  - P6 stays one 6 s shot.
  - Every spec carries the 0.5 s lead, the still-start sentence, 0.3 s of headroom and `edit_src_in_s: 0.38`.
- **`judge`**, on real clips (`shot_spec_judge.txt`):
  - in sync → ACCEPT;
  - constant offset → ACCEPT_WITH_SHIFT −140 ms;
  - weak lips → RETRY (stronger articulation), then ASK on a second failure.
  - Drift on a line longer than 3.5 s → RETRY_SPLIT.
- **Template rule:** talking shots are capped at about 6 s of speech (CLAUDE.md, both format files, produce.md). `--allow-long` only where lipsync_lag has passed longer shots of that character and framing.
