# Script bible

Akhilesh is the head writer and has the final say. You write the drafts, and you're free to surprise him. Give him correct facts, a tight structure,
real options, and a Hinglish draft he can punch up in two minutes. Clarity beats cleverness; a clever idea is welcome if it passes the two-second test.

## What to send at the script step (one message)
1. The story spine in English, 5 beats (hook / why care / evidence / turn / payoff), one line each.
2. Three hook options (A/B/C) and two ending options (X/Y), in English.
3. The full Hinglish script for your recommended combination, with speaker tags and delivery tags, e.g.
   **ANCHOR** [crisp]: ...
4. Word count and estimated duration (about 2.9 spoken words per second after the 1.15 tempo). Target 20–30 seconds in total, never over 30: about 50–75 spoken words plus the 2.5 s montage.
5. Fact box: every number or claim, with its source link.
6. Anything that needs his decision (length over cap, compliance edge).
7. Wardrobe: one line per on-screen character with this episode's outfit (new every episode; see the format file), e.g. "Sarika: bottle-green georgette, sweetheart blouse, low bun".

## What has worked (defaults, not laws)
- Write the idea in English first. Translate to Hinglish last. Thinking in Hindi first produced clunky lines.
- Hinglish = spoken Hindi with the English words people actually say (listing gain, investors, watchlist, business). Not shuddh Hindi.
- Hook: the viewer's stake, one plain sentence, understandable in two seconds. A question works well.
- One idea. If a second idea sneaks in, cut it.
- Escalation: each beat should raise the stakes or flip the expectation.
- The payoff answers the hook, ideally in the hook's own words ("galat faisla?" → "faisla nahin, iraada galat tha").
- One real joke line per reel minimum. Jokes land on the system, the seller or the herd.
- Characters in shot speak; long explanations go to the correspondent or a voice-over.
- Numbers: at most two per reel, and round them the way a person would say them.
- Compliance: no buy/sell calls, no promised returns (a hard rule in CLAUDE.md).

## What didn't land with Akhilesh (learn from it; don't fear trying new things)
- Clever-but-unclear hooks (a GMP countdown "300... 200... 150... 60" read as noise).
- Explaining mechanics the audience doesn't know (unlisted/pre-IPO shares) instead of cutting them.
- Treating viewers as dumb: explaining IPO or listing gain.
- The gimmick becoming the story (time travel).
- Hedged endings that don't answer the hook.
- Lists of facts with no causal chain ("toll booth, traffic, risk").
- Several numbers in one breath.

## Gold example 1 — Abhi Call Karein! (teleshop), ~50s, final as posted
Message: EMI doesn't make a thing cheaper; it makes the number smaller. The seller is mocked, not the buyer.
1. **SACHDEV** [excited]: पेश है नया iPhone 18 Pro! क़ीमत... सिर्फ़ एक लाख चौंसठ हज़ार नौ सौ! और हमारे पास पहला caller है! हाँजी, आप live हैं!
2. **RAHUL** [tired, on the phone]: सचदेव जी... इतने पैसे नहीं हैं।
3. **SACHDEV** [cheerful, sly]: Correct! सत्ताईस हज़ार महीना?
4. **RAHUL** [instantly hopeful]: Blue colour में मिलेगा?
5. **SACHDEV** [excited → fast mumble → casual]: बिल्कुल! No-Cost EMI! Interest ZERO! [fast] बस processing fee लगेगी, और processing fee पे GST, और interest पे GST, [casual] और माँ के गहने।
6. **RAHUL** [confused]: एक मिनट! Interest तो zero था ना?
7. **SACHDEV** [smug, grinning]: झूठ बोला था। [chuckles] EMI कटेगी पाँच तारीख़ को।
8. **RAHUL** [worried]: सैलरी तो सात को आती है।
9. **SACHDEV** [warmly]: हमें पता है।
Card "5 TAREEKH / SUBAH 6:02" → Rahul's phone buzzes → SMS: "HUM FINANCE: EMI of Rs.27,000 BOUNCED. Bounce charge Rs.590 + GST"
10. **SACHDEV** (VO over the HUM FINANCE slate): हम। उम्र भर फ़ाइनेंस। Terms and conditions apply। सारी शर्तें ध्यान से पढ़ें।

## Gold example 2 — Doordhan Samachar: NSE listing, ~34s, final as posted
1. **SARIKA** [crisp]: नमस्कार। क्या NSE के IPO में पैसा लगाना... ग़लत फ़ैसला था?
(montage 2.5s)
2. **SARIKA** [dry]: क्योंकि listing gain से तो investors ने बस समोसे खाए हैं।
3. **GUPTA** [flat, certain]: कतई नहीं। GMP यानी Grey Market Premium... सिर्फ़ listing का थर्मामीटर है, NSE के business का नहीं। हर शेयर को ख़रीदने या बेचने पर NSE कमाता है... और नए, युवा investors तेज़ी से उमड़ रहे हैं।
4. **SARIKA** [measured → sly]: तो सोचिए... NSE ख़रीदा किसलिए था? अगर listing gain के लिए... तो फ़ैसला नहीं, इरादा ग़लत था। NSE long term का खेल है। picture अभी बाक़ी है।
Why it works: plain stake question; the samosa joke explains the doubt; the correspondent answers flatly; the ending answers the hook in its own words.
