## Answer

There is a scientific study of speech rhythm, prosody and information organisation. The nearest useful analogue to poetic meter for our conversational scripts is prosodic phrasing/cadence. A strict recurring long/short-syllable pattern is a different constraint from natural spoken explanation. None of the research reviewed establishes a universal reel meter, ideal sentence length, fixed Hindi/English ratio or guaranteed retention formula.

## Evidence

1. **Poetic meter:** [Rekhta's own Taqti explanation](https://www.rekhta.org/taqti?wref=rweb) describes bahr and scanning syllable weights. Its rules support analysis of metrical verse; they do not establish rules for conversational business narration.

2. **Prosodic phrases:** Inbar, Grossman and Landau (2020), [Sequences of Intonation Units form a ~1 Hz rhythm](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-72739-4.pdf). Open primary paper inspected. Recordings of over 100 speakers in six languages show recurring temporal organisation of intonation units. Pitch resets, changes in articulation and syllable timing delineate phrases; silence is not required at every boundary. This is acoustic evidence, not an experiment demonstrating a reel-retention increase or a prescription for one-second sentences/cuts. The six-language sample does not include Hindi.

3. **Pauses and information structure:** Swerts and Geluykens (1994), [Prosody as a Marker of Information Flow in Spoken Discourse](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/002383099403700102). Publisher abstract reviewed; full paper restricted. Their production/perception work indicates that pitch and pause cues contribute to how listeners perceive discourse structure. This supports deliberate phrasing as a research-informed practice, not arbitrary fixed-duration pauses.

4. **Hinglish:** Rao et al. (2018), [A Study of Lexical and Prosodic Cues to Segmentation in a Hindi-English Code-switched Discourse](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2018/rao18_interspeech.pdf). Primary paper inspected. It analyses an hour from five talks by one speaker, BK Shivani. Hindi and English segments differ on acoustic cues, and inter-sentential switches coincide with phrase boundaries in these recordings. It is a narrow descriptive dataset, not evidence that every switch must occur at a clause boundary or that a particular mix optimises engagement. A useful hypothesis is to choose language by natural phrasing and thought, rather than word-by-word translation.

5. **Causal narrative:** Trabasso and Sperry (1985), [Causal relatedness and importance of story events](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0749596X85900488), primary abstract reviewed. In six folktales, causal connections and membership in an opening-to-closing causal chain predicted judgments of event importance. Related work by [Trabasso and van den Broek](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/trabassovandenbroek1985causal.pdf) examines recall and summarisation. These findings concern narrative comprehension/memory, not social-media retention trials. Do not turn correlation into proof that adding "but" or "therefore" improves a reel.

6. **Curiosity:** Golman and Loewenstein, [Curiosity, Information Gaps, and the Utility of Knowledge](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/golman/golman_loewenstein_curiosity.pdf), inspected as an author-hosted theoretical paper. It models attention to missing information and the utility of knowing. It motivates testing specific, meaningful questions; it does not prescribe a number of hooks, a fixed reveal timestamp or a dopamine ladder for reels.

## Working application — craft guidance, not established laws

Treat the script as three interacting structures:

- **Story:** what the listener wants to learn, and how successive observations explain or complicate it.
- **Information:** what is already established, what is newly introduced and which distinction matters now.
- **Performance:** the phrase boundaries, emphasis, pitch movement, tempo and pauses that make that distinction audible.

Fix the story before polishing its musicality. A pleasant cadence cannot repair an unexplained jump from Starlink to ARPU. Conversely, a well-connected argument can still sound like a document if the spoken phrasing is not designed.

### Drafting passes

1. State the opening question and the final answer privately. Check that the answer fulfils the actual question.
2. Give every intervening beat a function: establish context, supply evidence, complicate an assumption, reveal an overlap or draw a supported consequence. Mark missing relevance bridges. Use real causation where supported, logical relevance where causal evidence is unavailable; do not invent causation to make the script smoother.
3. Read aloud and mark coherent phrases with `|`. These are delivery boundaries, not mandatory silences or incomplete written sentences. Use `||` where a complete thought warrants more separation.
4. Mark the words that carry the new information or contrast. Not every word should be emphasised. Write complete sentences, as Akhilesh requests; a sentence may contain several prosodic phrases.
5. Use parallel phrasing or a short landing sentence when it clarifies the idea. Vary sentence/phrase lengths by purpose; no compulsory short–long–short pattern or word-count quota.
6. Choose English or Hindi where the whole phrase sounds natural. Preserve user wording and report any factual issue separately. Do not force literary Hindi, rhyme, equal syllable counts or an artificial radio cadence.
7. Time an actual spoken take, including numerals, company names and acronyms. As planning arithmetic only, T ≈ S/r + P, where S is spoken syllables, r is that speaker's measured articulation rate excluding pauses, and P is pause time. Never mix a rate measured including pauses with a separately added pause allowance. Counts remain estimates; recorded duration is the practical check.
8. Compare alternative delivery/phrasing with identical underlying facts and honest framing. Measure completion/watch time and shares over comparable samples. Do not read a single retention spike as proof of delight: confusion and replay can also produce it.

### Demonstration, not a manuscript revision

The current user-requested FY26 growth comparison can be marked:

> Last financial year, Jio ke home broadband customers **55% badhe**. | Mobile broadband mein growth **around 5.6% thi**. ||

Both clauses remain complete. The repeated customer-growth concept gives the comparison a recognisable shape; the emphasis makes the differing rates audible. This is flexible rhetorical cadence, not a scanned bahr. No requirement to pause after every number or emphasise it loudly. ## Guardrails for interpreting this research

- One written sentence is not one intonation unit, one breath or one edit.
- An average rhythm in observed speech is not an optimal prescribed beat for every listener or language.
- Syllable counts, stress patterns and word counts describe different things. Roman spellings alone do not reliably determine spoken meter.
- Retention depends on topic, relevance, delivery, visuals and audience/distribution as well as language. The cited studies do not isolate our reel performance.
- These methods inform the playbook and explicitly supplied writer/judge/voice inputs. Character rules and approved script words remain intact; the research does not approve production or a voice take.

## PDF lessons: Short-Form Storytelling Principles

User-supplied on 22 October; incorporated at Akhilesh's request. Five-page excerpt attributed to Kallaway's Short Form Academy. The original is at `/Users/akhilesh/Downloads/Short-Form Storytelling Principles.pdf`; these paraphrased lessons are kept in the repo so other writers do not depend on that local file. All five pages were extracted, rendered and read. This is a practitioner reference, distinct from the research papers above. The playbook contains the operative guidance; this section explains the adaptation.

- **Curiosity, contrast and anticipation:** establish a question the intended viewer cares about, supply meaningful developments, and let answers create the next relevant question. Contrast changes an expectation; anticipation gives the listener a reason to expect a consequential answer. Do not accumulate mysteries without paying them off. Akhilesh's multiple-peaks-and-troughs direction means successive changes of interpretation, not a prescribed waveform or a fixed number of hooks.
- **Speed to value and value density:** show an interesting stake or contradiction early, then keep adding understanding. Cut tangents and duplication. Keep causal or logical bridges and necessary qualifications: removing them can turn a story into a list of unexplained facts. The PDF's five-second example is a heuristic, not an experimentally established deadline.
- **Clarity, comprehension and absorption:** use direct phrasing and a concrete example where it makes an unfamiliar mechanism easier to grasp. Choose the explanation for this audience. Do not adopt a universal sixth-grade target or mandatory repetition of every important point; our finance-aware viewers already understand familiar finance terms. Kendra's beginner caller remains a separate format choice.
- **Emotional resonance, tone and energy:** identify the real stakes and the feeling the facts can support. Delivery follows the character and the thought; interest need not mean louder speech. Fear or shock can be creative options, but this PDF does not establish that they always produce the most shares. Do not invent damage, certainty or a bankruptcy forecast to create alarm.
- **Rhythm, cadence and flow:** vary phrase length, emphasis and pace when the meaning calls for it. Keep natural connections and complete spoken sentences. This reinforces the existing prosody method; it does not supply a meter, require staccato fragments or establish a retention guarantee.

## Follow-up evidence and practitioner methods

Reviewed for the 20 October discussion; integrated 22 October. The workflow's main instructions stay in the playbook. These sources explain why the options are useful and where their evidence stops.

- **Audible focus, with trade-offs.** Chuklin et al., [Prosody Modifications for Question-Answering in Voice-Only Settings](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.03957), full paper inspected. In crowdsourced English voice-only question answering with two TTS engines, some emphasis/rate/pitch modifications improved identification of key answers, while naturalness could suffer. Effects varied with the engine and answer characteristics. This supports selective prominence, not a universal SSML recipe or proof of higher reel completion.
- **Recognisable rhetorical structures.** Heritage and Greatbatch (1986), [Generating Applause](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/228465), primary abstract reviewed; [Bull's analysis](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/100468/3/A_PublishedVersion.pdf) inspected. Contrasts and three-part lists can make completion points recognisable in political speeches. This is evidence about live speaker/audience coordination, not a reel-retention trial. Parallel comparisons and purposeful repetition are optional craft choices; do not manufacture lists or an opposition merely to use them.
- **Telling before polishing.** [Noel King in Transom's Don't Write, Tell](https://transom.org/2026/revisiting-dont-write-tell/), practitioner interview transcript inspected. She describes telling a complex money story conversationally, noticing confusion/interest and composing passages for cadence. This is an experienced reporter's method, not a controlled experiment. Adapt it to the intended audience; conversational need not mean explaining familiar finance concepts.
- **Voice notes and table reads.** [Transom's account of WBUR Cognoscenti's process](https://transom.org/2026/deploy-this-one-tiny-maneuver-to-improve-audio-essays/), practitioner transcript inspected. Editors use authors' spoken answers and rehearsals to make broadcast versions sound closer to their actual speech. Use an actual reading when available; a model inspecting text must not claim it performed an oral test.
- **Our production observation.** `docs/voices/directing-tts.md` records a take with a normal pitch range that still sounded read because sentence contours and pauses repeated. Local measurements can expose a delivery problem; their numeric values are not universal targets. Keep the approved words separate from punctuation/tags used to direct performance. Do not change model, retry budgets or character constraints merely because a study used a different engine.

### Example: comparison and landing phrase

From Akhilesh's edited Jio script (an illustration of phrasing, not a new fact check):

> Last financial year, yahaan customers **55% badhe**. | Mobile broadband mein growth **sirf 5.6% thi**. ||

The second clause reuses the comparison's shape and changes the focus. The closing phrase, **future growth ko less profitable**, is the business distinction the story builds toward. A performance version can give it room to land without rewriting approved words or inserting silence after every written full stop. Prefer natural syntax over forcing every important word to the end.
